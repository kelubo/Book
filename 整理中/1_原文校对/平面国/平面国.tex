\chapter*{第二版编者序（1884）}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{第二版编者序}

If my poor Flatland friend retained the vigour of his mind which he enjoyed when he began to compose these Memoirs, I should not now need to represent him in this preface, as he himself desired, which indeed he would otherwise have written long ago. But he is not the Square he once was. Years of imprisonment, and the still heavier burden of general incredulity and mockery, have combined with the natural decay of old age to erase from his memory many of the thoughts and notions, and much also of the terminology, which he acquired during his short stay in Spaceland. He has, therefore, requested me to reply in his behalf to two objections, one of an intellectual, the other of a moral nature, which have been urged against his narrative; as he himself finds, to his regret, that his own powers are insufficient to write a preface such as he had planned.

即使我可怜的平面国朋友仍然保有着他刚开始撰写这些回忆录时那种充沛的脑力，我现在也不必代表他——如他所愿地——来写这篇序言了；确切地说，若非如此，他本人早在许久以前就该将这篇序言写好了。然而，他已不再是当年的那个正方形了。多年的监禁，加之普遍的不信与嘲弄所带来的更为沉重的负担，以及老年的自然衰退，已将他短暂停留在空间国期间所获得的许多思想、概念，乃至大量术语，从他的记忆中抹去了。因此，他请求我代表他来回应两个反对意见：一个来自智识层面，另一个来自道德层面——这两个反对意见都是针对他的叙述而提出的；而他本人也遗憾地发现，自己的精力已不足以撰写一篇如他原先所规划的那样的序言。

After he had explained to me these objections, which he modestly but earnestly entreated me to remove, I begged his permission to hear them in detail. To his first objection, viz., that a Flatlander seeing a Line would see something that would be thick as well as long (so that a Flatlander would actually see a long and thin parallelogram, but not a line), I was happily able to offer a complete reply. To the second objection, which is on the subject of Irregulars, I found no difficulty in answering at length. But I found it necessary to refer the reader to a certain Diagram, which my friend had himself prepared, and which he desired me to insert in this preface, in order to help the reader to understand the nature of the argument by which the second objection is met.

在他向我解释了这些反对意见——他谦逊而恳切地请求我予以消除——之后，我请求他允许我详细听取这些反对意见。对于他的第一个反对意见，即平面国的人看到一条线时，会看到既有宽度又有长度的东西（因此平面国的人实际上看到的是一条又长又窄的平行四边形，而不是线），我得以幸运地给出了圆满的答复。对于第二个反对意见——关于不规则图形的问题——我也毫无困难地作出了充分的答复。不过，我发现有必要请读者参照一张特定的图解，这张图解是我的朋友亲自准备的，他希望我将其载入这篇序言，以帮助读者理解回应第二个反对意见的论证的本质。

\section*{回应第一个反对意见}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{回应第一个反对意见}

But in order to explain the nature of the first objection fully, I must premise that the description of a Line given in the text is not accurate without explanation. When a straight Line is presented to a Flatlander, he sees it as a more or less bright stripe, but it is only by inference that he learns that it is a Line; for, as a matter of fact, a Line is always seen by him as a long and thin parallelogram, which he has been taught from infancy to represent to himself by the name ``Line.'' The inference that the object seen is a straight Line is based on experience; and really involves the assumption that the object approached sidewise or end-on according as its appearance is brighter or dimmer. The arguments for this conclusion are fully stated in the text (see pp. 20--23). To avoid confusion, the Square uses the term ``Line'' throughout; and when he speaks of ``seeing a Line,'' he means that he sees a long and thin parallelogram. And similarly, he uses the term ``Point'' for the bright end of a straight Line presented to him endwise, and ``plain Straight Line'' for a long and thin parallelogram.

然而，为了充分解释第一个反对意见的本质，我必须先说明一点：正文中对"线"的描述若不加解释，是不准确的。当一条直线呈现在平面国的居民面前时，他看到的是一条或多或少有些明亮的细条，但他只是通过推理才知道那就是一条线；因为事实上，在他的视觉中，一条线总是呈现为一个又长又窄的平行四边形，而他从小就被教导要用"线"这个名称来指代它。推断所看到的物体是一条直线，这一过程是基于经验的；实际上，它包含了一个假设，即物体究竟是从侧面靠近的还是以端面朝向观察者，取决于它看起来更亮还是更暗。支持这一结论的论证在正文中已充分陈述（见第20--23页）。为了避免混淆，正方形在全书中都使用了"线"这个术语；当他说到"看见一条线"时，他的意思是他看见一个又长又窄的平行四边形。同样，他用"点"来表示一条直线以端面朝向他时所呈现的那个明亮的端点，用"普通的直线"来表示一个又长又窄的平行四边形。

Now the objection is that a Flatlander actually sees a parallelogram, and so he cannot be rightly said to see a Line. But the reply is that a Flatlander learns to infer a Line from the long and thin parallelogram; and this inference is quite as valid as the inference which we, in Spaceland, make when we talk of ``seeing a Point,'' or ``seeing a Line.'' For we never see a mathematical point or line: we see a small patch of light or a long thin stripe of light, and infer the existence of a point or a line from these sense-impressions. And the inference is not merely a conscious act of reasoning, but becomes at last so habitual and instinctive that the sensation and the inference seem to be simultaneous, and to constitute a single act of perception. And just as we in Spaceland speak of ``seeing a Point'' or ``seeing a Line,'' so the Square speaks of ``seeing a Line'' and ``seeing a Point.''

这个反对意见是：平面国的居民实际上看到的是一个平行四边形，因此不能说他是"看见了一条线"。但答复是：平面国的居民学会了从一个又长又窄的平行四边形推断出线的存在；这种推断的有效性，与我们空间国的居民在谈论"看见一个点"或"看见一条线"时所做的推断完全一样。因为我们从来不曾看见过数学意义上的点或线：我们看见的是一小块光斑或一条又长又窄的亮条，然后从这些感觉印象中推断出一个点或一条线的存在。而且，这种推断不仅仅是一种有意识的推理行为，它最终会变得如此习惯性和本能性，以至于感觉和推断似乎是同时发生的，并构成了一个单一的感知行为。正如我们空间国的人说"看见一个点"或"看见一条线"一样，正方形也说他"看见一条线"或"看见一个点"。

A similar ambiguity of language arises with regard to the limits of the Flatlander's world. The Square tells us that in his world there is no such thing as thickness; and this seems to mean that the Flatlander has no conception of thickness. But he really has a conception of thickness, though it is of a very rudimentary kind, and his world has thickness, though it is a very thin thickness indeed. For if his world had absolutely no thickness, or rather if the thickness of his world were less than the finest conceivable thickness, the Flatlander could not perceive anything at all. There must be an appreciable thickness, since the Flatlander, in order to see a Line, must see something that has thickness. But the thickness is so small that it is negligible for all practical purposes; and the Flatlander, though he has thickness in his world, has no conception of it, and cannot speak of it.

在平面国世界的边界问题上，也存在着类似的语言模糊性。正方形告诉我们，在他的世界里不存在"厚度"这种东西；这似乎意味着平面国的人对厚度毫无概念。但实际上，他对厚度确有概念，尽管是一种非常初步的概念；他的世界也确实有厚度，尽管这厚度确实非常非常薄。因为如果他的世界完全没有厚度，或者说如果他的世界的厚度小于可以想象到的最细微的厚度，那么平面国的人将根本无法感知到任何东西。既然平面国的人要看见一条线就必须看见有一定厚度的东西，那么就必须存在一个可感知的厚度。但这个厚度如此之小，在所有实际用途中都可以忽略不计；而平面国的人，尽管他的世界中有厚度存在，却对此毫无概念，也无法表述它。

\section*{回应第二个反对意见}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{回应第二个反对意见}

The second objection is, that the Square speaks slightingly of the Irregulars, and of his own Isosceles ancestors. I admit that the tone is not always one of perfect urbanity; but the Square writes as a historian and from a historical point of view. As a historian, he shows us the struggles, prejudices, and errors of the past, and he has been careful not to mix up his own personal feelings with his historical judgments. He has, indeed, for the most part, suppressed his own personal feelings altogether; for his own generous nature revolts from the cruelty with which the upper classes at one time treated the Isosceles, and he has in several places expressed his sympathy with the lower classes. If the tone is sometimes too severe, I trust the reader will remember that the Square is, after all, a citizen of the nineteenth century, and that his language is that of his own time and country, and must be taken with the allowance due to the age in which he lived.

第二个反对意见是，正方形在谈到不规则图形以及他自己的等腰三角形祖先时，言辞间带有轻蔑之意。我承认，他行文的语气并不总是彬彬有礼的；但正方形是以历史学家的身份、从历史的角度来写作的。作为历史学家，他向我们展示了过去时代的斗争、偏见和错误，并且小心翼翼地避免将个人的情感与历史的判断混为一谈。事实上，在大多数情况下，他完全抑制了自己的个人情感；因为他那宽厚的天性使他厌恶上层阶级曾一度对待等腰三角形的残酷行为，他在书中多处表达了对下层阶级的同情。如果他的语气有时过于严厉，我相信读者会记得，正方形毕竟是一位十九世纪的公民，他的语言属于他那个时代、那个国家，必须以对他所生活的时代应有的宽容来理解。

I have now discharged what was, after all, a not very difficult task; and the reader must not suppose that the removal of the two objections implies any general agreement with the views of the Square on the part of the Editor. On the contrary, I differ from him on many important points. But this is not the place to enter into a discussion of these differences. I will only say that I respect the Square as a being of sincere and honest purpose, and that I wish him, in his captivity, as much happiness as is compatible with his unfortunate situation.

现在，我已经完成了这个说到底并不十分困难的任务；读者切不可认为，消除了这两个反对意见就代表编者普遍赞同正方形的观点。恰恰相反，我在许多重要问题上与他意见相左。但此处并非讨论这些分歧的场所。我只想说：我尊重正方形，视他为一个真诚而诚实的生灵；我希望，在他的囚禁中，他能在与其不幸处境不相矛盾的范围内，获得尽可能多的幸福。

\chapter*{序言}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{序言}

I call our world Flatland, not because we call it so, but to make its nature clearer to you, my happy readers, who are privileged to live in Space.

我将我们的世界称作平面国，并非因为我们自己这样称呼它，而是为了让你们——我幸运的读者们，享有生活在空间中之特权的人们——能够更清楚地理解它的本质。

Imagine a vast sheet of paper on which straight Lines, Triangles, Squares, Pentagons, Hexagons, and other figures, instead of remaining fixed in their places, move freely about, on or in the surface, but without the power of rising above or sinking below it, very much like shadows---only hard and with luminous edges---and you will then have a pretty correct notion of my country and countrymen. Alas, a few years ago, I should have said ``my universe'': but now my mind has been opened to higher views of things.

想象一张巨大的纸，在上面，直线、三角形、正方形、五边形、六边形以及其他图形，并非固定于原地不动，而是可以在表面上或表面中自由移动，却没有能力升到表面之上或沉到表面之下——很像影子，只是它们是坚硬的，而且边缘会发光——这样你就能对我们的国家和国民有一个相当准确的概念了。唉，若在几年之前，我本应说"我的宇宙"：但现在，我的心智已向更高远的事物观敞开了。

In such a country, you will perceive at once that it is impossible that there should be anything of what you call a ``solid'' kind; but I dare say you will suppose that we could at least distinguish by sight the Triangles, the Squares, and the other figures, moving about as I have described. On the contrary, we could see nothing of the kind, not at least so as to distinguish one figure from another. Nothing was visible, or could be visible, to us, except Straight Lines; and the necessity of this I will speedily demonstrate.

在这样的国家里，你立刻就会明白，不可能存在任何你们所谓的"立体"之物；但我敢说，你们也许会认为，我们至少可以通过视觉来区分那些如我所描述般移动的三角形、正方形和其他图形。恰恰相反，我们完全看不到这类东西，至少无法区分一个图形与另一个图形。对我们而言，除了直线之外，没有任何东西是可见的，或可能是可见的；我将很快证明其中的必然性。

Place a penny on the middle of one of your tables in Space, and, leaning over it, look down upon it. It will appear a circle.

在空间中，将一枚硬币放在你的一张桌子中央，俯身向下看。它看起来会是一个圆。

But now, drawing back to the edge of the table, gradually lower your eye (thus bringing yourself more and more into the condition of the inhabitants of Flatland), and you will find the penny becoming more and more oval to your view; and at last when you have placed your eye exactly on the edge of the table (so that you are, as it were, actually a Flatlander) the penny will then have ceased to appear oval at all, and will have become, so far as you can see, a straight line.

但现在，退到桌子边缘，逐渐降低你的视线（这样你就越来越接近平面国居民的状态），你会发现那枚硬币在你的视野中变得越来越椭圆；最后，当你的眼睛恰好处于桌面的边缘时（这样你仿佛就是一个真正的平面国人了），那枚硬币将完全不再呈椭圆形，而是变成了一条——就你所能看到的而言——直线。

The same thing would happen if you were treating in the same way a Triangle, or a Square, or any other figure cut out of pasteboard. As soon as you look at it with your eye on the edge of the table, you will find that it ceases to appear to you a figure, and that it becomes in appearance a straight line. Take for example an equilateral Triangle---who represents with us a Tradesman of the respectable class. Fig.\ 1 represents the Tradesman as you would see him while you were bending over him from above; figs.\ 2 and 3 represent the Tradesman as you would see him if your eye were close to the level, or all but on the level of the table; and if your eye were quite on the level of the table (and that is how we see him in Flatland) you would see nothing but a straight line.

同样的情形也会发生，如果你以相同方式观察一个三角形、正方形，或是任何用硬纸板剪出的图形。一旦你将眼睛贴到桌面的边缘去看它，你就会发现它不再呈现为一个图形，而是在外观上成了一条直线。以一个等边三角形为例——他在我们这里代表一位受人尊敬的商人阶层。图1所示的商人，是你从上方向下俯视时看到的样子；图2和图3所示的商人，是你的眼睛接近桌面水平面、或几乎处于桌面高度时所看到的样子；而如果你的眼睛完全处于桌面的水平面上（这正是我们在平面国中看他的方式），你就只能看到一条直线。

When I was in Spaceland I understood that your sailors, while they traverse your mighty oceans, have very similar experiences. They see the distant land, as a line only, very even and level, and the masts and chimneys of the distant shipping as so many erect lines, and nothing else. Well, that is a very good description of the way in which we see things in Flatland; and you will see at once that the same rule applies, of course, to all our vision. If, then, the penny, or the pasteboard Triangle, or Square, appears to you a straight line, how much more must that be the case when you look at an inhabitant of Flatland, whose thickness is, to all appearance, no thicker than the thickness of a line!

我在空间国时了解到，你们的水手在横渡广阔海洋时，有着非常相似的体验。他们看到远方的陆地，仅仅是一条非常平整而水平的线，远处船只的桅杆和烟囱也只是一条条竖直的线，别无其他。嗯，这正是对我们在平面国中观看事物方式的一个很好的描述；而且你立刻就会明白，同样的规则当然适用于我们所有的视觉。那么，如果一枚硬币、或一个硬纸板的三角形、正方形在你看来都是一条直线，那么当你看一个平面国的居民——他的厚度，从外观上看，不比一条线的厚度更厚——时，情况更是如此！

\part{这个世界}

\chapter{平面国的自然特征}

I call our world Flatland, not because we call it so, but to make its nature clearer to you, my happy readers, who are privileged to live in Space.

我将我们的世界称为平面国，并非因为我们自己这样称呼它，而是为了向你们——幸运地享有空间生活特权的读者们——更清楚地说明它的特性。

Imagine a vast sheet of paper on which straight Lines, Triangles, Squares, Pentagons, Hexagons, and other figures, instead of remaining fixed in their places, move freely about, on or in the surface, but without the power of rising above or sinking below it, very much like shadows---only hard and with luminous edges---and you will then have a pretty correct notion of my country and countrymen. Alas, a few years ago, I should have said ``my universe'': but now my mind has been opened to higher views of things.

想象一张巨大的纸，在上面，直线、三角形、正方形、五边形、六边形以及其他图形，并非固定停留在原地，而是可以在表面上或表面中自由移动，却没有能力升到表面之上或沉到表面之下——很像影子，只是它们是坚硬的，并且带有发光的边缘——这样你就能对我们的国家和国民有一个相当准确的概念了。唉，若是几年前，我本应说"我的宇宙"：但现在，我的心智已向更高的视野敞开了。

In such a country, you will perceive at once that it is impossible that there should be anything of what you call a ``solid'' kind; but I dare say you will suppose that we could at least distinguish by sight the Triangles, the Squares, and the other figures, moving about as I have described. On the contrary, we could see nothing of the kind, not at least so as to distinguish one figure from another. Nothing was visible, or could be visible, to us, except Straight Lines; and the necessity of this I will speedily demonstrate.

在这样一个国家里，你立刻就会明白，不可能存在任何你们所谓的"立体"之物；但我敢说，你们也许会以为，我们至少能够通过视觉来辨别我所描述的那些四处移动的三角形、正方形和其他图形。恰恰相反，我们根本看不到这类东西，至少无法将一个图形与另一个图形区分开来。除了直线之外，对我们来说没有任何东西是可见的，也不可能有任何东西是可见的；我将很快证明其中的必然性。

Place a penny on the middle of one of your tables in Space; and leaning over it, look down upon it. It will appear a circle.

在空间中将一枚一便士硬币放在你的一张桌子中央；俯身向下看。它看起来会是一个圆。

But now, drawing back to the edge of the table, gradually lower your eye (thus bringing yourself more and more into the condition of the inhabitants of Flatland), and you will find the penny becoming more and more oval to your view; and at last when you have placed your eye exactly on the edge of the table (so that you are, as it were, actually a Flatlander) the penny will then have ceased to appear oval at all, and will have become, so far as you can see, a straight line.

但现在，退到桌子边缘，逐渐降低你的眼睛（从而让自己越来越接近平面国居民的处境），你会发现那枚硬币在你的视野中变得越来越椭圆；最后，当你的眼睛恰好位于桌面的边缘时（这样你仿佛就成了一个真正的平面国人），那枚硬币将完全不再呈椭圆形，而是变成了——就你所能看到的而言——一条直线。

The same thing would happen if you were treating in the same way a Triangle, or Square, or any other figure cut out of pasteboard. As soon as you look at it with your eye on the edge on the table, you will find that it ceases to appear to you a figure, and that it becomes in appearance a straight line. Take for example an equilateral Triangle---who represents with us a Tradesman of the respectable class. Fig.\ 1 represents the Tradesman as you would see him while you were bending over him from above; figs.\ 2 and 3 represent the Tradesman, as you would see him if your eye were close to the level, or all but on the level of the table; and if your eye were quite on the level of the table (and that is how we see him in Flatland) you would see nothing but a straight line.

同样的情形也会发生，如果你以同样的方式来处理一个三角形、正方形，或是任何用硬纸板剪出的图形。一旦你将眼睛放在桌面的边缘上去看它，你就会发现它不再向你呈现为一个图形，而是在外观上变成了一条直线。以一个等边三角形为例——在我们这里，他代表一位受人尊敬的商人阶层。图1所示，是你从上方向下俯视商人时他所呈现的样子；图2和图3所示，是你的眼睛接近桌面水平面、或几乎处于桌面高度时商人所呈现的样子；而如果你的眼睛完全处于桌面的水平面上（这正是我们在平面国中看他的方式），你就只能看到一条直线。

When I was in Spaceland I heard that your sailors have very similar experiences while they traverse your seas and discern some distant island or coast lying on the horizon. The far-off land may have bays, forelands, angles in and out to any number and extent; yet at a distance you see none of these (unless indeed your sun shines bright upon them revealing the projections and retirements by means of light and shade), nothing but a grey unbroken line upon the water.

我在空间国时听说，你们的水手在穿越海洋、辨别地平线上远处的岛屿或海岸时，有着非常相似的体验。遥远的陆地可能有海湾、海角、各种角度的凹凸，数不胜数；但在远处你什么也看不见（除非确实有阳光照亮它们，通过明暗显示出凸出和凹进），只有水面上一条灰色的、不间断的线。

Well, that is just what we see when one of our triangular or other acquaintances comes towards us in Flatland. As there is neither sun with us, nor any light of such a kind as to make shadows, we have none of the helps to the sight that you have in Spaceland. If our friend comes closer to us we see his line becomes larger; if he leaves us it becomes smaller; but still he looks like a straight line; be he a Triangle, Square, Pentagon, Hexagon, Circle, what you will---a straight Line he looks and nothing else.

嗯，这正是当我们的三角形或其他熟人在平面国中向我们走来时所看到的情景。由于我们既没有太阳，也没有任何能制造阴影的光，我们没有你们在空间国中拥有的那些视觉辅助手段。如果我们的朋友向我们靠近，我们看到他的线条变大了；如果他离开我们，线条变小了；但他仍然看起来是一条直线；无论他是三角形、正方形、五边形、六边形、圆形，还是什么别的——他看起来就是一条直线，仅此而已。

You may perhaps ask how under these disadvantagous circumstances we are able to distinguish our friends from one another: but the answer to this very natural question will be more fitly and easily given when I come to describe the inhabitants of Flatland. For the present let me defer this subject, and say a word or two about the climate and houses in our country.

你或许会问，在这些不利的情况下，我们如何能够区分我们的朋友呢？但这个非常自然的问题的答案，将在我描述平面国的居民时更适合、也更容易给出。目前，让我先把这个话题放一放，来谈谈我们国家的气候和房屋。

\chapter{平面国的气候与房屋}

As with you, so also with us, there are four points of the compass---North, South, East, and West.

正如你们一样，我们也有四个方位点——北、南、东、西。

There being no sun nor other heavenly bodies, it is impossible for us to determine the North in the usual way; but we have a method of our own. By a Law of Nature with us, there is a constant attraction to the South; and, although in temperate climates this is very slight---so that even a Woman in reasonable health can journey several furlongs northward without much difficulty---yet the hampering effort of the southward attraction is quite sufficient to serve as a compass in most parts of our earth. Moreover, the rain (which falls at stated intervals) coming always from the North, is an additional assistance; and in the towns we have the guidance of the houses, which of course have their side-walls running for the most part North and South, so that the roofs may keep off the rain from the North. In the country, where there are no houses, the trunks of the trees serve as some sort of guide. Altogether, we have not so much difficulty as might be expected in determining our bearings.

由于没有太阳或其他天体，我们不可能以通常的方式确定北方；但我们有自己的方法。根据我们的自然法则，存在一个恒定的向南吸引力；而且，尽管在温带气候中这种吸引力非常微弱——以至于即使是一个健康状况良好的女人也可以毫不费力地向北走几弗隆——但这种向南的吸引力阻碍作用，足以在我们地球的大部分地区充当指南针。此外，雨水（在固定的间隔时间降落）总是从北方而来，这是一个额外的帮助；在城镇中，我们有房屋的指引——房屋的侧墙大多朝南朝北，这样屋顶就能挡住来自北方的雨水。在没有房屋的乡下，树干充当某种向导。总的来说，我们在确定方向方面并没有想象中那么困难。

Yet in our more temperate regions, in which the southward attraction is hardly felt, walking sometimes in a perfectly desolate plain where there have been no houses nor trees to guide me, I have been occasionally compelled to remain stationary for hours together, waiting till the rain came before continuing my journey. On the weak and aged, and especially on delicate Females, the force of attraction tells much more heavily than on the robust of the Male Sex, so that it is a point of breeding, if you meet a Lady on the street, always to give her the North side of the way---by no means an easy thing to do always at short notice when you are in rude health and in a climate where it is difficult to tell your North from your South.

然而在我们较为温和的地区，向南的吸引力几乎感受不到，有时我走在一片完全荒凉的平原上，那里没有房屋也没有树木可以指引方向，我有时被迫连续几个小时站在原地，等待雨水到来后再继续我的旅程。对于弱者和老人，尤其是娇弱的女性，这种吸引力的作用比对健壮的男性要大得多；因此，如果你在街上遇到一位女士，总是给她让路的北侧——这绝不是一件在你身体健康、且在一个难以区分东南西北的气候中总能轻易做到的事情——是一种礼貌的表现。

Windows there are none in our houses: for the light comes to us alike in our homes and out of them, by day and by night, equally at all times and in all places, whence we know not. It was in old days, with our learned men, an interesting and oft-investigate question, ``What is the origin of light?'' and the solution of it has been repeatedly attempted, with no other result than to crowd our lunatic asylums with the would-be solvers. Hence, after fruitless attempts to suppress such investigations indirectly by making them liable to a heavy tax, the Legislature, in comparatively recent times, absolutely prohibited them. I---alas, I alone in Flatland---know now only too well the true solution of this mysterious problem; but my knowledge cannot be made intelligible to a single one of my countrymen; and I am mocked at---I, the sole possessor of the truths of Space and of the theory of the introduction of Light from the world of three Dimensions---as if I were the maddest of the mad! But a truce to these painful digressions: let me return to our homes.

我们的房屋没有窗户：因为无论在白天还是黑夜，无论在何时何地，光线都同样地从我们家中和屋外照来，我们不知道它从何而来。在过去，我们的学者们曾经常常研究一个有趣的问题："光的起源是什么？"人们反复尝试解答这个问题，结果只是把我们的疯人院塞满了那些想要解答它的人。因此，在通过征收重税来间接压制这类调查的徒劳尝试之后，立法机构在 comparatively recent times（较近的时期）绝对禁止了这些调查。我——唉，在整个平面国只有我一个人——现在对这个神秘问题的真正答案知道得太清楚了；但我的知识无法让任何一个同胞理解；我被嘲笑——我，空间真理的唯一拥有者，以及从三维世界引入光的理论的唯一掌握者——仿佛我是疯子中最疯的一个！但请中止这些痛苦的离题：让我回到我们的房屋话题上。

The most common form for the construction of a house is five-sided or pentagonal, as in the annexed figure. The two Northern sides \_RO\_, \_OF\_, constitute the roof, and for the most part have no doors; on the East is a small door for the Women; on the West a much larger one for the Men; the South side or floor is usually doorless.

[插图]

房屋建造中最常见的形式是五边形，如附图所示。北面的两条边 \_RO\_、\_OF\_ 构成屋顶，通常没有门；东面有一扇供女性使用的小门；西面有一扇供男性使用的大得多的门；南面或地面通常没有门。

Square and triangular houses are not allowed, and for this reason. The angles of a Square (and still more those of an equilateral Triangle,) being much more pointed than those of a Pentagon, and the lines of inanimate objects (such as houses) being dimmer than the lines of Men and Women, it follows that there is no little danger lest the points of a square or triangular house residence might do serious injury to an inconsiderate or perhaps absentminded traveller suddenly running against them: and therefore, as early as the eleventh century of our era, triangular houses were universally forbidden by Law, the only exceptions being fortifications, powder-magazines, barracks, and other state buildings, which is not desirable that the general public should approach without circumspection.

方形和三角形房屋是不被允许的，原因如下。方形的角（更不用说等边三角形的角）比五边形的角要尖锐得多，而无生命物体（如房屋）的线条比男人和女人的线条要暗淡，因此，方形或三角形房屋的尖角可能会对一个不小心或心不在焉的突然撞上它们的旅行者造成严重伤害，这是相当危险的。因此，早在我们这个时代的第十一世纪，三角形房屋就被法律普遍禁止，唯一的例外是防御工事、弹药库、兵营和其他国家建筑，这些地方不适合公众不加谨慎地靠近。

At this period, square houses were still everywhere permitted, though discouraged by a special tax. But, about three centuries afterwards, the Law decided that in all towns containing a population above ten thousand, the angle of a Pentagon was the smallest house-angle that could be allowed consistently with the public safety. The good sense of the community has seconded the efforts of the Legislature; and now, even in the country, the pentagonal construction has superseded every other. It is only now and then in some very remote and backward agricultural district that an antiquarian may still discover a square house.

在这个时期，方形房屋仍然到处被允许，尽管通过特别税收加以抑制。但大约三个世纪之后，法律决定在所有人口超过一万的城镇中，五边形的角是公共安全所能允许的最小房屋角度。社区的良好判断力支持了立法机构的努力；现在，即使在乡下，五边形建筑也已取代了所有其他形式。只有在一些非常偏远和落后的农业地区，古物学家才偶尔能发现一座方形房屋。

\chapter{关于平面国的居民}

The greatest length or breadth of a full-grown inhabitant of Flatland may be estimated at about eleven of your inches. Twelve inches may be regarded as a maximum.

平面国一个成年居民的最大长度或宽度，可以估计为约十一英寸（你们空间国的英寸）。十二英寸可视为最大值。

Our Women are Straight Lines. Our Soldiers and our Lowest Class of Workmen are Triangles with two equal sides, each about eleven inches long, and a base or third side so short (often not exceeding half an inch) that they form at their vertices a very sharp and formidable angle. Indeed when their bases are of the most degraded type (not more than the eighth part of an inch in size) they can hardly be distinguished from Straight Lines or Women; so extremely pointed are their vertices. With us, as with you, these Triangles are distinguished from all others by being called Isosceles; and by this name I shall call them.

我们的女性是直线。我们的士兵和最底层的工人是两条等边的三角形，每条约十一英寸长，底边或第三边非常短（通常不超过半英寸），使得它们在其顶点处形成一个非常尖锐而令人生畏的角。事实上，当它们的底边是最低劣的类型（尺寸不超过八分之一英寸）时，它们几乎无法与直线或女性区分开来；它们的顶点极其尖锐。在我们这里，正如在你们那里一样，这些三角形因被称为等腰三角形而与所有其他图形区别开来；我将以此名称指称它们。

Our Middle Class consists of Equilateral or Equal-Sided Triangles. Our Professional Men and Gentlemen are Squares (to which class I myself belong) and Five-Sided Figures or Pentagons. Next above these come the Nobility, of whom there are several degrees, beginning with Six-Sided Figures, or Hexagons, and from thence rising in the number of their sides till they receive the honourable title of Polygonal, or many-sided. Finally, when the number of the sides becomes so numerous, and the sides themselves so small, that the figure cannot be distinguished from a circle, he is included in the Circular or Priestly order; and this is the highest class of all.

我们的中产阶级由等边三角形（即各边相等的三角形）组成。我们的专业人士和绅士是正方形（我本人即属于这一阶层）以及五边形。在这些之上是贵族阶层，其中有多个等级，从六边形开始，随着边数的增加而不断上升，直到被授予多边形（即多边的）的尊称。最后，当边数变得如此之多、边本身如此之小，以至于图形无法与圆区分开来时，他便被归入圆形或祭司阶层；这是所有阶层中的最高等级。

It is a Law of Nature with us that a male child shall have one more side than his father, so that each generation shall rise (as a rule) one step in the scale of development and nobility. Thus the son of a Square is a Pentagon; the son of a Pentagon, a Hexagon; and so on.

我们这里有一条自然法则：一个男孩应比他的父亲多一条边，因此每一代人（按常规）都在发展和贵族的阶梯上上升一级。因此，正方形的儿子是五边形；五边形的儿子是六边形；以此类推。

But this rule applies not always to the Tradesmen, and still less often to the Soldiers, and to the Workmen; who indeed can hardly be said to deserve the name of human Figures, since they have not all their sides equal. With them therefore the Law of Nature is not so absolute as with the higher classes; a fact which, as we shall see, is of the utmost importance for the welfare of the land---providing, as it does, a means for the expulsion of the superfluous population of the lower classes into the upper, and thus enabling the whole country to advance in culture and prosperity.

但这一规则并不总是适用于商人阶层，更少地适用于士兵和工人；实际上，他们几乎很难说配得上人类图形的称号，因为他们的各边并不全都相等。因此，在这些人身上，自然法则并不像在高等阶层中那样绝对；这一事实——我们将会看到——对这片土地的福祉至关重要，因为它提供了一种将下层阶级的多余人口输送到上层阶级的手段，从而使整个国家得以在文化和繁荣中前进。

But the sons of the Isosceles, when their parents are very poor, or when they are of a degraded type, are often themselves Isosceles, and sometimes even Irregulars, i.e., figures with sides of different lengths.

但是，等腰三角形的儿子们——当他们的父母非常贫困，或者他们属于低劣类型时——往往自己也是等腰三角形，有时甚至是不规则图形，即各边长度不同的图形。

The length of an adult's side, when added to the length of his neighbour's side, generally gives us an excellent means of judging of a man's rank. For instance, if two of my neighbours, being both of the Equilateral class, should disagree about their respective rank, I can settle the matter at once by measuring the length of one of their sides. But when they are of different classes, such a measurement is of no use, because the number of sides, and not the length of any particular side, determines the class. And for this reason, all our important public transactions are carried on by persons of the Equilateral class, who are considered the most accurate, and who therefore are entrusted with all our metrical standards.

一个成年人边的长度，加上他邻人边的长度，通常为我们提供了判断一个人等级的绝好方法。例如，如果我的两个邻人都属于等边形阶层，却因各自的等级发生争执，我只需测量他们某一条边的长度即可立刻解决此事。但当他们属于不同阶层时，这样的测量就没有用了，因为决定阶层的是边的数量，而不是某条特定边的长度。出于这个原因，我们所有重要的公共事务都由等边形阶层的人来处理，他们被认为是最精确的，因此被委托掌管我们所有的度量标准。

Our soldiers and the lowest class of workmen are, as I have said, Isosceles Triangles, with very sharp angles at the vertex. Consequently, when they are not under actual command, they are for the most part harmless, and even timid; but when excited by the drill sergeant, or by the sight of a foe, their acute angles are sharpened by a very natural process into still more piercing keenness. Many of them are even unable to see a Straight Line without the most dismal forebodings; and year by year our rulers have to provide for the removal of thousands of these poor creatures to the West, where they are used as a kind of rampart against the attacks of our enemies, who are the wild inhabitants of the West.

如前所述，我们的士兵和最底层的工人是等腰三角形，其顶角非常尖锐。因此，当他们不处于实际指挥之下时，他们大体上是无害的，甚至胆怯的；但当他们被教官激发起来，或看到敌人时，他们的锐角会通过一个非常自然的过程被磨得更加锋利，达到更具穿透性的尖锐程度。他们中的许多人甚至无法看到一条直线而不产生最凄惨的预感；年复一年，我们的统治者不得不将成千上万个这些可怜的家伙送往西方，在那里他们被用作一种壁垒，以抵御我们的敌人——即西方的野蛮居民——的进攻。

As a rule, all our soldiers are drawn from the lowest of the people; and they are kept in a state of profound ignorance, for otherwise they would use their sharp angles for the destruction of the domestic constitution. But in times of public danger, the rulers relax the severity of their discipline, and allow the soldier to use his angle freely against the common enemy. Thus, when the Western barbarians make an incursion, our soldiers are permitted to wreak their fury upon them; and during such times there is not a single Isosceles that is not furnished with a certificate from the State, authorizing him to kill as many of the enemy as his angle will permit.

按照常规，我们所有的士兵都是从人民中最低的阶层征募来的；他们被保持在一种极度无知的状态中，因为否则他们就会利用其尖锐的角来破坏国内的宪法。但在公共危险时期，统治者会放松严苛的纪律，允许士兵自由地使用他的角来对付共同的敌人。因此，当西方野蛮人发起入侵时，我们的士兵被允许向他们发泄怒火；在这种时候，每一个等腰三角形都从国家那里获得了一份证书，授权他用他的角杀死尽可能多的敌人。

\chapter{关于女性}

If our highly pointed Triangles of the Soldier class are formidable, it may be readily inferred that far more formidable are our Women. For, if a Soldier is a wedge, a Woman is a needle; being, so to speak, all point, at both extremities. Add to this the power of making herself practically invisible at will, and you will perceive that a Female, in Flatland, is a creature by no means to be trifled with.

如果说我们军人阶层中那些极其尖锐的三角形令人望而生畏，那么不难推知，我们的女性要更加令人生畏得多。因为，如果把士兵比作楔子，那么女人就是针；可以说，她在两端都是尖的。再加上她可以随意使自己实际上隐形的能力，你就会明白，在平面国中，女性是一个绝不可轻视的存在。

But here, perhaps, some of my younger Readers may ask how a woman in Flatland can make herself invisible. This ought, I think, to be apparent without any explanation. However, a few words will make it clear to the most unreflecting.

不过，也许我的一些年轻读者会问：平面国的女人怎么能使自己隐形呢？我认为，这本应无需任何解释便十分了然。不过，寥寥数语就能让最不善反思的人明白。

Place a needle on a table. Then, with your eye on the level of the table, look at it side-ways, and you will see the whole length of it; but look at it end-ways, and you will see nothing but a point: it has become practically invisible. Just so it is with one of our Women. When her side is turned towards us, we see her as a straight line; when the end which contains her eye or mouth---for with us these two organs are identical---is the part that meets our eye, then we see nothing but a highly lustrous point; but when the back is presented to our view, then---being only sub-lustrous, and, indeed, almost as dim as an inanimate object---her hinder extremity serves her as a kind of Invisible Cap.

将一根针放在桌子上。然后，将眼睛放在桌面的水平面上，从侧面看，你会看到它的整个长度；但从端面看，你只会看到一个点：它已经实际上隐形了。我们的女性正是如此。当她的侧面对着我们时，我们看到她是一条直线；当她那包含眼睛或嘴巴的末端——因为在平面国中，这两个器官是一体的——面向我们时，我们只看到一个极其明亮的光点；但当她的背部呈现在我们眼前时，由于那仅是半光亮的，实际上几乎和一个无生命的物体一样暗淡，她的后部便充当了一种隐形帽。

The dangers to which we are exposed from our Women must now be manifest to the meanest capacity in Spaceland. If even the angle of a respectable Triangle in the middle class is not without its dangers, if to run against a Working Man involves a gash, if collision with an officer of the military class necessitates a serious wound, if a mere touch from the vertex of a Private Soldier brings with it danger of death,---what can it be to run against a Woman, except absolute and immediate destruction?

我们的女性给我们带来的危险，如今即便在空间国中最愚钝的头脑看来，也必然是显而易见的了。如果说中等阶级中一位体面三角形的角都并非没有危险，如果说撞上一位工人就会留下一道切口，如果说与军人阶层的军官碰撞就会导致重伤，如果说仅仅被一个普通士兵的顶点碰到就会有死亡的危险——那么撞上一位女性，除了立即而绝对的毁灭之外，还能有什么呢？

And when a Woman is invisible, or visible only as a dim sub-lustrous point, how difficult must it be, even for the most cautious, always to avoid collision! Many are the enactments made at different times in the various States of Flatland, in order to minimize this peril; and in the Southern and less temperate climates, where the force of the Law is more needed, there is a special provision that every Female shall, on pain of death, utter a continuous cry, at intervals of not more than ten minutes, so as to give warning of her approach. The cry is commonly a kind of monotonous chant or howl; but in the case of a young and beautiful Woman it has been known to cause the destruction of a whole regiment of Isosceles soldiers, who, being thrown into confusion by the sweetness of the sound, have lost all presence of mind, and been instantly pierced through the brain by her vertex.

当一个女人是隐形的，或者只能被看到是一个暗淡的半光亮点时，即使对最谨慎的人来说，要时时避免碰撞又是何等的困难！在平面国各个不同的邦国中，不同时期都颁布了许多法令，以尽量减少这一危险；在南方及气候不那么温和的地区，法律的力量更为必要，那里有一条特殊规定：每个女性在疼痛死亡的威胁下，必须以不超过十分钟的间隔持续发出叫声，以警示她的到来。这叫声通常是一种单调的吟唱或嚎叫；但若是一位年轻貌美的女子，这种声音曾有导致整整一个团的等腰三角形士兵覆灭的记录——他们被甜美之声搅得阵脚大乱，全然丧失了镇定，瞬间便被她的顶点刺穿了大脑。

It must not be supposed, however, that our Women are entirely without the gentler affections. They are, on the contrary, capable of the most devoted attachment. But their affections are by nature concentrated upon their offspring, and upon their husbands. In the choice of a husband, the Female is allowed little or no voice. The marriage is arranged by the parents, who are naturally anxious that their daughter shall be well settled; and the principal consideration in the choice of a son-in-law is his position in society.

然而，切不可以为我们的女性完全没有温柔的情感。恰恰相反，她们能够拥有最为忠诚的依恋。但她们的情感本性上是集中在她们的子女和丈夫身上的。在选择丈夫时，女性几乎没有或根本没有发言权。婚姻由父母安排，父母自然渴望女儿能够妥善安顿；选择女婿的主要考虑因素，是他在社会中的地位。

The above remarks apply to the middle classes and the Aristocracy, among whom Women are, as a rule, treated with a certain amount of consideration. But among the lower classes, the lot of the Female is very different. There, she is regarded as a mere drudge, and is indeed not much more than a servant. Her form being that of a very acute-angled Isosceles, she is able to perform many household duties which the males, with their broader and more substantial angles, could not undertake. For instance, she can sweep the corners of rooms, and poke the fire in the stove; and it is even said that, in the Northern States, where the use of the Female for domestic purposes is carried to the greatest extent, the Women are taught from their earliest childhood to make themselves useful as pins, and to pick up crumbs with their acute extremities.

以上论述适用于中等阶级和贵族阶级，在这些阶层中，女性按惯例受到一定程度的体谅。但下层阶级中女性的命运则大不相同。在那里，她被视为一个纯粹的苦力，实际上并不比一个仆人强多少。她的形状是一个非常锐角的等腰三角形，因此能够承担许多家务职责，而男性以其更宽、更实质性的角是无法承担的。例如，她能清扫房间的角落、捅炉子里的火；甚至在北方各邦——女性被用于家务目的已达极致——据说，女性从幼年起就被教导要像针一样派上用场，用她们尖锐的顶端捡拾碎屑。

\chapter{我们相互识别的方法}

You, who are blessed with shade as well as light, you, who are gifted with two eyes, endowed with a knowledge of perspective, and charmed with the enjoyment of various colours, you, who can actually see an angle, and contemplate the entire circumference of a Circle in the happy region of the Three Dimensions---how shall I make clear to you the extreme difficulty which we in Flatland experience in recognizing one another's configuration?

你们，既享有亮光又有暗影，既被赋予双眼、拥有透视知识，又欣悦于各种色彩的享受；你们，能够实际看到一个角，能够在三维空间的幸福领域中注视整个圆的圆周——我该如何向你们说明我们平面国居民在辨识彼此的轮廓时，所经历的极其巨大的困难呢？

Recall what I told you above. All beings in Flatland, animate or inanimate, no matter what their form, present to our view the same, or nearly the same, appearance, viz., that of a straight Line. How then can one be distinguished from another, where all appear the same?

回想我上面告诉你的话。平面国中的所有存在物，有生命的或无生命的，无论其形态如何，在我们看来都是同样的、或几乎同样的外观，即一条直线。那么，在所有物体看起来都相同的情况下，我们又如何能将一个与另一个区分开来呢？

The answer is threefold. The first means of recognition is the sense of hearing; which with us is far more highly developed than with you, and which enables us not only to distinguish by the voice our personal friends, but even to discriminate between different classes, at least so far as concerns the three lowest orders, the Equilateral, the Square, and the Pentagon; for the Isosceles take no part in polite conversation, and are indeed not allowed to speak, except in the presence of their superiors.

答案有三个方面。第一个识别手段是听觉；在我们这里，听觉远较在你们那里更为发达，它不仅使我们能够通过声音区分我们个人的朋友，甚至能辨别不同的阶层，至少在三个最低的等级——等边三角形、正方形和五边形——方面是如此；因为等腰三角形不参与礼貌的交谈，事实上，除非在他们的上级面前，他们是不允许说话的。

But to make this mode of recognition as little dangerous as possible, it has been ordained that all women, and all men of the lower classes, shall use a common form of speech, which is a kind of sing-song, and which appears to us to have a certain rhythm, but which has no variation of pitch. The consequence is, that whenever they speak, they can be recognized at once, not to be of the higher classes, and so we are put upon our guard. But as for distinguishing the higher classes from one another by the ear, that is impossible, because they all speak exactly in the same tone of voice.

但为了使这种识别方式尽可能少地带来危险，已颁布律令规定，所有女性及所有下层阶级的男性，都必须使用一种共同的言语形式，这形式是一种吟唱调，在我们听来有一定节奏，但没有音高的变化。结果是，每当他们开口说话，立刻就可以被识别出来不属高等阶层，我们也就因此提高了警惕。但至于通过耳朵来区分高等阶层之间彼此的不同，那是不可能的，因为他们全都以完全相同的语调说话。

Secondly, there is the method of Feeling. But this is not without danger, and, among the higher classes, it is considered to be a mark of the grossest vulgarity. For instance, when a stranger is introduced to a Square, the Square, if he be a gentleman, will never himself touch the stranger, but simply ask him his name and his state. But if the stranger be of the lower classes, he must be content to be felt all over, and to have his angles counted. And, as I need hardly say, the operation is a very painful one, not only on account of the rudeness of the touch, but also because the feeling is generally accompanied by a very severe pressure, which is necessary in order to ascertain whether the angle be really an angle, or merely a bright point; i.e., whether the stranger be a Polygon, or a Woman.

其次，有触摸法。但这不是没有危险的，而且在高等阶层中，这被视作最粗俗的标志。例如，当一个陌生人被引见给一个正方形时，正方形如果是位绅士，绝不会亲自去触摸陌生人，而只是询问他的姓名和身份。但如果陌生人是下层阶级的，他就必须甘愿被人摸遍全身，让人数清他的角。而且，几乎不用说，这个操作是非常痛苦的，不仅因为触摸的粗鲁，还因为触摸过程通常伴随着非常用力的按压——这按压是为了确认那个角确实是一个角，而不只是一个光点；也就是说，要确认这个陌生人到底是一个多边形，还是一个女人。

But the third, and, to the highest classes, the most usual means of recognition is by Sight. Before I describe this method, however, I must premise that our power of distinguishing by sight is extremely limited. As already stated, all objects appear to us as Straight Lines. And yet, by a kind of instinct, which I cannot explain, but which is possessed by all Flatlanders, we are able to infer the inclinations of Lines, and consequently the shapes of the objects from which they emanate. But this inference is very imperfect, and requires long practice.

但第三种，也是对于最高阶层来说最常用的识别手段，是通过视觉。然而，在描述此法之前，我必须先行交代：我们通过视觉进行分辨的能力是极其有限的。前已提及，所有物体在我们看来都是直线。然而，出于一种我也无法解释、但所有平面国居民都拥有的本能，我们能够推断出直线的倾斜方向，并由此推断出它们所从属的物体的形状。但这种推断是非常不完善的，并且需要长期的练习。

Thus, in the case of one of ourselves, if a straight Line approach us, our impression is at first merely that a straight Line is approaching. As it comes nearer, we are able to see whether it is accompanied by any motion of the ambient light, and if so, we infer that the Line is not one of our animate fellow-creatures, but a mere inanimate object, such as a wall, or a piece of furniture. But, in the case of a fellow-creature, his approach is indicated by the same kind of instinct as that which tells a sailor that a ship is approaching, even when he cannot see anything but the mast; and a very little practice enables us to distinguish between the approach of a Woman, a Soldier, and a Polygon.

因此，拿我们中的一员来说，如果一条直线向我们靠近，我们最初的感觉仅仅是"有一条直线正在靠近"。当它来得更近时，我们能够看到它是否伴有周围光线的任何移动；如果有，我们就推断这条线不是我们的某位有生命的同胞，而只是一个无生命的物体，如一堵墙或一件家具。但是，在遇到同胞的情况下，他的靠近是由一种本能来指示的——正如水手即使除了桅杆什么也看不见，也能知道有一条船正在靠近一样；只需很少的练习，我们就能将女人、士兵和多边形的靠近区分开来。

But the exact shape of an Equilateral Triangle, a Square, or a Pentagon, can rarely be determined by the sense of Sight alone; and in order to determine this, we must resort to Feeling, or to the method of Recognition by Sight, which, as I have said, is practised only by the highest classes, and only upon occasions of the greatest importance.

但是，一个等边三角形、一个正方形或一个五边形的精确形状，是很少能仅凭视觉来确定的；为了确定这一点，我们必须借助触摸，或借助我们在前面说过的"视觉识别法"——而此法，如前文所言，只有最高阶层的人才使用，且只在最为重要的场合下才使用。

\chapter{视觉识别法}

I am about to appear very inconsistent. In previous sections I have said that all figures in Flatland present the appearance of a straight line; and it was added or implied, that it is therefore impossible to distinguish by the visual organ between individuals of different classes: yet now I am about to explain to my Spaceland critics how we are able to recognize one another by the sense of sight.

我马上就要显得非常前后矛盾了。在前面的章节中，我曾说过，平面国中所有的图形都呈现为直线的外观；并且还补充或暗示，因此不可能通过视觉器官区分不同阶层的个体：然而，现在我却要向我在空间国的批评者们解释，我们是如何能够通过视觉来识别彼此的。

If, however, my Reader will take the trouble to refer to the passage in which Recognition by Feeling is stated to be universal, he will find this qualification---``among the lower classes.'' It is only among the higher classes and in our more temperate climes that Sight Recognition is practised.

然而，如果我的读者愿意费心查阅关于"触摸识别法"被说成是普遍通行的那段文字，他会发现这样一个限定词——"在下层阶级中"。只有高等阶层以及在我们气候较为温和的地区，视觉识别法才被实行。

That this power exists in any regions and for any classes, is the result of Fog; which prevails during the greater part of the year in all parts save the torrid zones. That which is with you in Spaceland an unmixed evil, blotting out the landscape, depressing the spirits, and enfeebling the health, is by us found not to be wholly unserviceable: it has been, indeed, our ally, our friend, our deliverer, in our greatest need.

这一能力之所以能在某些地区和某些阶层中存在，是雾的结果；除了热带地区之外，雾在一年的大部分时间里都笼罩着所有地方。那种在你们空间国中全然为害的东西——遮蔽风景、压抑精神、损害健康——在我们这里却被发现并非全无用处：事实上，它在我们最需要的时候，是我们的盟友、我们的朋友、我们的拯救者。

But let me explain my meaning, without further eulogies on this beneficent Element.

不过，让我先解释我的意思，不必再对这个仁慈的元素多加颂扬了。

If Fog were non-existent, all lines would appear equally and indistinguishably clear; and this is actually the case in those unhappy countries in which the atmosphere is perfectly dry and transparent. But wherever there is a rich supply of Fog, objects that are at a distance, say of three feet, are appreciably dimmer than those at a distance of two feet eleven inches; and the result is that by careful and constant experimental observation of comparative dimness and clearness, we are enabled to infer the configuration of the object observed with very considerable accuracy.

假如没有雾的存在，所有线条都会显得同样地且不可区分地清晰；而这实际上正是那些不幸的、空气完全干燥透明的地区的情况。但是，只要有着丰富的雾气供应，距离三英尺的物体就明显地比距离两英尺十一英寸的物体更暗淡；结果是，通过对相对暗淡程度和清晰程度进行仔细而持续的实验观察，我们便能够以相当高的精确度推断出所观察物体的轮廓。

An instance will do more than a volume of generalities to make my meaning clear. Suppose I see two individuals approaching whose rank I wish to ascertain. They are, we will suppose, a Merchant and a Physician---or, in other words, an Equilateral Triangle and a Pentagon: how am I to distinguish them?

一个例子比我所能阐明的一整卷泛泛之谈更能够表明我的意思。假设我看到两个人正在靠近，我想确定他们的等级。假定他们是一位商人（等边三角形）和一位医师（五边形）——我该如何区分他们呢？

It will be obvious, to every child in Spaceland who has touched the threshold of Geometrical Studies, that, if I can bring my eye so that its glance may bisect an angle (A) of the approaching stranger, my view will lie as it were evenly between his two sides that are next to me (viz., CA and AB), so that I shall contemplate the two impartially, and both will appear of the same size.

对于空间国中任何一个已踏入几何学门槛的孩子来说，这一点是很明显的：如果我能将视线调整到恰好能够平分靠近的陌生人某一个角（角A）的位置，我的视线就会仿佛是公平地处于他那两条面向我的边（即CA和AB）之间，这样一来，我就能不偏不倚地注视这两条边，而这两条边会显得大小相同。

Now in the case of (1) the Merchant, what shall I see? I shall see a straight line DAE, in which the middle point (A) will be very bright because it is nearest to me; but on either side the line will shade away rapidly into dimness, because the sides AC and AB recede rapidly into the fog; and what appear to me as the Merchant's extremities, viz., D and E, will be very dim indeed.

那么，在商人（例1）这一情形中，我将会看到什么？我会看到一条直线DAE，在这条线中，中点（A）会非常明亮，因为它离我最近；但在这条线的两侧，亮度会迅速消褪为昏暗，因为边AC和AB迅速地退入雾气之中；而在我看来是商人两端的D和E，则会确实非常暗淡。

On the other hand in the case of (2) the Physician, though I shall here also see a line (D'A'E') with a bright centre (A'), yet it will shade away less rapidly into dimness, because the sides (A'C', A'B') recede less rapidly into the fog; and what appear to me the Physician's extremities, viz., D' and E', will be not so dim as the extremities of the Merchant.

另一方面，对于医师（例2）的情形，虽然我也会看到一条带有明亮中心（A'）的直线（D'A'E'），但它的亮度消退为暗淡的速度要慢一些，因为边（A'C', A'B'）退入雾中的速度不那么快；因此在我眼中是医师两端的D'和E'，就不会像商人的两端那样暗淡。

The reader will probably understand from these two instances how---after a very long training supplemented by constant experience---it is possible for the well-educated classes among us to discriminate with fair accuracy between the middle and the lowest orders, by the sense of sight. If my Spaceland Patrons have grasped this general conception, so far as to conceive the possibility of it and not to reject my account as altogether incredible---I shall have attained all I can reasonably expect. Were I to attempt further details, I should only perplex.

读者也许能从这两个例子中理解到——经过非常长时间的训练和持续经验的补充——我们当中受过良好教育的阶层是如何能够通过视觉，相当准确地辨别中等阶层和最低阶层的。如果我在空间国的庇护者们已经掌握了这一总体概念，并且至少能够设想其可能性、而不是将我的叙述视为全不可信而予以拒绝——那么，我就已经达到了我所能合理期望的一切。如果我试图对更多细节加以阐述，只会让人徒增困惑。

\chapter{关于不规则图形}

Throughout the previous pages I have been assuming---what perhaps should have been laid down at the beginning as a distinct and fundamental proposition---that every human being in Flatland is a Regular Figure, that is to say of regular construction. By this I mean that a Woman must not only be a line, but a straight line; that an Artisan or Soldier must have two of his sides equal; that Tradesmen must have three sides equal; Lawyers (of which class I am a humble member), four sides equal, and, generally, that in every Polygon, all the sides must be equal.

在前面各页中，我一直假设——这个假设也许本应在开篇之处就作为一条明确的基本命题提出——平面国中每个人都是一个规则图形，也就是说具有规则的构造。我的意思是：女性不仅必须是一条线，而且必须是一条直线；工匠或士兵必须有两条相等的边；商人必须有三条相等的边；律师（我正是这个阶层的卑微一员）必须有四条相等的边；而且，一般来说，对于每一个多边形，所有的边都必须相等。

The sizes of the sides would of course depend upon the age of the individual. A Female at birth would be about an inch long, while a tall adult Woman might extend to a foot. As to the Males of every class, it may be roughly said that the length of an adult's size, when added together, is two feet or a little more. But the size of our sides is not under consideration. I am speaking of the \_equality\_ of sides, and it does not need much reflection to see that the whole of the social life in Flatland rests upon the fundamental fact that Nature wills all Figures to have their sides equal.

边的大小当然取决于个人的年龄。一个女性出生时约一英寸长，而一个高个子成年女性可能延伸至一英尺。至于每个阶层的男性，粗略地说，一个成年人的边长加起来总共是两英尺或略多一点。但我们现在讨论的不是边的大小。我所说的是边的\textbf{相等性}，这一点无需多想就能明白：平面国的整个社会生活都建立在一个基本事实之上，即大自然意志所有图形的边都相等。

If our sides were unequal our angles might be unequal. Instead of its being sufficient to feel, or estimate by sight, a single angle in order to determine the form of an individual, it would be necessary to ascertain each angle by the experiment of Feeling. But life would be too short for such a tedious groping. The whole science and art of Sight Recognition would at once perish; Feeling, so far as it is an art, would not long survive; intercourse would become perilous or impossible; there would be an end to all confidence, all forethought; no one would be safe in making the most simple social arrangements; in a word, civilization might relapse into barbarism.

如果我们的边不相等，我们的角也可能不相等。那样的话，为了确定一个人的形状，仅凭触摸或目测一个角就足够了，而必须通过触摸的实验来确定每一个角。但人生太短暂，经不起如此繁琐的摸索。整个视觉识别的科学与艺术将立刻毁灭；触摸——就其是一门艺术而言——也将不久于人世；交往将变得危险或不可能；一切信心、一切深谋远虑都将终结；没有人在做最简单的社交安排时还能感到安全；简言之，文明可能会退化为野蛮状态。

Am I going too fast to carry my Readers with me to these obvious conclusions? Surely a moment's reflection, and a single instance from common life, must convince every one that our social system is based upon Regularity, or Equality of Angles. You meet, for example, two or three Tradesmen in the street, whom you recognize at once to be Tradesman by a glance at their angles and rapidly bedimmed sides, and you ask them to step into your house to lunch. This you do at present with perfect confidence, because everyone knows to an inch or two the area occupied by an adult Triangle: but imagine that your Tradesman drags behind his regular and respectable vertex, a parallelogram of twelve or thirteen inches in diagonal:---what are you to do with such a monster sticking fast in your house door?

我是否走得太快，以至于我的读者们跟不上我得出这些显而易见的结论？当然，片刻的思考，以及日常生活中的一个简单例子，就足以说服每一个人：我们的社会制度建立在规则性，即角的相等性之上。例如，你在街上遇到两三个商人，你看一眼他们的角和迅速变暗的边，就立刻认出他们是商人，并请他们到你家里吃午饭。你现在这样做是完全有信心的，因为每个人都知道一个成年三角形所占的面积只有一两英寸的误差：但想象一下，你的商人在他那规则而体面的顶点后面，拖着一个对角线为十二或十三英寸的平行四边形——你将如何处置这样一个卡在你家门里的怪物？

But I am insulting the intelligence of my Readers by accumulating details which must be patent to everyone who enjoys the advantages of a Residence in Spaceland. Obviously the measurements of a single angle would no longer be sufficient under such portentous circumstances; one's whole life would be taken up in feeling or surveying the perimeter of one's acquaintances. Already the difficulties of avoiding a collision in a crowd are enough to tax the sagacity of even a well-educated Square; but if no one could calculate the Regularity of a single figure in the company, all would be chaos and confusion, and the slightest panic would cause serious injuries, or---if there happened to be any Women or Soldiers present---perhaps considerable loss of life.

但我这样堆砌细节，简直是在侮辱我读者的智商——对于有幸居住在空间国的每一个人来说，这些细节都是显而易见的。显然，在如此重大的情况下，测量单个角已不再足够；一个人的整个生命都将耗费在触摸或勘测熟人的周长上。在人群中避免碰撞的困难，已足以考验一个受过良好教育的正方形的智慧；但如果没有人能够计算出同伴中任何一个图形的规则性，一切都将陷入混乱与困惑，最轻微的恐慌也会造成严重伤害，或者——如果碰巧有女性或士兵在场——甚至可能造成重大生命损失。

Expediency therefore concurs with Nature in stamping the seal of its approval upon Regularity of conformation: nor has the Law been backward in seconding their efforts. ``Irregularity of Figure'' means with us the same as, or more than, a combination of moral obliquity and criminality with you, and is treated accordingly. There are not wanting, it is true, some promulgators of paradoxes who maintain that there is no necessary connection between geometrical and moral Irregularity. ``The Irregular,'' they say, ``is from his birth scouted by his own parents, derided by his brothers and sisters, neglected by the domestics, scorned and suspected by society, and excluded from all posts of responsibility, trust, and useful activity. His every movement is jealously watched by the police till he comes of age and presents himself for inspection; then he is either destroyed, if he is found to exceed the fixed margin of deviation, at an uninteresting occupation for a miserable stipend; obliged to live and board at the office, and to take even his vacation under close supervision; what wonder that human nature, even in the best and purest, is embittered and perverted by such surroundings!''

因此，权宜之计与大自然一道，在构造的规则性上盖上了认可的印章；而法律也没有落后于支持它们的努力。在我们这里，"图形的不规则"所意味的，无异于——甚至更甚于——你们那里道德败坏与犯罪性的结合，并受到相应的对待。诚然，不乏一些悖论的鼓吹者，他们坚持认为几何的不规则与道德的不规则之间没有必然联系。"不规则者，"他们说，"从出生起就被自己的父母所嫌弃，被兄弟姐妹们所嘲笑，被仆人所忽视，被社会所轻蔑和猜疑，被排斥于所有负有责任、值得信任和有用的活动之外。他的每一个动作都受到警察的嫉妒般的监视，直到他成年并接受检查；然后，如果他被发现超出了固定的偏差幅度，他要么被消灭，要么被安排在一种无趣的职业中，领取微薄的薪水；他被要求在办公室里吃住，甚至连休假也在严密的监督之下；在这样的环境中，即使是最好、最纯洁的人性也会变得苦涩和扭曲，这有什么好奇怪的呢！"

All this very plausible reasoning does not convince me, as it has not convinced the wisest of our Statesmen, that our ancestors erred in laying it down as an axiom of policy that the toleration of Irregularity is incompatible with the safety of the State. Doubtless, the life of an Irregular is hard; but the interests of the Greater Number require that it shall be hard. If a man with a triangular front and a polygonal back were allowed to exist and to propagate a still more Irregular posterity, what would become of the arts of life? Are the houses and doors and churches in Flatland to be altered in order to accommodate such monsters? Are our ticket-collectors to be required to measure every man's perimeter before they allow him to enter a theatre, or to take his place in a lecture room? Is an Irregular to be exempted from the militia? And if not, how is he to be prevented from carrying desolation into the ranks of his comrades? Again, what irresistible temptations to fraudulent impostures must needs beset such a creature! How easy for him to enter a shop with his polygonal front foremost, and to order goods to any extent from a confiding tradesman! Let the advocates of a falsely called Philanthropy plead as they may for the abrogation of the Irregular Penal Laws, I for my part have never known an Irregular who was not also what Nature evidently intended him to be---a hypocrite, a misanthropist, and, up to the limits of his power, a perpetrator of all manner of mischief.

所有这些似乎非常有理的论证，并不能说服我——正如它也没有说服我们最智慧的政治家们——认为我们的祖先把容忍不规则与国家安全不相容作为政策公理是错误的。毫无疑问，不规则者的生活是艰难的；但多数人的利益要求它必须艰难。如果一个前面是三角形、后面是多边形的人被允许存在并繁衍出更加不规则的后代，那么生活的艺术将会变成什么？平面国的房屋、门窗和教堂难道要为了容纳这些怪物而改造吗？我们的检票员难道要被要求在允许每个人进入剧院或在讲堂就座之前，先测量他的周长吗？一个不规则者难道要被免除兵役吗？如果不是，如何阻止他在同伴的队伍中散布混乱？再者，这样的生物必然会受到多么不可抗拒的欺诈诱惑！他只要把多边形的一面朝前，走进一家商店，就可以从一个信任他的商人那里订购任意数量的货物，这是多么容易！那些鼓吹所谓慈善的人可以尽情地为废除不规则者刑法而辩护，但就我而言，我从未认识一个不规则者不是大自然显然有意造就的那种人——一个伪君子，一个厌世者，一个在其力所能及的范围内犯下各种恶作剧的人。

Not that I should be disposed to recommend (at present) the extreme measures adopted by some States, where an infant whose angle deviates by half a degree from the correct angularity is summarily destroyed at birth. Some of our highest and ablest men, men of real genius, have during their earliest days laboured under deviations as great as, or even greater than forty-five minutes: and the loss of their precious lives would have been an irreparable injury to the State. The art of healing also has achieved some of its most glorious triumphs in the compressions, extensions, trepannings, colligations, and other surgical or diaetetic operations by which Irregularity has been partly or wholly cured. Advocating therefore a \_Via Media\_, I would lay down no fixed or absolute line of demarcation; but at the period when the frame is just beginning to set, and when the Medical Board has reported that recovery is improbable, I would suggest that the Irregular offspring be painlessly and mercifully consumed.

我并不是倾向于（目前）推荐有些国家所采取的极端措施——在那些国家，一个角度偏离正确角度半度的婴儿会在出生时被立即销毁。我们当中一些最高级、最有能力的人，真正的天才人物，在他们最早的日子里，也曾承受过大至四十五分甚至更大的角度偏差；如果失去了他们宝贵的生命，对国家来说将是不可弥补的损失。治愈的艺术也在压缩、扩展、穿孔、接合以及其他外科或饮食操作中取得了一些最辉煌的胜利，通过这些操作，不规则性被部分或完全治愈。因此，我主张\textbf{中庸之道}，我不会划定一条固定或绝对的分界线；但在身体结构刚刚开始定型的时期，当医学委员会报告说康复不太可能时，我建议将不规则后代无痛地、仁慈地予以销毁。

\chapter{古老的染色术}

If my Readers have followed me with any attention up to this point, they will not be surprised to hear that life is somewhat dull in Flatland. I do not, of course, mean that there are not battles, conspiracies, tumults, factions, and all those other phenomena which are supposed to make History interesting; nor would I deny that the strange mixture of the problems of life and the problems of Mathematics, continually inducing conjecture and giving an opportunity of immediate verification, imparts to our existence a zest which you in Spaceland can hardly comprehend. I speak now from the aesthetic and artistic point of view when I say that life with us is dull; aesthetically and artistically, very dull indeed.

如果我的读者们一直认真地跟随我到目前为止，他们听到平面国的生活有些乏味，就不会感到惊讶。当然，我并不是说没有战斗、阴谋、骚乱、派系以及所有那些被认为使历史有趣的现象；我也不会否认，生活问题与数学问题的奇怪混合，不断引发猜测并提供即时验证的机会，给我们的生存增添了一种你们空间国的人难以理解的趣味。我现在是从美学和艺术的角度来说，我们的生活是乏味的；从美学和艺术的角度来看，确实非常乏味。

How can it be otherwise, when all one's prospect, all one's landscapes, historical pieces, portraits, flowers, still life, are nothing but a single line, with no varieties except degrees of brightness and obscurity?

怎么可能不是这样呢？当一个人所有的远景、所有的风景、历史画、肖像、花卉、静物，都不过是一条线，没有任何变化，只有明暗程度的不同？

It was not always thus. Colour, if Tradition speaks the truth, once for the space of half a dozen centuries or more, threw a transient splendour over the lives of our ancestors in the remotest ages. Some private individual---a Pentagon whose name is variously reported---having casually discovered the constituents of the simpler colours and a rudimentary method of painting, is said to have begun by decorating first his house, then his slaves, then his Father, his Sons, and Grandsons, lastly himself. The convenience as well as the beauty of the results commended themselves to all. Wherever Chromatistes,---for by that name the most trustworthy authorities concur in calling him,---turned his variegated frame, there he at once excited attention, and attracted respect. No one now needed to ``feel'' him; no one mistook his front for his back; all his movements were readily ascertained by his neighbours without the slightest strain on their powers of calculation; no one jostled him, or failed to make way for him; his voice was saved the labour of that exhausting utterance by which we colourless Squares and Pentagons are often forced to proclaim our individuality when we move amid a crowd of ignorant Isosceles.

情况并非总是如此。如果传统说法可信，色彩曾经在六七个世纪或更长的时间里，为我们远古祖先的生活投下了短暂的光辉。有一个人——一个名字说法不一的五边形——偶然发现了较简单色彩的成分和一种初步的染色方法，据说他最先开始装饰自己的房子，然后是他的奴隶，然后是他的父亲、儿子和孙子，最后是他自己。这种结果的便利性和美观性为所有人所赞许。无论克罗马蒂斯特斯——最可靠的权威们一致这样称呼他——把他那五彩缤纷的身躯转向何处，他立刻都会引起注意并赢得尊重。现在没有人需要"触摸"他；没有人会把他的正面误认为背面；他所有的动作都能被邻居们毫不费力地察觉，无需最轻微的计算负担；没有人会撞到他，也没有人不让路给他；他的声音省去了那种令人精疲力竭的呼喊——我们这些无色的正方形和五边形在一群无知的等腰三角形中移动时，常常被迫用这种呼喊来宣示自己的个体性。

The fashion spread like wildfire. Before a week was over, every Square and Triangle in the district had copied the example of Chromatistes, and only a few of the more conservative Pentagons still held out. A month or two found even the Dodecagons infected with the innovation. A year had not elapsed before the habit had spread to all but the very highest of the Nobility. Needless to say, the custom soon made its way from the district of Chromatistes to surrounding regions; and within two generations no one in all Flatland was colourless except the Women and the Priests.

这种时尚如野火般蔓延。不到一个星期，该地区的每一个正方形和三角形都效仿了克罗马蒂斯特斯的榜样，只有少数更为保守的五边形还在坚持。一两个月后，就连十二边形也受到了这一革新的感染。还不到一年，这种习惯就传遍了除最高贵族之外的所有阶层。不用说，这一习俗很快就从克罗马蒂斯特斯的地区传到了周围的地区；两代人之后，整个平面国就没有人是无色的了——除了女性和祭司。

Here Nature herself appeared to erect a barrier, and to plead against extending the innovations to these two classes. Many-sidedness was almost essential as a pretext for the Innovators. ``Distinction of sides is intended by Nature to imply distinction of colours''---such was the sophism which in those days flew from mouth to mouth, converting whole towns at a time to a new culture. But manifestly to our Priests and Women this adage did not apply. The latter had only one side, and therefore---plurally and pedantically speaking---\_no sides\_. The former---if at least they would assert their claim to be readily and truly Circles, and not mere high-class Polygons, with an infinitely large number of infinitesimally small sides---were in the habit of boasting (what Women confessed and deplored) that they also had no sides, being blessed with a perimeter of only one line, or, in other words, a Circumference. Hence it came to pass that these two Classes could see no force in the so-called axiom about ``Distinction of Sides implying Distinction of Colour;'' and when all others had succumbed to the fascinations of corporal decoration, the Priests and the Women alone still remained pure from the pollution of paint.

在这一点上，大自然本身似乎树立了一道屏障，反对将这一革新扩展到这两个阶层。多边性几乎是革新者们必不可少的借口。"边的区别是大自然有意要暗示颜色的区别"——这就是当时口耳相传的诡辩，一次就把整个城镇都转变到了一种新的文化之中。但显然，对于我们的祭司和女性来说，这个格言并不适用。后者只有一条边，因此——用复数和学究的话来说——\_没有边\_。前者——如果他们至少要坚持他们真正的、可被识别的圆的主张，而不是仅仅拥有无限数量的无限小边的高阶多边形——习惯于吹嘘（女性承认并痛惜这一点）他们也没有边，有幸拥有的只是一条线的周长，换句话说，即圆周。因此，这两个阶层在所谓的"边的区别暗示颜色的区别"这一公理中看不到任何说服力；当所有其他人都屈服于身体装饰的魅力时，唯有祭司和女性仍然保持着纯洁，不受颜料的污染。

Immoral, licentious, anarchical, unscientific---call them by what names you will---yet, from an aesthetic point of view, those ancient days of the Colour Revolt were the glorious childhood of Art in Flatland---a childhood, alas, that never ripened into manhood, nor even reached the blossom of youth. To live then in itself a delight, because living implied seeing. Even at a small party, the company was a pleasure to behold; the richly varied hues of the assembly in a church or theatre are said to have more than once proved too distracting from our greatest teachers and actors; but most ravishing of all is said to have been the unspeakable magnificence of a military review.

不道德的、放荡的、无政府的、不科学的——无论你怎么称呼它们——然而，从美学的角度来看，那些色彩暴动的古老日子是平面国艺术的光辉童年——唉，这个童年从未成熟为成年，甚至从未达到青春的绽放。那时的生活本身就是一种快乐，因为生活意味着看见。即使在一个小型聚会上，那群人也是一道可观的风景；教堂或剧院里集会的丰富多彩的色调，据说不止一次地让我们最伟大的教师和演员分心；但最令人陶醉的，据说是一场军事检阅的那种难以言喻的壮丽。

The sight of a line of battle of twenty thousand Isosceles suddenly facing about, and exchanging the sombre black of their bases for the orange of the two sides including their acute angle; the militia of the Equilateral Triangles tricoloured in red, white, and blue; the mauve, ultra-marine, gamboge, and burnt umber of the Square artillerymen rapidly rotating near their vermillion guns; the dashing and flashing of the five-coloured and six-coloured Pentagons and Hexagons careering across the field in their offices of surgeons, geometricians and aides-de-camp---all these may well have been sufficient to render credible the famous story how an illustrious Circle, overcome by the artistic beauty of the forces under his command, threw aside his marshal's baton and his royal crown, exclaiming that he henceforth exchanged them for the artist's pencil. How great and glorious the sensuous development of these days must have been is in part indicated by the very language and vocabulary of the period. The commonest utterances of the commonest citizens in the time of the Colour Revolt seem to have been suffused with a richer tinge of word or thought; and to that era we are even now indebted for our finest poetry and for whatever rhythm still remains in the more scientific utterance of those modern days.

两万个等腰三角形的战斗行列突然转向，将其底部的深黑色换成包括其锐角在内的两条边的橙色；等边三角形的民兵身着红、白、蓝三色；正方形炮兵们在其朱红色大炮旁迅速旋转时所呈现的紫红色、群青色、藤黄色和焦褐色；五边形和六边形的外科医生、几何学家和副官们身着五色和六色的盛装在战场上疾驰而过、光彩夺目——所有这些都足以让人相信那个著名的故事：一位杰出的圆形，被他麾下部队的艺术之美所征服，抛开了元帅的权杖和王冠，高呼他从此将它们换成艺术家的画笔。那个时代的语言和词汇，在一定程度上表明了这些日子的感官发展是多么伟大而辉煌。色彩暴动时期最普通的公民的最普通的话语，似乎都浸染了一种更为丰富的言辞或思想色彩；我们至今仍受益于那个时代留给我们的最精美的诗歌，以及那些仍然存在于现代科学表述中的韵律。

\chapter{全民颜色法案}

But meanwhile the intellectual Arts were fast decaying.

但与此同时，智识艺术正在迅速衰落。

The Art of Sight Recognition, being no longer needed, was no longer practised; and the studies of Geometry, Statics, Kinetics, and other kindred subjects, came soon to be considered superfluous, and fell into disrespect and neglect even at our University. The inferior Art of Feeling speedily experienced the same fate at our Elementary Schools. Then the Isosceles classes, asserting that the Specimens were no longer used nor needed, and refusing to pay the customary tribute from the Criminal classes to the service of Education, waxed daily more numerous and more insolent on the strength of their immunity from the old burden which had formerly exercised the twofold wholesome effect of at once taming their brutal nature and thinning their excessive numbers.

视觉识别术，由于不再被需要，也就不再被实践；而几何学、静力学、动力学及其他相关学科的研究，很快就被视为多余的，甚至在我们的大学中也遭到轻视和忽视。较低级的触摸术在我们的小学中也迅速遭遇了同样的命运。然后，等腰三角形阶层声称那些标本不再被使用也不再被需要，并拒绝向教育事业缴纳罪犯阶层的常规贡品，他们凭借着摆脱了昔日负担的豁免地位，变得日益壮大、日益傲慢——那种负担从前曾发挥了双重的有益作用，既驯服了他们的野蛮本性，又减少了他们过剩的数量。

Year by year the Soldiers and Artisans began more vehemently to assert---and with increasing truth---that there was no great difference between them and the very highest class of Polygons, now that they were raised to an equality with the latter, and enabled to grapple with all the difficulties and solve all the problems of life, whether Statical or Kinetical, by the simple process of Colour Recognition. Not content with the natural neglect into which Sight Recognition was falling, they began boldly to demand the legal prohibition of all ``monopolizing and aristocratic Arts'' and the consequent abolition of all endowments for the studies of Sight Recognition, Mathematics, and Feeling. Soon, they began to insist that inasmuch as Colour, which was a second Nature, had destroyed the need of aristocratic distinctions, the Law should follow in the same path, and that henceforth all individuals and all classes should be recognized as absolutely equal and entitled to equal rights.

年复一年，士兵和工匠们开始更加激烈地断言——而且越来越有道理——他们与最高等级的多边形之间没有什么重大区别，既然他们现在已经被提升到与后者平等的地位，并且能够通过简单的颜色识别过程来应对生活中的一切困难和解决一切问题——无论是静力学的还是动力学的。他们不满足于视觉识别术正在遭受的自然忽视，还开始大胆地要求法律禁止所有"垄断性的和贵族式的艺术"，并随之废除所有为视觉识别、数学和触摸研究而设立的捐赠基金。不久，他们开始坚持认为，既然颜色这"第二自然"已经摧毁了对贵族区别的需要，那么法律也应该沿着同样的道路前进，从今以后，所有个人和所有阶层都应被承认为绝对平等并享有平等权利。

Finding the higher Orders wavering and undecided, the leaders of the Revolution advanced still further in their requirements, and at last demanded that all classes alike, the Priests and the Women not excepted, should do homage to Colour by submitting to be painted. When it was objected that Priests and Women had no sides, they retorted that Nature and Expediency concurred in dictating that the front half of every human being (that is to say, the half containing his eye and mouth) should be distinguishable from his hinder half. They therefore brought before a general and extraordinary Assembly of all the States of Flatland a Bill proposing that in every Woman the half containing the eye and mouth should be coloured red, and the other half green. The Priests were to be painted in the same way, red being applied to that semicircle in which the eye and mouth formed the middle point; while the other or hinder semicircle was to be coloured green.

发现高阶阶层动摇不定、犹豫不决，革命的领袖们在他们的要求上更进一步，最终要求所有阶层——包括祭司和女性在内无一例外——都应通过同意被涂色来向颜色致敬。当有人反对说祭司和女性没有边时，他们反驳道，大自然和权宜之计一致规定，每个人的前半部分（即包含眼睛和嘴巴的那一半）应与后半部分有所区别。因此，他们向平面国所有国家的特别大会提出了一项法案，提议在每一个女性身上，包含眼睛和嘴巴的那一半应涂成红色，另一半涂成绿色。祭司也应被同样涂彩，红色应用于以眼睛和嘴巴为中点的那个半圆；而另一个或后面的半圆则涂成绿色。

There was no little cunning in this proposal, which indeed emanated not from any Isosceles---for no being so degraded would have angularity enough to appreciate, much less to devise, such a model of state-craft---but from an Irregular Circle who, instead of being destroyed in his childhood, was reserved by a foolish indulgence to bring desolation on his country and destruction on myriads of followers.

这一提议中不乏狡猾之处，它并非出自任何等腰三角形之手——因为如此低等的存在不可能有足够的角度去欣赏，更不用说去设计这样一种政治谋略的典范——而是出自一个不规则的圆形之手，他没有在童年时被销毁，而是被一种愚蠢的放纵所保留下来，给他的国家带来毁灭，给无数追随者带来灾难。

On the one hand the proposition was calculated to bring the Women in all classes over to the side of the Chromatic Innovation. For by assigning to the Women the same two colours as were assigned to the Priests, the Revolutionists thereby ensured that, in certain positions, every Woman would appear as a Priest, and be treated with corresponding respect and deference---a prospect that could not fail to attract the Female Sex in a mass.

一方面，这一命题旨在将所有阶层的女性都争取到色彩革新的一边。因为通过赋予女性与祭司相同的两种颜色，革命者从而确保了在某些位置上，每一个女性都会以祭司的身份出现，并获得相应的尊重和敬意——这一前景必然会吸引广大女性。

But by some of my Readers the possibility of the identical appearance of Priests and Women, under a new Legislation, may not be recognized; if so, a word or two will make it obvious.

但我的一些读者可能还没有意识到，在新的立法下，祭司和女性出现相同外貌的可能性；如果是这样，一两句话就能说明白。

Imagine a woman duly decorated, according to the new Code; with the front half (\_i.e.\_, the half containing the eye and mouth) red, and with the hinder half green. Look at her from one side. Obviously you will see a straight line, \_half red, half green\_.

想象一个女性按照新法典的规定恰当地装饰好了；前半部分（即包含眼睛和嘴巴的那一半）为红色，后半部分为绿色。从一侧看她。显然你会看到一条直线，\_一半红，一半绿\_。

Now imagine a Priest, whose mouth is at M, and whose front semicircle (AMB) is consequently coloured red, while his hinder semicircle is green; so that the diameter AB divides the green from the red. If you contemplate the Great Man so as to have your eye in the same straight line as his dividing diameter (AB), what you will see will be a straight line (CBD), of which \_one half\_ (CB) \_will be red, and the other\_ (BD) \_green\_. The whole line (CD) will be rather shorter perhaps than that of a full-sized Woman, and will shade off more rapidly towards its extremities; but the identity of the colours would give you an immediate impression of identity in Class, making you neglectful of other details. Bear in mind the decay of Sight Recognition which threatened society at the time of the Colour revolt; add too the certainty that Woman would speedily learn to shade off their extremities so as to imitate the Circles; it must then be surely obvious to you, my dear Reader, that the Colour Bill placed us under a great danger of confounding a Priest with a young Woman.

现在想象一个祭司，他的嘴在M点，因此他的前半圆（AMB）被涂成红色，而后半圆被涂成绿色；所以直径AB将绿色和红色分开。如果你注视这位大人物，使你的眼睛与他的分割直径（AB）在同一条直线上，你将看到一条直线（CBD），其中\_一半\_（CB）\_将是红色，另一半\_（BD）\_为绿色\_。整条线（CD）可能比一个完整体型女性的线稍短，并且向两端变暗的速度更快；但颜色的同一性会给你一种阶层同一性的直接印象，使你忽略其他细节。请记住，在色彩暴动时期，视觉识别的衰落威胁着社会；再加上女性会迅速学会让她们的两端变暗以模仿圆形的必然性；那么亲爱的读者，你一定清楚了，颜色法案使我们面临着将祭司与年轻女性混淆的巨大危险。

How attractive this prospect must have been to the Frail Sex may readily be imagined. They anticipated with delight the confusion that would ensue. At home they might hear political and ecclesiastical secrets intended not for them but for their husbands and brothers, and might even issue some commands in the name of a priestly Circle; out of doors the striking combination of red and green without addition of any other colours, would be sure to lead the common people into endless mistakes, and the Woman would gain whatever the Circles lost, in the deference of the passers by. As for the scandal that would befall the Circular Class if the frivolous and unseemly conduct of the Women were imputed to them, and as to the consequent subversion of the Constitution, the Female Sex could not be expected to give a thought to these considerations. Even in the households of the Circles, the Women were all in favour of the Universal Colour Bill.

这一前景对柔弱的女性有多大吸引力，是不难想象的。她们愉快地期待着随之而来的混乱。在家里，她们可能会听到原本不是给她们而是给她们丈夫和兄弟的政治和教会秘密，甚至可能以祭司圆形的名义发布一些命令；在户外，红与绿的醒目组合，由于没有添加任何其他颜色，势必会让平民百姓陷入无穷的错误之中，而女性则会获得圆形们所失去的一切——在过路人的尊敬方面。至于如果女性的轻佻和不得体的行为被归咎于圆形阶层而给他们带来的丑闻，以及随之而来的宪法颠覆，女性是不会想到要去考虑这些的。即使在圆形的家庭中，女性也全都支持《全民颜色法案》。

The second object aimed at by the Bill was the gradual demoralization of the Circles themselves. In the general intellectual decay they still preserved their pristine clearness and strength of understanding. From their earliest childhood, familiarized in their Circular households with the total absence of Colour, the Nobles alone preserved the Sacred Art of Sight Recognition, with all the advantages that result from that admirable training of the intellect. Hence, up to the date of the introduction of the Universal Colour Bill, the Circles had not only held their own, but even increased their lead of the other classes by abstinence from the popular fashion.

该法案的第二个目标，是逐步削弱圆形阶层自身的道德。在整个智识衰落的过程中，他们仍然保持着原有的清晰和理解的力量。从他们最早的童年起，在他们的圆形家庭中就熟悉了完全没有颜色的环境，唯有贵族们保留了神圣的视觉识别术，以及由那种对心智的卓越训练所带来的一切优势。因此，在《全民颜色法案》获得通过之前，圆形阶层不仅保持了自己的地位，甚至还通过拒绝流行时尚而增加了对其他阶层的领先优势。

Now therefore the artful Irregular whom I described above as the real author of this diabolical Bill, determined at one blow to lower the status of the Hierarchy by forcing them to submit to the pollution of Colour, and at the same time to destroy their domestic opportunities of training in the Art of Sight Recognition, so as to enfeeble their intellects by depriving them of their pure and colourless homes. Once subjected to the chromatic taint, every parental and every childish Circle would demoralize each other. Only in discerning between the Father and the Mother would the Circular infant find problems for the exercise of his understanding---problems too often likely to be corrupted by maternal impostures with the result of shaking the child's faith in all logical conclusions. Thus by degrees the intellectual lustre of the Priestly Order would wane, and the road would then lie open for a total destruction of all Aristocratic Legislature and for the subversion of our Privileged Classes.

因此，我上面描述的那个狡猾的不规则圆形——这一邪恶法案的真正作者——决定一举通过强迫神职人员阶层屈服于颜色的污染来降低其地位，同时摧毁他们在家中训练视觉识别术的机会，通过剥夺他们纯净无色的家庭环境来削弱他们的智力。一旦受到色彩的沾染，每一个父代圆形和每一个子代圆形都会互相使对方道德堕落。唯有在辨别父亲和母亲时，圆形婴儿才会发现运用其理解力的问题——而这些问题太容易被母亲的欺骗所腐蚀，结果动摇了孩子对所有逻辑结论的信念。这样一来，祭司阶层的智识光辉将逐渐减弱，随后道路将敞开，为彻底摧毁所有贵族立法机构和颠覆我们的特权阶层铺平道路。

\chapter{镇压色彩暴动}

The agitation for the Universal Colour Bill continued for three years; and up to the last moment of that period it seemed as though Anarchy were destined to triumph.

《全民颜色法案》的骚动持续了三年；在那个时期的最后一刻，似乎无政府状态注定要取得胜利。

A whole army of Polygons, who turned out to fight as private soldiers, was utterly annihilated by a superior force of Isosceles Triangles---the Squares and Pentagons meanwhile remaining neutral.

整个多边形军队，作为士兵参战，被一支占优势的等腰三角形部队彻底歼灭——与此同时正方形和五边形保持中立。

Worse than all, some of the ablest Circles fell a prey to conjugal fury. Infuriated by political animosity, the wives in many a noble household wearied their lords with prayers to give up their opposition to the Colour Bill; and some, finding their entreaties fruitless, fell on and slaughtered their innocent children and husband, perishing themselves in the act of carnage. It is recorded that during that triennial agitation no less than twenty-three Circles perished in domestic discord.

更糟糕的是，一些最能干的圆形成了婚姻愤怒的牺牲品。被政治仇恨所激怒，许多贵族家庭的妻子们以放弃反对颜色法案的祈求来纠缠她们的丈夫；有些妻子在发现她们的恳求无果之后，便袭击并屠杀了她们无辜的孩子和丈夫，自己也在屠杀的过程中丧命。据记载，在那三年的骚动中，不少于二十三个圆形死于家庭纠纷。

Great indeed was the peril. It seemed as though the Priests had no choice between submission and extermination; when suddenly the course of events was completely changed by one of those picturesque incidents which Statesmen ought never to neglect, often to anticipate, and sometimes perhaps to originate, because of the absurdly disproportionate power with which they appeal to the sympathies of the populace.

危险确实很大。祭司们似乎在屈服与灭绝之间别无选择；就在这时，事件的进程突然被一种戏剧性的事件所完全改变——政治家们从不应该忽视这类事件，而应该经常预见，有时甚至可能发起，因为它们以荒谬的不成比例的力量唤起了民众的同情。

It happened that an Isosceles of a low type, with a brain little if at all above four degrees---accidentally dabbling in the colours of some Tradesman whose shop he had plundered---painted himself, or caused himself to be painted (for the story varies) with the twelve colours of a Dodecagon. Going into the Market Place he accosted in a feigned voice a maiden, the orphan daughter of a noble Polygon, whose affection in former days he had sought in vain; and by a series of deceptions---aided, on the one side, by a string of lucky accidents too long to relate, and, on the other, by an almost inconceivable fatuity and neglect of ordinary precautions on the part of the relations of the bride---he succeeded in consummating the marriage. The unhappy girl committed suicide on discovering the fraud to which she had been subjected.

事情发生在一个低等的等腰三角形身上，他的头脑能力如果有的话也略微高于四度——他偶然在一个他劫掠的商人店铺里接触到了颜色——他把自己涂成了十二边形的十二种颜色（或者让人给他涂成了——因为故事说法不一）。他走进市场，用一种假装的声音跟一位少女搭话——她是一个贵族多边形的孤女，他过去曾徒劳地追求过她的爱情；通过一系列的欺骗——一方面得益于一连串说来话长的幸运巧合，另一方面也得益于新娘亲属们几乎难以置信的愚昧和对常规防范的忽视——他成功地完成了这场婚姻。不幸的姑娘在发现自己遭受的欺骗后自杀了。

When the news of this catastrophe spread from State to State the minds of the Women were violently agitated. Sympathy with the miserable victim and anticipations of similar deceptions for themselves, their sisters, and their daughters, made them now regard the Colour Bill in an entirely new aspect. Not a few openly avowed themselves converted to antagonism; the rest needed only a slight stimulus to make a similar avowal. Seizing this favourable opportunity, the Circles hastily convened an extraordinary Assembly of the States; and besides the usual guard of Convicts, they secured the attendance of a large number of reactionary Women.

当这一灾难的消息从一个州传到另一个州时，女性的思想被激烈地搅动了。对那位悲惨受害者的同情，以及对自己、姐妹和女儿可能遭受类似欺骗的预感，使她们现在以一种全新的眼光看待颜色法案。不少人公开宣布自己转而反对该法案；其余的人只需要一点小小的刺激就会做出同样的声明。圆形们抓住这一有利时机，匆忙召开了一次各州的特别大会；除了常规的罪犯警卫之外，他们还确保了大量反动女性的出席。

Amidst an unprecedented concourse, the Chief Circle of those days---by name Pantocyclus---arose to find himself hissed and hooted by a hundred and twenty thousand Isosceles. But he secured silence by declaring that henceforth the Circles would enter on a policy of Concession; yielding to the wishes of the majority, they would accept the Colour Bill. The uproar being at once converted to applause, he invited Chromatistes, the leader of the Sedition, into the centre of the hall, to receive in the name of his followers the submission of the Hierarchy. Then followed a speech, a masterpiece of rhetoric, which occupied nearly a day in the delivery, and to which no summary can do justice.

在前所未有的人群中，当时的主圆——名叫潘托库勒斯——站起身来，发现自己被十二万个等腰三角形所嘘声和喝倒彩。但他通过宣布圆形们从今以后将采取让步政策而获得了沉默；他将顺应大多数人的意愿，接受颜色法案。喧嚣立刻变成了掌声，他邀请暴动的领袖克罗马蒂斯特斯进入大厅中央，以其追随者的名义接受神职人员阶层的归顺。随后是一场演讲——修辞的杰作——其发表几乎用了一天的时间，任何摘要都无法公正地呈现其全貌。

With a grave appearance of impartiality he declared that as they were now finally committing themselves to Reform or Innovation, it was desirable that they should take one last view of the perimeter of the whole subject, its defects as well as its advantages. Gradually introduction the mention of the dangers to the Tradesmen, the Professional Classes and the Gentlemen, he silenced the rising murmurs of the Isosceles by reminding them that, in spite of all these defects, he was willing to accept the Bill if it was approved by the majority. But it was manifest that all, except the Isosceles, were moved by his words and were either neutral or averse to the Bill.

他带着一副庄重的公正外表宣称，既然他们现在最终决定投身于改革或革新，就有必要对整个问题的方方面面——其缺陷和其优点——做最后一次审视。他逐渐引入了对商人、专业阶层和绅士阶层的危险的提及，通过提醒等腰三角形们尽管有这些缺陷，他愿意在该法案得到大多数批准的情况下接受它，从而平息了他们日益高涨的低语声。但显然，除了等腰三角形之外，所有人都被他的话所感动，对该法案要么持中立态度，要么表示反对。

Turning now to the Workmen he asserted that their interests must not be neglected, and that, if they intended to accept the Colour Bill, they ought at least to do so with full view of the consequences. Many of them, he said, were on the point of being admitted to the class of the Regular Triangles; others anticipated for their children a distinction they could not hope for themselves. That honourable ambition would not have to be sacrificed. With the universal adoption of Colour, all distinctions would cease; Regularity would be confused with Irregularity; development would give place to retrogression; the Workman would in a few generations be degraded to the level of the Military, or even the Convict Class; political power would be in the hands of the greatest number, that is to say the Criminal Classes, who were already more numerous than the Workmen, and would soon out-number all the other Classes put together when the usual Compensative Laws of Nature were violated.

他转而对工人说，他们的利益不应被忽视，如果他们打算接受颜色法案，至少应该在充分了解后果的情况下这样做。他说，他们中的许多人即将被接纳为规则三角形阶层；另一些人则为他们的孩子预期着一种他们自己不曾希望获得的荣耀。那种可敬的抱负将不必被牺牲。随着颜色的普遍采用，一切区别都将终止；规则性将与不规则性混淆；发展将让位于倒退；工人在几代人之内将被降级到士兵阶层的水平，甚至罪犯阶层；政治权力将落入最多数人手中，也就是罪犯阶层——他们的人数已经超过了工人，而且当大自然通常的补偿法则被违反时，他们的人数很快就会超过所有其他阶层加在一起。

A subdued murmur of assent ran through the ranks of the Artisans, and Chromatistes, in alarm, attempted to step forward and address them. But he found himself encompassed with guards and forced to remain silent while the Chief Circle in a few impassioned words made a final appeal to the Women, exclaiming that, if the Colour Bill passed, no marriage would henceforth be safe, no woman's honour secure; fraud, deception, hypocrisy would pervade every household; domestic bliss would share the fate of the Constitution and pass to speedy perdition. ``Sooner than this,'' he cried, ``come death.''

一阵压抑的赞同低语声传遍了工匠的队伍，克罗马蒂斯特斯惊恐地试图上前对他们讲话。但他发现自己被警卫包围，被迫保持沉默；与此同时，主圆用几句充满激情的话向女性发出了最后的呼吁，他高呼道，如果颜色法案通过，从今以后没有一桩婚姻是安全的，没有一个女人的名誉是有保障的；欺诈、欺骗、虚伪将充斥每一个家庭；家庭幸福将与宪法同命运，迅速走向毁灭。"与其如此，"他喊道，"不如死。"

At these words, which were the preconcerted signal for action, the Isosceles Convicts fell on and transfixed the wretched Chromatistes; the Regular Classes, opening their ranks, made way for a band of Women who, under direction of the Circles, moved back foremost, invisibly and unerringly upon the unconscious soldiers; the Artisans, imitating the example of their betters, also opened their ranks. Meantime bands of Convicts occupied every entrance with an impenetrable phalanx.

听到这些作为事先约定的行动信号的话，等腰三角形罪犯们扑向并刺穿了不幸的克罗马蒂斯特斯；规则的各阶层打开了他们的队伍，为一队女性让路——她们在圆形的指导下，以看不见的方式、万无一失向毫无察觉的士兵们推进；工匠们效仿他们上司的榜样，也打开了他们的队伍。与此同时，一队队罪犯以坚不可摧的方阵占据了每一个入口。

The battle, or rather carnage, was of short duration. Under the skillful generalship of the Circles almost every Woman's charge was fatal and very many extracted their sting uninjured, ready for a second slaughter. But no second blow was needed; the rabble of the Isosceles did the rest of the business for themselves. Surprised, leader-less, attacked in front by invisible foes, and finding egress cut off by the Convicts behind them, they at once---after their manner---lost all presence of mind, and raised the cry of ``treachery.'' This sealed their fate. Every Isosceles now saw and felt a foe in every other. In half an hour not one of that vast multitude was living; and the fragments of seven score thousand of the Criminal Class slain by one another's angles attested the triumph of Order.

这场战斗，或更确切地说这场屠杀，持续时间很短。在圆形们的熟练指挥下，几乎每一次女性的冲锋都是致命的，而且很多女性毫发无损地撤出了她们的"毒刺"，准备进行第二次屠杀。但不需要第二次打击了；乌合之众的等腰三角形们自己完成了剩下的事情。他们被突袭、失去领导、被看不见的敌人从前方攻击，又发现退路被后面的罪犯切断，于是他们立刻——按照他们的方式——失去了所有的镇定，高喊"背叛"。这注定了他们的命运。每个等腰三角形现在都把其他所有人视为敌人。半小时内，那一大群人中没有一个还活着；七万多罪犯阶层的碎片——被彼此的角度所杀——见证了秩序的胜利。

The Circles delayed not to push their victory to the uttermost. The Working Men they spared but decimated. The Militia of the Equilaterals was at once called out, and every Triangle suspected of Irregularity on reasonable grounds, was destroyed by Court Martial, without the formality of exact measurement by the Social Board. The homes of the Military and Artisan classes were inspected in a course of visitation extending through upwards of a year; and during that period every town, village, and hamlet was systematically purged of that excess of the lower orders which had been brought about by the neglect to pay the tribute of Criminals to the Schools and University, and by the violation of other natural Laws of the Constitution of Flatland. Thus the balance of classes was again restored.

圆形们毫不犹豫地将他们的胜利推向极致。工人阶层他们只饶恕了十分之一。等边三角形的民兵立刻被召集，每一个有合理理由怀疑其不规则的三角形，都被军事法庭处决，免去了社会委员会精确测量的正式程序。士兵和工匠阶层的家庭在一年多的时间里被巡回检查；在那段时期里，每一个城镇、村庄和小村都被系统地清除了过剩的下层阶级——这种过剩是由于忽视向学校和大学缴纳罪犯贡品，以及违反平面国宪法的其他自然法则所造成的。这样，各阶层的平衡又恢复了。

Needless to say that henceforth the use of Colour was abolished, and its possession prohibited. Even the utterance of any word denoting Colour, except by the Circles or by qualified scientific teachers, was punished by a severe penalty. Only at our University in some of the very highest and most esoteric classes---which I myself have never been privileged to attend---it is understood that the sparing use of Colour is still sanctioned for the purpose of illustrating some of the deeper problems of mathematics. But of this I can only speak from hearsay.

不用说，从此以后颜色的使用被废除了，拥有颜色也被禁止了。甚至说出任何表示颜色的词——除非是圆形或合格的科学教师——也会受到严厉的惩罚。只有在我们大学中某些最高级、最深奥的班级里——我本人从未有幸听过这些课——据说仍然允许有限地使用颜色来说明某些更深层次的数学问题。但这一点我只能道听途说。

Elsewhere in Flatland, Colour is now non-existent. The art of making it is known to only one living person, the Chief Circle for the time being; and by him it is handed down on his death-bed to none but his Successor. One manufactory alone produces it; and, lest the secret should be betrayed, the Workmen are annually consumed, and fresh ones introduced. So great is the terror with which even now our Aristocracy looks back to the far-distant days of the agitation for the Universal Colour Bill.

在平面国的其他地方，颜色现在已不存在。制作颜色的艺术只被一个活着的人所知——即当时的主圆；他在临终前将其传授给他的继任者。只有一家工厂生产它；为了防止秘密被泄露，工人每年都会被处死，然后换上新的工人。我们的贵族即使在现在回顾那遥远的为《全民颜色法案》而骚动的日子时，也充满了如此巨大的恐惧。

\chapter{关于我们的祭司}

It is high time that I should pass from these brief and discursive notes about things in Flatland to the central event of this book, my initiation into the mysteries of Space. \_That\_ is my subject; all that has gone before is merely preface.

现在是我从这些关于平面国事物的简短而散漫的笔记，转向本书核心事件——我进入空间奥秘的启蒙——的时候了。\_那\_就是我的主题；此前所述的一切都只是序言。

For this reason I must omit many matters of which the explanation would not, I flatter myself, be without interest for my Readers: as for example, our method of propelling and stopping ourselves, although destitute of feet; the means by which we give fixity to structures of wood, stone, or brick, although of course we have no hands, nor can we lay foundations as you can, nor avail ourselves of the lateral pressure of the earth; the manner in which the rain originates in the intervals between our various zones, so that the northern regions do not intercept the moisture falling on the southern; the nature of our hills and mines, our trees and vegetables, our seasons and harvests; our Alphabet and method of writing, adapted to our linear tablets; these and a hundred other details of our physical existence I must pass over, nor do I mention them now except to indicate to my readers that their omission proceeds not from forgetfulness on the part of the author, but from his regard for the time of the Reader.

因此，我必须省略许多我自信对读者并非无趣的事情的解释：例如，我们尽管没有脚，却用来推进和停止自身的方法；我们用来固定木、石或砖结构的手段——尽管我们当然没有手，也不能像你们那样奠基，或利用地球的侧压力；雨水在我们各个区域之间的间隔中形成的方式，使北方地区不致截断降在南方的水分；我们的山丘和矿藏、我们的树木和蔬菜、我们的季节和收获的性质；我们的字母表和书写方式——适配于我们的线性书写板；这些以及我们物理存在的上百个其他细节，我必须略过。我现在提到它们，只是为了向读者表明，它们的省略不是出于作者的遗忘，而是出于作者对读者时间的尊重。

Yet before I proceed to my legitimate subject some few final remarks will no doubt be expected by my Readers upon these pillars and mainstays of the Constitution of Flatland, the controllers of our conduct and shapers of our destiny, the objects of universal homage and almost of adoration: need I say that I mean our Circles or Priests?

然而，在我进入我真正的主题之前，读者们无疑会期待我对平面国宪法的这些支柱和中流砥柱——我们行为的 controllers、命运的塑造者、普遍崇敬几乎崇拜的对象——说几句最后的话：我还需要说吗，我指的正是我们的圆形或祭司？

When I call them Priests, let me not be understood as meaning no more than the term denotes with you. With us, our Priests are Administrators of all Business, Art, and Science; Directors of Trade, Commerce, Generalship, Architecture, Engineering, Education, Statesmanship, Legislature, Morality, Theology; doing nothing themselves, they are the Causes of everything worth doing, that is done by others.

当我称他们为祭司时，请不要将其理解为与你们那里这个术语的含义别无二致。在我们这里，祭司是所有事务、艺术和科学的管理者；是贸易、商业、军事、建筑、工程、教育、治国、立法、道德、神学的指导者；他们自己什么也不做，却是所有值得做的事情的推动者——这些事情都是由其他人完成的。

Although popularly everyone called a Circle is deemed a Circle, yet among the better educated Classes it is known that no Circle is really a Circle, but only a Polygon with a very large number of very small sides. As the number of the sides increases, a Polygon approximates to a Circle; and, when the number is very great indeed, say for example three or four hundred, it is extremely difficult for the most delicate touch to feel any polygonal angles. Let me say rather it \_would\_ be difficult: for, as I have shown above, Recognition by Feeling is unknown among the highest society, and to \_feel\_ a Circle would be considered a most audacious insult. This habit of abstention from Feeling in the best society enables a Circle the more easily to sustain the veil of mystery in which, from his earliest years, he is wont to enwrap the exact nature of his Perimeter or Circumference. Three feet being the average Perimeter it follows that, in a Polygon of three hundred sides each side will be no more than the hundredth part of a foot in length, or little more than the tenth part of an inch; and in a Polygon of six or seven hundred sides the sides are little larger than the diameter of a Spaceland pin-head. It is always assumed, by courtesy, that the Chief Circle for the time being has ten thousand sides.

虽然在大众眼中，被称为圆形的人都被认为是真正的圆形，但在受过良好教育的阶层中，人们知道没有哪个圆形真正是圆形的，它只是一个有着非常多非常小的边的多边形。随着边数的增加，多边形趋近于圆形；当边数确实非常大时，比如说三四百条边，即使是最精细的触摸也极难感觉到任何多边形的角度。让我更确切地说，它\_将是\_困难的：因为，如我上面所示，在最高社会中，通过触摸来识别是闻所未闻的，而\_触摸\_一个圆形会被视为一种极其大胆的侮辱。在最高社会中戒除触摸的习惯，使一个圆形能够更容易地维持那层神秘的面纱——从他最早的岁月起，他就习惯于用这层面纱来包裹他的周长或圆周的精确性质。三英尺是平均周长，因此在一个三百条边的多边形中，每条边的长度不超过一英尺的百分之一，或略多于十分之一英寸；在一个六七百条边的多边形中，边的大小略大于空间国中一个针头的直径。按照惯例，人们总是假定当时的主圆拥有一万条边。

The ascent of the posterity of the Circles in the social scale is not restricted, as it is among the lower Regular classes, by the Law of Nature which limits the increase of sides to one in each generation. If it were so, the number of sides in the Circle would be a mere question of pedigree and arithmetic, and the four hundred and ninety-seventh descendant of an Equilateral Triangle would necessarily be a polygon with five hundred sides. But this is not the case. Nature's Law prescribes two antagonistic decrees affecting Circular propagation; first, that as the race climbs higher in the scale of development, so development shall proceed at an accelerated pace; second, that in the same proportion, the race shall become less fertile. Consequently in the home of a Polygon of four or five hundred sides it is rare to find a son; more than one is never seen. On the other hand the son of a five-hundred-sided Polygon has been known to possess five hundred and fifty, or even six hundred sides.

圆形的后代在社会等级中的上升，不像较低的规则阶层那样受到自然法则的限制——那个法则将每代边数的增加限制为一。如果是这样的话，圆形的边数就只是一个谱系和算术的问题，等边三角形的第四百九十七代后代必然会是一个有五百条边的多边形。但事实并非如此。自然法则规定了影响圆形繁殖的两个对抗性的法令；第一，随着种族在发展阶梯上攀得更高，发展应以加速的步伐进行；第二，以同样的比例，种族的繁殖力会下降。因此，在一个四五百条边的多边形的家中，很少能找到一个儿子；多于一个的情况从未见过。另一方面，一个五百条边的多边形的儿子，已知能拥有五百五十条边，甚至六百条边。

Art also steps in to help the process of higher Evolution. Our physicians have discovered that the small and tender sides of an infant Polygon of the higher class can be fractured, and his whole frame re-set, with such exactness that a Polygon of two or three hundred sides sometimes---by no means always, for the process is attended with serious risk---but sometimes overleaps two or three hundred generations, and as it were double at a stroke, the number of his progenitors and the nobility of his descent.

艺术也介入帮助更高进化的过程。我们的医生发现，高阶阶层的一个婴儿多边形的小而嫩的边可以被骨折，然后他的整个骨架可以被重新矫正，其精确度如此之高，以至于一个两三百条边的多边形有时——绝不是总是，因为这个过程伴随着严重的风险——但有时能跳过两三百代，仿佛一举将其祖先的数量和其血统的高贵性加倍。

Many a promising child is sacrificed in this way. Scarcely one out of ten survives. Yet so strong is the parental ambition among those Polygons who are, as it were, on the fringe of the Circular class, that it is very rare to find the Nobleman of that position in society, who has neglected to place his first-born in the Circular Neo-Therapeutic Gymnasium before he has attained the age of a month.

许多有前途的孩子就这样被牺牲了。几乎十个中只有一个能活下来。然而，在那些可以说处于圆形阶层边缘的多边形中，父母的抱负是如此强烈，以至于很少能找到一个处在那个社会位置的贵族，会在他的长子满一个月之前，不把他送到圆形的新治疗体操馆去。

One year determines success or failure. At the end of that time the child has, in all probability, added one more to the tombstones that crowd the Neo-Therapeutic Cemetery; but on rare occasional a glad procession bears back the little one to his exultant parents, no longer a Polygon, but a Circle, at least by courtesy: and a single instance of so blessed a result induces multitudes of Polygonal parents to submit to similar domestic sacrifice, which have a dissimilar issue.

一年决定成功或失败。在那个时候结束时，孩子很可能已经为拥挤的新治疗墓地的墓碑增添了一块新的石碑；但在极少数情况下，一个欢乐的队伍会把小家伙带回给他欣喜若狂的父母——他不再是一个多边形，而是一个圆形，至少在礼貌上是如此：如此幸福的结果的一个实例，会促使众多多边形的父母忍受类似的家庭牺牲，而这些牺牲却有着不同的结局。

\chapter{祭司们的教义}

As to the doctrine of the Circles it may briefly be summed up in a single maxim, ``Attend to your Configuration.'' Whether political, ecclesiastical, or moral, all their teaching has for its object the improvement of individual and collective Configuration---with special reference of course to the Configuration of the Circles, to which all other objects are subordinated.

至于圆形们的教义，简要地可以用一句格言来概括："注意你的构形。"无论是政治的、教会的还是道德的，他们所有的教导都以改善个人和集体的构形为目标——当然特别涉及圆形的构形，所有其他对象都从属于它。

It is the merit of the Circles that they have effectually suppressed those ancient heresies which led men to waste energy and sympathy in the vain belief that conduct depends upon will, effort, training, encouragement, praise, or anything else but Configuration. It was Pantocyclus---the illustrious Circle mentioned above, as the queller of the Colour Revolt---who first convinced mankind that Configuration makes the man; that if, for example, you are born an Isosceles with two uneven sides, you will assuredly go wrong unless you have them made even---for which purpose you must go to the Isosceles Hospital; similarly, if you are a Triangle, or Square, or even a Polygon, born with any Irregularity, you must be taken to one of the Regular Hospitals to have your disease cured; otherwise you will end your days in the State Prison or by the angle of the State Executioner.

圆形们的功绩在于，他们有效地镇压了那些古老的异端邪说——那些邪说引导人们将精力和同情心浪费在一种徒劳的信念上，即认为行为取决于意志、努力、训练、鼓励、赞美或构形之外的任何东西。正是潘托库勒斯——上面提到的那位杰出的圆形，色彩暴动的镇压者——首先使人类相信构形造就人；例如，如果你出生时是一个两条边不相等的等腰三角形，你肯定会走入歧途，除非你把它们弄相等——为此你必须去等腰三角形医院；同样，如果你是一个三角形、正方形、甚至一个多边形，出生时有任何不规则性，你必须被送到规则医院之一去治疗你的疾病；否则你将在国家监狱中或被国家刽子手的角度终结你的一生。

All faults or defects, from the slightest misconduct to the most flagitious crime, Pantocyclus attributed to some deviation from perfect Regularity in the bodily figure, caused perhaps (if not congenital) by some collision in a crowd; by neglect to take exercise, or by taking too much of it; or even by a sudden change of temperature, resulting in a shrinkage or expansion in some too susceptible part of the frame. Therefore, concluded that illustrious Philosopher, neither good conduct nor bad conduct is a fit subject, in any sober estimation, for either praise or blame. For why should you praise, for example, the integrity of a Square who faithfully defends the interests of his client, when you ought in reality rather to admire the exact precision of his right angles? Or again, why blame a lying, thievish Isosceles, when you ought rather to deplore the incurable inequality of his sides?

一切过错或缺陷——从最轻微的不当行为到最严重的罪行——潘托库勒斯都归因于身体图形在完全规则性上的某种偏差，这种偏差也许（如果不是先天的）由人群中的某种碰撞引起；由忽视锻炼，或过度锻炼引起；或甚至由温度的突然变化，导致身体结构中某个过于敏感的部分收缩或膨胀。因此，那位杰出的哲学家总结道，无论是良好行为还是不良行为，在任何清醒的估计中，都不适合作为赞美或责备的对象。因为你为什么要赞美，例如，一个忠实地捍卫客户利益的正方形的正直，而实际上你应该更钦佩他的直角的精确性？或者再问，为什么要责备一个撒谎、偷盗的等腰三角形，而你应该更痛惜的是他的边的无法矫正的不平等？

Theoretically, this doctrine is unquestionable; but it has practical drawbacks. In dealing with an Isosceles, if a rascal pleads that he cannot help stealing because of his unevenness, you reply that for that very reason, because he cannot help being a nuisance to his neighbours, you, the Magistrate, cannot help sentencing him to be consumed---and there's an end of the matter. But in little domestic difficulties, when the penalty of consumption, or death, is out of the question, this theory of Configuration sometimes comes in awkwardly; and I must confess that occasionally when one of my own Hexagonal Grandsons pleads as an excuse for his disobedience that a sudden change of temperature has been too much for his Perimeter, and that I ought to lay the blame not on him but on his Configuration, which can only be strengthened by abundance of the choicest sweetmeats, I neither see my way logically to reject, nor practically to accept, his conclusions.

从理论上讲，这一教义是无可置疑的；但它在实践中有缺陷。在处理一个等腰三角形的案件时，如果一个无赖辩称他忍不住偷窃是因为他的不平等性，你回答说，正是因为这个原因，因为他忍不住要对邻居造成滋扰，你——作为法官——也忍不住要判他被处决——事情就这样结束了。但在小小的家庭困难中，当处决或死刑不适用时，这种构形理论有时就会令人尴尬地出现；我必须承认，偶尔当我的一个六边形孙子为他的不服从辩解时，说温度的突然变化对他的周长影响太大了，我应该把责任归咎于他的构形而不是他，而这种构形只能通过大量最精美的糖果来加强——我在逻辑上既无法拒绝，在实践中也无法接受他的结论。

For my own part, I find it best to assume that a good sound scolding or castigation has some latent and strengthening influence on my Grandson's Configuration; though I own that I have no grounds for thinking so. At all events I am not alone in my way of extricating myself from this dilemma; for I find that many of the highest Circles, sitting as Judges in law courts, use praise and blame towards Regular and Irregular Figures; and in their homes I know by experience that, when scolding their children, they speak about ``right'' and ``wrong'' as vehemently and passionately as if they believe that these names represented real existence, and that a human Figure is really capable of choosing between them.

就我自己而言，我发现最好假定一次真正的训斥或鞭打对我孙子的构形有某种潜在的强化影响；尽管我承认我没有理由这样想。无论如何，我并不是唯一一个从这种困境中脱身的人；因为我发现许多最高级的圆形，在法庭上担任法官时，确实会对规则和不规则的图形使用赞美和责备；而在他们的家中，我凭经验知道，当他们责骂自己的孩子时，他们谈论"对"和"错"时的激烈和热情，仿佛他们相信这些名字代表着真实的存在，而一个人的图形确实能够在它们之间作出选择。

Constantly carrying out their policy of making Configuration the leading idea in every mind, the Circles reverse the nature of that Commandment which in Spaceland regulates the relations between parents and children. With you, children are taught to honour their parents; with us---next to the Circles, who are the chief object of universal homage---a man is taught to honour his Grandson, if he has one; or, if not, his Son. By ``honour,'' however, is by no means mean ``indulgence,'' but a reverent regard for their highest interests: and the Circles teach that the duty of fathers is to subordinate their own interests to those of posterity, thereby advancing the welfare of the whole State as well as that of their own immediate descendants.

圆形们贯彻执行他们使构形成为每个人心中主导思想的政策，颠倒了空间国中规范父母与子女关系的那条诫命的性质。在你们那里，孩子被教导要尊敬父母；在我们这里——仅次于作为普遍崇敬主要对象的圆形——一个人被教导要尊敬他的孙子（如果他有的话）；或者，如果没有，就尊敬他的儿子。然而，这里的"尊敬"绝不是指"纵容"，而是指对他们最高利益的虔敬关怀；圆形们教导说，父亲的责任是将自己的利益服从于后代的利益，从而推进整个国家的福利，以及他们自己直接后代的福利。

The weak point in the system of the Circles---if a humble Square may venture to speak of anything Circular as containing any element of weakness---appears to me to be found in their relations with Women.

圆形体系的弱点——如果一个卑微的正方形敢于谈论任何圆形的东西包含弱点的话——在我看来，存在于他们与女性的关系中。

As it is of the utmost importance for Society that Irregular births should be discouraged, it follows that no Woman who has any Irregularities in her ancestry is a fit partner for one who desires that his posterity should rise by regular degrees in the social scale.

由于对社会来说，阻止不规则的出生至关重要，因此，任何在其祖先中有任何不规则性的女性，都不适合成为一个希望其后代在社会等级中按规则上升的人的配偶。

Now the Irregularity of a Male is a matter of measurement; but as all Women are straight, and therefore visibly Regular so to speak, one has to devise some other means of ascertaining what I may call their invisible Irregularity, that is to say their potential Irregularities as regards possible offspring. This is effected by carefully-kept pedigrees, which are preserved and supervised by the State; and without a certified pedigree no Woman is allowed to marry.

男性的不规则性是一个测量问题；但由于所有女性都是直线的，因此可以说在视觉上是规则的，人们必须设计一些其他方法来确定我所说的她们的不可见的不规则性——也就是说，她们关于可能后代的潜在不规则性。这是通过精心保存的谱系来实现的，这些谱系由国家保存和监督；没有经过认证的谱系，任何女性都不允许结婚。

Now it might have been supposed that a Circle---proud of his ancestry and regardful for a posterity which might possibly issue hereafter in a Chief Circle---would be more careful than any other to choose a wife who had no blot on her escutcheon. But it is not so. The care in choosing a Regular wife appears to diminish as one rises in the social scale. Nothing would induce an aspiring Isosceles, who has hopes of generating an Equilateral Son, to take a wife who reckoned a single Irregularity among her Ancestors; a Square or Pentagon, who is confident that his family is steadily on the rise, does not inquire above the five-hundredth generation; a Hexagon or Dodecagon is even more careless of the wife's pedigree; but a Circle has been known deliberately to take a wife who has had an Irregular Great-Grandfather, and all because of some slight superiority of lustre, or because of the charms of a low voice---which, with us, even more than with you, is thought ``an excellent thing in a Woman.''

人们本以为，一个圆形——为自己的祖先感到自豪并关心可能会在后代中产生主圆的可能性——会比任何人都更谨慎地选择一个在其纹章上没有污点的妻子。但事实并非如此。选择一个规则妻子的谨慎程度，似乎随着社会等级的上升而降低。没有什么能诱使一个有抱负的等腰三角形——他希望生出一个等边的儿子——去娶一个在其祖先中有一个不规则性的妻子；一个正方形或五边形——他自信他的家族正在稳步上升——不会查询超过五百代的谱系；一个六边形或十二边形对妻子的谱系更加粗心；但一个圆形已知会故意娶一个有不规则曾祖父的妻子，这完全是因为光泽上的某种轻微优越性，或因为一个低沉声音的魅力——在我们这里，甚至比在你们那里更甚，低沉声音被认为是"女性的一大优点"。

Such ill-judged marriages are, as might be expected, barren, if they do not result in positive Irregularity or in diminution of sides; but none of these evils have hitherto provided sufficiently deterrent. The loss of a few sides in a highly-developed Polygon is not easily noticed, and is sometimes compensated by a successful operation in the Neo-Therapeutic Gymnasium, as I have described above; and the Circles are too much disposed to acquiesce in infecundity as a law of the superior development. Yet, if this evil be not arrested, the gradual diminution of the Circular class may soon become more rapid, and the time may not be far distant when, the race being no longer able to produce a Chief Circle, the Constitution of Flatland must fall.

这样的不明智婚姻，正如所料，如果不会导致实际的不规则性或边数减少，往往是不育的；但这些恶目前还没有提供足够的遏制力。在一个高度发达的多边形中失去几条边不容易被注意到，而且有时可以通过在新治疗体操馆的成功手术来补偿，如我上面所述；而圆形们太倾向于默认不育是高级发展的一个法则。然而，如果这一邪恶不被阻止，圆形阶层的逐渐减少可能很快加速，时间可能不远——当这个种族不再能产生主圆时，平面国的宪法就必须垮台。

One other word of warning suggest itself to me, though I cannot so easily mention a remedy; and this also refers to our relations with Women. About three hundred years ago, it was decreed by the Chief Circle that, since women are deficient in Reason but abundant in Emotion, they ought no longer to be treated as rational, nor receive any mental education. The consequence was that they were no longer taught to read, nor even to master Arithmetic enough to enable them to count the angles of their husband or children; and hence they sensibly declined during each generation in intellectual power. And this system of female non-education or quietism still prevails.

还有一个警告之词出现在我脑海中，尽管我不能轻易地提到补救办法；这也涉及我们与女性的关系。大约三百年前，主圆颁布法令说，既然女性缺乏理性但富有情感，她们不应再被视为理性的存在，也不应接受任何心智教育。结果是，她们不再被教阅读，甚至不被教掌握足够的算术去数她们丈夫或孩子的角；因此她们在每一代中智力都明显下降。而这种女性不受教育或寂静主义的体系仍然盛行。

My fear is that, with the best intentions, this policy has been carried so far as to react injuriously on the Male Sex.

我担心，这一政策即使出于最好的意图，也被推行得如此之远，以至于对男性产生了有害的反作用。

For the consequence is that, as things now are, we Males have to lead a kind of bi-lingual, and I may almost say bimental, existence. With Women, we speak of ``love,'' ``duty,'' ``right,'' ``wrong,'' ``pity,'' ``hope,'' and other irrational and emotional conceptions, which have no existence, and the fiction of which has no object except to control feminine exuberances; but among ourselves, and in our books, we have an entirely different vocabulary and I may also say, idiom. ``Love'' them becomes ``the anticipation of benefits''; ``duty'' becomes ``necessity'' or ``fitness''; and other words are correspondingly transmuted. Moreover, among Women, we use language implying the utmost deference for their Sex; and they fully believe that the Chief Circle Himself is not more devoutly adored by us than they are: but behind their backs they are both regarded and spoken of---by all but the very young—as being little better than ``mindless organisms.''

因为结果是，在目前的情况下，我们男性必须过一种双语的、甚至可以说双心智的生活。与女性在一起时，我们谈论"爱"、"责任"、"对"、"错"、"怜悯"、"希望"和其他非理性的情感概念，这些概念没有存在，其虚构也没有对象，除了控制女性的情感泛滥；但在我们自己中间，在我们的书中，我们有一种完全不同的词汇，甚至可以说不同的习语。"爱"变成了"对利益的预期"；"责任"变成了"必然性"或"适应性"；其他词也相应地被改变了。此外，在女性面前，我们使用的语言暗示着对她们性别的最大尊重；她们完全相信主圆本人也不如我们崇拜她们那样被我们虔诚地崇拜着；但在她们背后，她们都被视为——除了非常年轻的人——仅仅比"无心智的有机体"好一点。

Our Theology also in the Women's chambers is entirely different from our Theology elsewhere.

我们在女性房间里的神学，也完全不同于我们在其他地方的神学。

Now my humble fear is that this double training, in language as well as in thought, imposes somewhat too heavy a burden upon the young, especially when, at the age of three years old, they are taken from the maternal care and taught to unlearn the old language---except for the purpose of repeating it in the presence of the Mothers and Nurses---and to learn the vocabulary and idiom of science. Already methinks I discern a weakness in the grasp of mathematical truth at the present time as compared with the more robust intellect of our ancestors three hundred years ago. I say nothing of the possible danger if a Woman should ever surreptitiously learn to read and convey to her Sex the result of her perusal of a single popular volume; nor of the possibility that the indiscretion or disobedience of some infant Male might reveal to a Mother the secrets of the logical dialect. On the simple ground of the enfeebling of the male intellect, I rest this humble appeal to the highest Authorities to reconsider the regulations of Female education.

现在，我卑微地担心，这种在语言和思想上的双重训练，给年轻人施加了过重的负担，特别是当他们在三岁时被从母亲的照顾中带走，被教导去忘掉旧的语言——除了在母亲和保姆面前重复它的目的——并去学习科学的词汇和习语。我想我已经察觉到，与三百年前我们祖先更健壮的智力相比，目前对数学真理的掌握存在弱点。我不说如果一个女性偷偷学会了阅读并把她阅读一本通俗书籍的结果传达给她的性别可能带来的危险；也不说如果一个男婴的轻率或不服从可能向母亲泄露逻辑方言的秘密的可能性。基于男性智力衰弱这一简单的理由，我向最高当局提出这一卑微的呼吁，请求重新考虑女性教育的规定。

\part{其他世界}

\chapter{我如何看见了直线国}

It was the last day but one of the 1999th year of our era, and the first day of the Long Vacation. Having amused myself till a late hour with my favourite recreation of Geometry, I had retired to rest with an unsolved problem in my mind. In the night I had a dream.

那是我们纪元第1999年的倒数第二天，也是长假的第一天。我沉浸在最爱的几何娱乐中直到很晚，然后带着一个未解的问题去休息。夜里我做了一个梦。

I saw before me a vast multitude of small Straight Lines (which I naturally assumed to be Women) interspersed with other Beings still smaller and of the nature of lustrous points---all moving to and fro in one and the same Straight Line, and, as nearly as I could judge, with the same velocity.

我看见面前有无数的小直线（我自然地认为它们是女性），其间点缀着其他更小的、具有发光点性质的存在——全都在同一条直线上来回运动，而且据我判断，是以相同的速度运动。

A noise of confused, multitudinous chirping or twittering issued from them at intervals as long as they were moving; but sometimes they ceased from motion, and then all was silence.

只要它们在运动，就会不时发出一种混乱的、繁复的唧唧声或啁啾声；但有时它们停止运动，然后一切归于寂静。

Approaching one of the largest of what I thought to be Women, I accosted her, but received no answer. A second and third appeal on my part were equally ineffectual. Losing patience at what appeared to me intolerable rudeness, I brought my mouth to a position full in front of her mouth so as to intercept her motion, and loudly repeated my question, ``Woman, what signifies this concourse, and this strange and confused chirping, and this monotonous motion to and fro in one and the same Straight Line?''

我走向其中最大的一个——我以为是女性——向她搭话，但没有得到回答。第二次和第三次呼唤也同样无效。对我来说，这种不可容忍的无礼使我失去了耐心，我把嘴移到她嘴的正前方以拦截她的运动，然后大声重复我的问题："女士，这一群聚意味着什么？这奇怪而混乱的啁啾声，以及这在同一条直线上单调的来回运动，又是怎么回事？"

``I am no Woman,'' replied the small Line: ``I am the Monarch of the world. But thou, whence intrudest thou into my realm of Lineland?'' Receiving this abrupt reply, I begged pardon if I had in any way startled or molested his Royal Highness; and describing myself as a stranger I besought the King to give me some account of his dominions. But I had the greatest possible difficulty in obtaining any information on points that really interested me; for the Monarch could not refrain from constantly assuming that whatever was familiar to him must also be known to me and that I was simulating ignorance in jest. However, by preserving questions I elicited the following facts:

"我不是女性，"那条小直线回答说："我是这个世界的君主。但你是谁，怎么闯入了我的直线国疆域？"听到这突如其来的回答，我为惊扰了殿下而请求原谅；并说明自己是一个陌生人，恳求国王向我介绍一下他的领地。但我在获取真正感兴趣的信息时遇到了最大的困难；因为君主禁不住不断地假设，凡是他熟悉的东西我也一定知道，而我是在假装无知开玩笑。然而，通过持续的提问，我引出了以下事实：

It seemed that this poor ignorant Monarch---as he called himself---was persuaded that the Straight Line which he called his Kingdom, and in which he passed his existence, constituted the whole of the world, and indeed the whole of Space. Not being able either to move or to see, save in his Straight Line, he had no conception of anything out of it. Though he had heard my voice when I first addressed him, the sounds had come to him in a manner so contrary to his experience that he had made no answer, ``seeing no man,'' as he expressed it, ``and hearing a voice as it were from my own intestines.'' Until the moment when I placed my mouth in his World, he had neither seen me, nor heard anything except confused sounds beating against, what I called his side, but what he called his \_inside\_ or \_stomach;\_ nor had he even now the least conception of the region from which I had come. Outside his World, or Line, all was a blank to him; nay, not even a blank, for a blank implies Space; say, rather, all was non-existent.

这位可怜的无知君主——正如他自称的那样——相信他称之为王国的那条直线，也就是他在其中度过一生的那条直线，构成了整个世界，乃至整个空间。除了在他的直线上，他既不能移动也不能看见，因此他对直线之外的任何事物都没有概念。尽管我第一次跟他说话时他听到了我的声音，但这些声音以一种与他的经验如此相悖的方式传到他那里，以至于他没有回答——"看不到任何人，"正如他所说，"听到一个声音，仿佛来自我自己的内脏。"直到我把嘴放进他的世界那一刻，他才既没有看见过我，也除了敲打在我称之为他的侧面、而他称之为他的\_内部\_或\_胃\_的东西上的混乱声音之外什么也没听到；甚至现在他对我来自的区域也没有丝毫概念。在他的世界或直线之外，一切对他来说都是空白；不，甚至不是空白，因为空白意味着空间；更确切地说，一切都是不存在的。

His subjects---of whom the small Lines were men and the Points Women---were all alike confined in motion and eyesight to that single Straight Line, which was their World. It need scarcely be added that the whole of their horizon was limited to a Point; nor could any one ever see anything but a Point. Man, woman, child, thing---each as a Point to the eye of a Linelander. Only by the sound of the voice could sex or age be distinguished. Moreover, as each individual occupied the whole of the narrow path, so to speak, which constituted his Universe, and no one could move to the right or left to make way for passers by, it followed that no Linelander could ever pass another. Once neighbours, always neighbours. Neighbourhood with them was like marriage with us. Neighbours remained neighbours till death did them part.

他的臣民——其中小直线是男性，点是女性——都同样被限制在那条唯一的直线上运动和观看，那条直线就是他们的世界。不必多说，他们的整个视野仅限于一个点；任何人都只能看到一个点。男人、女人、孩子、事物——在直线国人的眼中，每一个都是一个点。只有通过声音才能区分性别或年龄。此外，由于每个人占据了构成他的宇宙的整条狭窄路径，可以这么说，而且没有人能向右或向左移动来为路人让路，因此没有一个直线国人能超过另一个。一旦成为邻居，永远都是邻居。他们的邻里关系就像我们的婚姻一样。邻居始终是邻居，直到死亡将他们分开。

Such a life, with all vision limited to a Point, and all motion to a Straight Line, seemed to me inexpressibly dreary; and I was surprised to note that vivacity and cheerfulness of the King. Wondering whether it was possible, amid circumstances so unfavourable to domestic relations, to enjoy the pleasures of conjugal union, I hesitated for some time to question his Royal Highness on so delicate a subject; but at last I plunged into it by abruptly inquiring as to the health of his family. ``My wives and children,'' he replied, ``are well and happy.''

这样一种生活——一切视觉限于一个点，一切运动限于一条直线——在我看来难以言喻地沉闷；但我惊讶地注意到国王的活泼和愉快。我想知道，在如此不利于家庭关系的环境中，是否有可能享受婚姻结合的乐趣，于是我犹豫了一段时间才就如此微妙的问题向殿下提问；但最后我还是突然询问了他家人的健康，从而跳入了这个话题。"我的妻子和孩子们，"他回答说，"都很好很幸福。"

Staggered at this answer---for in the immediate proximity of the Monarch (as I had noted in my dream before I entered Lineland) there were none but Men---I ventured to reply, ``Pardon me, but I cannot imagine how your Royal Highness can at any time either see or approach their Majesties, when there at least half a dozen intervening individuals, whom you can neither see through, nor pass by? Is it possible that in Lineland proximity is not necessary for marriage and for the generation of children?''

这个回答让我震惊——因为在君主的紧邻（正如我在进入直线国之前的梦中所注意到的）只有男性——我冒昧地回答说："请原谅我，但我无法想象殿下如何能在任何时候看到或接近王后们，当中间至少有六个人挡着，您既不能看穿他们，也不能绕过他们？难道在直线国中，接近对于婚姻和生育孩子不是必需的吗？"

``How can you ask so absurd a question?'' replied the Monarch. ``If it were indeed as you suggest, the Universe would soon be depopulated. No, no; neighbourhood is needless for the union of hearts; and the birth of children is too important a matter to have been allowed to depend upon such an accident as proximity. You cannot be ignorant of this. Yet since you are pleased to affect ignorance, I will instruct you as if you were the veriest baby in Lineland. Know, then, that marriages are consummated by means of the faculty of sound and the sense of hearing.

"你怎么能问如此荒谬的问题？"君主回答说。"如果真如你所说，宇宙很快就会人口灭绝。不，不；邻里关系对于心灵的结合是不必要的；而孩子的出生是一件太重要的事，不能允许它依赖于接近这样的偶然因素。你不可能不知道这一点。但既然你乐意假装无知，我就把你当作直线国中最年幼的婴儿来教导你。那么你要知道，婚姻是通过声音的能力和听觉来完成的。

``You are of course aware that every Man has two mouths or voices---as well as two eyes---a bass at one and a tenor at the other of his extremities. I should not mention this, but that I have been unable to distinguish your tenor in the course of our conversation.'' I replied that I had but one voice, and that I had not been aware that his Royal Highness had two. ``That confirms my impression,'' said the King, ``that you are not a Man, but a feminine Monstrosity with a bass voice, and an utterly uneducated ear. But to continue.

"你当然知道，每个男人都有两张嘴或两个声音——以及两只眼睛——一个低音在一端，一个男高音在另一端。我本不该提这个，但在我们的谈话中我没能分辨出你的男高音。"我回答说我只有一个声音，我不知道殿下有两个。"这证实了我的印象，"国王说，"你不是一个男人，而是一个有低音的女性怪物，一个完全没有受过训练的耳朵。但让我继续。

``Nature having herself ordained that every Man should wed two wives---'' ``Why two?'' asked I. ``You carry your affected simplicity too far,'' he cried. ``How can there be a completely harmonious union without the combination of the Four in One, viz. the Bass and Tenor of the Man and the Soprano and Contralto of the two Women?'' ``But supposing,'' said I, ``that a man should prefer one wife or three?'' ``It is impossible,'' he said; ``it is as inconceivable as that two and one should make five, or that the human eye should see a Straight Line.'' I would have interrupted him; but he proceeded as follows:

"自然亲自规定每个男人应该娶两个妻子——""为什么是两个？"我问。"你把你假装的愚蠢做得太过分了，"他喊道。"没有四合一的结合，即男人的低音和男高音，以及两个女人的女高音和女低音，怎么可能有完全和谐的结合呢？""但假如，"我说，"一个男人更愿意要一个妻子或三个呢？""这是不可能的，"他说；"这就像二加一等于五，或人眼能看到一条直线一样不可想象。"我本想打断他；但他继续说道：

``Once in the middle of each week a Law of Nature compels us to move to and fro with a rhythmic motion of more than usual violence, which continues for the time you would take to count a hundred and one. In the midst of this choral dance, at the fifty-first pulsation, the inhabitants of the Universe pause in full career, and each individual sends forth his richest, fullest, sweetest strain. It is in this decisive moment that all our marriages are made. So exquisite is the adaptation of Bass and Treble, of Tenor to Contralto, that oftentimes the Loved Ones, though twenty thousand leagues away, recognize at once the responsive note of their destined Lover; and, penetrating the paltry obstacles of distance, Love unites the three. The marriage in that instance consummated results in a threefold Male and Female offspring which takes its place in Lineland.''

"每周中总有一次，自然法则迫使我们以非同寻常的剧烈节奏做来回运动，这种运动持续的时间大约是你数完一百零一的时间。在这场合唱舞的中间，在第五十一次脉动时，宇宙的居民们在全速运动中暂停，每个人发出他最丰富、最饱满、最甜美的旋律。正是在这个决定性的时刻，我们所有的婚姻都被缔结。低音与高音、男高音与女低音的配合是如此精妙，以至于常常那些所爱之人，纵然在万里之遥，也能立刻认出他们命中注定的恋人的回应音符；并且，跨越微不足道的距离障碍，爱情将三者结合。在那种情况下完成的婚姻，会产生一个三重的男女后代，在直线国中占据它的位置。"

``What! Always threefold?'' said I. ``Must one wife then always have twins?''

"什么！总是三重？"我说。"那一个妻子总是要生双胞胎吗？"

``Bass-voice Monstrosity! yes,'' replied the King. ``How else could the balance of the Sexes be maintained, if two girls were not born for every boy? Would you ignore the very Alphabet of Nature?'' He ceased, speechless for fury; and some time elapsed before I could induce him to resume his narrative.

"低音怪物！是的，"国王回答说。"如果不是每个男孩对应两个女孩出生，性别平衡怎么能维持呢？你要无视自然的最基本元素吗？"他停下来，愤怒得说不出话来；过了一段时间我才让他继续讲述。

``You will not, of course, suppose that every bachelor among us finds his mates at the first wooing in this universal Marriage Chorus. On the contrary, the process is by most of us many times repeated. Few are the hearts whose happy lot is at once to recognize in each other's voice the partner intended for them by Providence, and to fly into a reciprocal and perfectly harmonious embrace. With most of us the courtship is of long duration. The Wooer's voices may perhaps accord with one of the future wives, but not with both; or not, at first, with either; or the Soprano and Contralto may not quite harmonize. In such cases Nature has provided that every weekly Chorus shall bring the three Lovers into closer harmony. Each trial of voice, each fresh discovery of discord, almost imperceptibly induces the less perfect to modify his or her vocal utterance so as to approximate to the more perfect. And after many trials and many approximations, the result is at last achieved. There comes a day at last when, while the wonted Marriage Chorus goes forth from universal Lineland, the three far-off Lovers suddenly find themselves in exact harmony, and, before they are aware, the wedded Triplet is rapt vocally into a duplicate embrace; and Nature rejoices over one more marriage and over three more births.''

"你当然不会以为，我们中间的每个单身汉都能在这个普遍的婚姻合唱中第一次求爱就找到伴侣。相反，这个过程对我们大多数人来说要重复多次。很少有人能有幸立刻在彼此的声音中辨认出天意注定的伴侣，并飞入相互的、完全和谐的拥抱。对我们大多数人来说，求爱是长期的。求爱者的声音也许与一个未来的妻子相合，但与两个都不相合；或者起初与任何一个都不相合；或者女高音和女低音可能不完全协调。在这种情况下，自然规定每周的合唱会使三个恋人达到更紧密的和谐。每一次声音的尝试，每一次新发现的不和谐，都会几乎不知不觉地引导不那么完美的一方修改他或她的发声，以接近更完美的一方。经过多次尝试和多次接近，结果终于实现。终于有一天，当惯常的婚姻合唱从整个直线国传出时，三个远方的恋人突然发现自己处于完全和谐之中，而且在他们意识到之前，已婚的三重唱在声音上被吸入一个双重拥抱；自然为又一桩婚姻和又三个新生命的诞生而欢欣。"

\chapter{我如何徒劳地试图向直线国国王解释平面国的性质}

Thinking that it was time to bring down the Monarch from his raptures to the level of common sense, I determined to endeavour to open up to him some glimpses of the truth, that is to say of the nature of things in Flatland. So I began thus: ``How does your Royal Highness distinguish the shapes and positions of his subjects? I for my part noticed by the sense of sight, before I entered your Kingdom, that some of your people are lines and others Points; and that some of the lines are larger---'' ``You speak of an impossibility,'' interrupted the King; ``you must have seen a vision; for to detect the difference between a Line and a Point by the sense of sight is, as every one knows, in the nature of things, impossible; but it can be detected by the sense of hearing, and by the same means my shape can be exactly ascertained. Behold me---I am a Line, the longest in Lineland, over six inches of Space---'' ``Of Length,'' I ventured to suggest. ``Fool,'' said he, ``Space is Length. Interrupt me again, and I have done.''

我认为现在是时候将君主从他的狂喜中拉回到常识的层面了，我决定努力让他窥见一些真理的曙光，也就是说，让他了解平面国事物的本质。于是我这样开口道："殿下如何分辨他的臣民的形状和位置？我在进入您的王国之前，通过视觉注意到，您的一些子民是直线，另一些是点；而且有些直线更长——""你说的是不可能的事，"国王打断道；"你一定是看到了幻象；因为通过视觉来分辨直线和点的区别，众所周知，就事物的本质而言，是不可能的；但这可以通过听觉来察觉，通过同样的方法，我的形状也可以被精确地确定。看我——我是一条直线，是直线国中最长的，超过六英寸的空间——""是长度，"我斗胆建议道。"傻瓜，"他说，"空间就是长度。再打断我，我就不说了。"

I apologized; but he continued scornfully, ``Since you are impervious to argument, you shall hear with your ears how by means of my two voices I reveal my shape to my Wives, who are at this moment six thousand miles seventy yards two feet eight inches away, the one to the North, the other to the South. Listen, I call to them.''

我道歉了；但他轻蔑地继续道："既然你不可理喻，那就用你的耳朵听听，我是如何通过我的两个声音向我的妻子们揭示我的形状的——她们此刻在六千英里七十码两英尺八英寸之外，一个在北方，一个在南方。听着，我在叫她们。"

He chirruped, and then complacently continued: ``My wives at this moment receiving the sound of one of my voice, closely followed by the other, and perceiving that the latter reaches them after an interval in which sound can traverse 6.457 inches, infer that one of my mouths is 6.457 inches further from them than the other, and accordingly know my shape to be 6.457 inches. But you will of course understand that my wives do not make this calculation every time they hear my two voices. They made it, once for all, before we were married. But they \_could\_ make it at any time. And in the same way I can estimate the shape of any of my Male subjects by the sense of sound.''

他发出一声啁啾，然后得意地继续说："我的妻子们此刻听到我的一个声音，紧接着是另一个，并察觉到后者到达她们的时间间隔是声音可以传播6.457英寸的距离，于是推断出我的一个嘴比另一个嘴离她们远6.457英寸，因此知道我的形状是6.457英寸。但你当然会明白，我的妻子们并不是每次听到我的两个声音都做这个计算。她们在我们结婚前就一劳永逸地算过了。但她们\_可以\_在任何时候这样做。同样地，我可以通过听觉来估计我任何一位男性臣民的形状。"

``But how,'' said I, ``if a Man feigns a Woman's voice with one of his two voices, or so disguises his Southern voice that it cannot be recognized as the echo of the Northern? May not such deceptions cause great inconvenience? And have you no means of checking frauds of this kind by commanding your neighbouring subjects to feel one another?'' This of course was a very stupid question, for feeling could not have answered the purpose; but I asked with the view of irritating the Monarch, and I succeeded perfectly.

"但是，"我说，"如果一个男人用他的两个声音中的一个来假装女人的声音，或者把他南端的声音伪装得让别人无法认出是北端声音的回声，那该怎么办呢？这样的欺骗难道不会造成很大的不便吗？您难道没有办法通过命令您相邻的臣民互相触摸来防范这种欺诈吗？"这当然是一个非常愚蠢的问题，因为触摸无法达到这个目的；但我问这个问题是为了激怒君主，而且我完美地达到了目的。

``What!'' cried he in horror, ``explain your meaning.'' ``Feel, touch, come into contact,'' I replied. ``If you mean by \_feeling\_,'' said the King, ``approaching so close as to leave no space between two individuals, know, Stranger, that this offence is punishable in my dominions by death. And the reason is obvious. The frail form of a Woman, being liable to be shattered by such an approximation, must be preserved by the State; but since Women cannot be distinguished by the sense of sight from Men, the Law ordains universally that neither Man nor Woman shall be approached so closely as to destroy the interval between the approximator and the approximated.

"什么！"他惊恐地喊道，"解释你的意思。""触摸，触碰，接触，"我回答。"如果你所说的\_触摸\_是指，"国王说，"靠得如此之近以至于两个个体之间没有任何空间，那么请记住，陌生人，在我的领地内，这种违法行为可处以死刑。原因很明显。女性脆弱的身体结构，由于很容易被这种接近所击碎，必须受到国家的保护；但由于女性不能通过视觉与男性区分开来，法律普遍规定，无论男性还是女性，都不能被靠近到足以破坏接近者与被接近者之间间隔的程度。

``And indeed what possible purpose would be served by this illegal and unnatural excess of approximation which you call \_touching\_, when all the ends of so brutal and course a process are attained at once more easily and more exactly by the sense of hearing? As to your suggested danger of deception, it is non-existent: for the Voice, being the essence of one's Being, cannot be thus changed at will. But come, suppose that I had the power of passing through solid things, so that I could penetrate my subjects, one after another, even to the number of a billion, verifying the size and distance of each by the sense of \_feeling:\_ How much time and energy would be wasted in this clumsy and inaccurate method! Whereas now, in one moment of audition, I take as it were the census and statistics, local, corporeal, mental and spiritual, of every living being in Lineland. Hark, only hark!''

"而且事实上，你所称的这种非法且不自然的过度接近——即\_触碰\_——究竟有什么可能的用途呢？如此残忍粗俗的过程的所有目的，通过听觉都能更轻松、更精确地实现。至于你所暗示的欺骗危险，它是不存在的：因为声音作为一个人存在的本质，不能随意改变。但是，假设我有能力穿过固体，这样我就能穿透我的臣民，一个接一个，甚至达到十亿之多，通过\_触觉\_来验证每一个的大小和距离——这种笨拙而不精确的方法会浪费多少时间和精力！而现在，在一瞬间的听觉中，我仿佛就能对直线国中每一个生物进行普查和统计——包括当地的、肉体的、精神的和心灵的。听着，你只管听！"

So saying he paused and listened, as if in an ecstasy, to a sound which seemed to me no better than a tiny chirping from an innumerable multitude of lilliputian grasshoppers.

说着，他停下来倾听，仿佛在狂喜中倾听一个声音——在我看来，这声音不过是来自无数侏儒蚱蜢的细微唧唧声而已。

``Truly,'' replied I, ``your sense of hearing serves you in good stead, and fills up many of your deficiencies. But permit me to point out that your life in Lineland must be deplorably dull. To see nothing but a Point! Not even to be able to contemplate a Straight Line! Nay, not even to know what a Straight Line is! To see, yet to be cut off from those Linear prospects which are vouchsafed to us in Flatland! Better surely to have no sense of sight at all than to see so little! I grant you I have not your discriminative faculty of hearing; for the concert of all Lineland which gives you such intense pleasure, is to me no better than a multitudinous twittering or chirping. But at least I can discern, by sight, a Line from a Point. And let me prove it. Just before I came into your kingdom, I saw you dancing from left to right, and then from right to left, with Seven Men and a Woman in your immediate proximity on the left, and eight Men and two Women on your right. Is not this correct?''

"确实，"我回答说，"你的听觉对你很有帮助，弥补了你的许多不足。但请允许我指出，你在直线国的生活一定是可悲地沉闷。除了一个点什么也看不见！甚至不能凝视一条直线！不，甚至不知道直线是什么！能看见，却被剥夺了我们平面国人所享有的那些直线的风景！肯定地说，完全没有视觉能力也比看见得这么少要好！我承认我没有你那种敏锐的听觉辨别力；因为给你带来如此强烈快乐的整个直线国的合唱，在我看来不过是无数的啁啾或唧唧声。但至少我能通过视觉分辨直线和点。让我证明给你看。就在我进入你的王国之前，我看见你从左到右跳舞，然后从右到左，在你左边紧邻处有七个男人和一个女人，在你右边有八个男人和两个女人。这不是正确的吗？"

``It is correct,'' said the King, ``so far as the numbers and sexes are concerned, though I know not what you mean by `right' and `left.' But I deny that you saw these things. For how could you see the Line, that is to say the inside, of any Man? But you must have heard these things, and then dreamed that you saw them. And let me ask what you mean by those words `left' and `right.' I suppose it is your way of saying Northward and Southward.''

"这是正确的，"国王说，"就数量和性别而言，虽然我不知道你所说的'左'和'右'是什么意思。但我否认你看到了这些东西。因为你怎么能看到任何男人的直线，也就是说，他的内部？但你一定是听到了这些事，然后梦到你看到了它们。让我问一下，你所说的'左'和'右'是什么意思？我猜这是你说北方和南方的方式。"

``Not so,'' replied I; ``besides your motion of Northward and Southward, there is another motion which I call from right to left.''

"不是这样，"我回答说；"除了你向北和向南的运动之外，还有另一种运动，我称之为从右到左。"

\_King\_. Exhibit to me, if you please, this motion from left to right.

\_国王\_. 请你向我展示一下这种从左到右的运动。

\_I\_. Nay, that I cannot do, unless you could step out of your Line altogether.

\_我\_. 不，我做不到，除非你能完全走出你的直线。

\_King\_. Out of my Line? Do you mean out of the world? Out of Space?

\_国王\_. 走出我的直线？你是说走出世界？走出空间？

\_I\_. Well, yes. Out of \_your\_ world. Out of \_your\_ Space. For your Space is not the true Space. True Space is a Plane; but your Space is only a Line.

\_我\_. 是的。走出\_你的\_世界。走出\_你的\_空间。因为你的空间不是真正的空间。真正的空间是一个平面；但你的空间只是一条直线。

\_King\_. If you cannot indicate this motion from left to right by yourself moving in it, then I beg you to describe it to me in words.

\_国王\_. 如果你不能通过自己在其中运动来指示这种从左到右的运动，那么我请求你用言语向我描述它。

\_I\_. If you cannot tell your right side from your left, I fear that no words of mine can make my meaning clearer to you. But surely you cannot be ignorant of so simple a distinction.

\_我\_. 如果你分不清你的右侧和左侧，我恐怕我的任何言语都不能让你更明白我的意思。但你肯定不会对如此简单的区别一无所知。

\_King\_. I do not in the least understand you.

\_国王\_. 我一点也不理解你。

\_I\_. Alas! How shall I make it clear? When you move straight on, does it not sometimes occur to you that you \_could\_ move in some other way, turning your eye round so as to look in the direction towards which your side is now fronting? In other words, instead of always moving in the direction of one of your extremities, do you never feel a desire to move in the direction, so to speak, of your side?

\_我\_. 唉！我怎样才能说清楚呢？当你一直向前移动时，你难道从来没有想过你\_可以\_以其他方式移动吗？转动你的眼睛，使其朝向你的侧面现在所朝向的方向？换句话说，除了总是沿你某一端的方向移动之外，你难道从来没有感到有一种可以说沿你侧面方向移动的渴望吗？

\_King\_. Never. And what do you mean? How can a man's inside ``front'' in any direction? Or how can a man move in the direction of his inside?

\_国王\_. 从来没有。你是什么意思？一个人的内部怎么能朝向任何方向？或者一个人怎么能沿他内部的方向移动？

\_I\_. Well then, since words cannot explain the matter, I will try deeds, and will move gradually out of Lineland in the direction which I desire to indicate to you.

\_我\_. 那么，既然言语不能解释这件事，我就试着用行动，沿着我想向你指示的方向，逐渐移出直线国。

At the word I began to move my body out of Lineland. As long as any part of me remained in his dominion and in his view, the King kept exclaiming, ``I see you, I see you still; you are not moving.'' But when I had at last moved myself out of his Line, he cried in his shrillest voice, ``She is vanished; she is dead.'' ``I am not dead,'' replied I; ``I am simply out of Lineland, that is to say, out of the Straight Line which you call Space, and in the true Space, where I can see things as they are. And at this moment I can see your Line, or side---or inside as you are pleased to call it; and I can see also the Men and Women on the North and South of you, whom I will now enumerate, describing their order, their size, and the interval between each.''

话音未落，我开始将身体移出直线国。只要我的任何一部分还留在他的领地和视野内，国王就不停地喊道："我看见你，我还看见你；你没有在移动。"但当我最终将自己移出他的直线时，他用最尖锐的声音喊道："她消失了；她死了。""我没有死，"我回答说；"我只是在直线国之外，也就是说，在你称之为空间的那条直线之外，在真正的空间里，在那里我能看到事物的本来面目。此刻我能看到你的直线，或者说侧面——或者你乐意称之为内部的东西；我还能看到你北边和南边的男人们和女人们，现在我将列举他们，描述他们的顺序、他们的大小，以及每一个之间的间隔。"

When I had done this at great length, I cried triumphantly, ``Does that at last convince you?'' And, with that, I once more entered Lineland, taking up the same position as before.

当我详细地完成了这些描述后，我得意地喊道："这最终说服你了吗？"说完，我再次进入直线国，占据了和之前一样的位置。

But the Monarch replied, ``If you were a Man of sense---though, as you appear to have only one voice I have little doubt you are not a Man but a Woman---but, if you had a particle of sense, you would listen to reason. You ask me to believe that there is another Line besides that which my senses indicate, and another motion besides that of which I am daily conscious. I, in return, ask you to describe in words or indicate by motion that other Line of which you speak. Instead of moving, you merely exercise some magic art of vanishing and returning to sight; and instead of any lucid description of your new World, you simply tell me the numbers and sizes of some forty of my retinue, facts known to any child in my capital. Can anything be more irrational or audacious? Acknowledge your folly or depart from my dominions.''

但君主回答说："如果你是一个有理智的人——虽然从你只有一个声音来看，我几乎不怀疑你不是一个男人而是一个女人——但如果你有一丝理智，你就会听从理性。你要求我相信存在着我的感官所指示的那条直线之外的另一条直线，以及我每天所意识到的那种运动之外的另一种运动。作为回报，我要求你用言语描述或用运动来指示你所说的那另一条直线。你没有移动，而只是运用了某种消失并重新出现的魔术；你没有对你的新世界做出任何清晰的描述，而只是告诉我你的大约四十个随从的数量和大小——这些事实在我的首都中任何一个孩子都知道。还有什么比这更荒谬或更放肆的吗？承认你的愚蠢，或者离开我的领地。"

Furious at his perversity, and especially indignant that he professed to be ignorant of my sex, I retorted in no measured terms, ``Besotted Being! You think yourself the perfection of existence, while you are in reality the most imperfect and imbecile. You profess to see, whereas you see nothing but a Point! You plume yourself on inferring the existence of a Straight Line; but I \_can see\_ Straight Lines, and infer the existence of Angles, Triangles, Squares, Pentagons, Hexagons, and even Circles. Why waste more words? Suffice it that I am the completion of your incomplete self. You are a Line, but I am a Line of Lines called in my country a Square: and even I, infinitely superior though I am to you, am of little account among the great nobles of Flatland, whence I have come to visit you, in the hope of enlightening your ignorance.''

我对他的固执感到愤怒，特别是对他自称不知道我的性别感到愤慨，我毫不留情地反驳道："沉醉的东西！你自以为是存在的完美，而实际上你是最不完美、最愚蠢的。你自称能看见，而你除了一个点什么也看不见！你以推断出一条直线的存在而自豪；但我\_能看见\_直线，并能推断出角、三角形、正方形、五边形、六边形，甚至圆形的存在。为什么要浪费更多的言辞？只需记住，我是你不完整自我的完成。你是一条直线，但我是直线中的直线，在我的国家被称为正方形：即使我比你优越无限倍，在平面国的伟大贵族中也微不足道——我从那里来拜访你，希望能启发你的无知。"

Hearing these words the King advanced towards me with a menacing cry as if to pierce me through the diagonal; and in that same movement there arose from myriads of his subjects a multitudinous war-cry, increasing in vehemence till at last methought it rivalled the roar of an army of a hundred thousand Isosceles, and the artillery of a thousand Pentagons. Spell-bound and motionless, I could neither speak nor move to avert the impending destruction; and still the noise grew louder, and the King came closer, when I awoke to find the breakfast-bell recalling me to the realities of Flatland.

\chapter{来自空间国的陌生人}

From dreams I proceed to facts.

从梦境回到现实。

It was the last day of our 1999th year of our era. The patterning of the rain had long ago announced nightfall; and I was sitting\footnote{我说的"坐"，当然不是指你们空间国用这个词所表示的那种姿势变化；因为我们没有脚，我们不能像你们那样"坐"或"站"（在你们的意义上），就像你们的比目鱼或鲽鱼一样。不过，我们完全能识别"躺"、"坐"和"站"所蕴涵的不同的意志心理状态，这些状态在某种程度上通过光泽的轻微增加向旁观者表示出来，光泽的增加与意志的增强相对应。但关于这一点，以及其他上千个类似的主题，时间不允许我详谈。} in the company of my wife, musing on the events of the past and the prospects of the coming year, the coming century, the coming Millennium.

那是我们纪元第1999年的最后一天。雨点的滴答声早已宣告了夜幕的降临；我坐在妻子的身旁，沉思着过去一年的事件和来年、来世纪、来千年的展望。

My four Sons and two orphan Grandchildren had retired to their several apartments; and my wife alone remained with me to see the old Millennium out and the new one in.

我的四个儿子和两个孤儿孙子孙女都已回到各自的房间；只有妻子一人陪着我，一起守岁，告别旧千年，迎接新千年。

I was rapt in thought, pondering in my mind some words that had casually issued from the mouth of my youngest Grandson, a most promising young Hexagon of unusual brilliancy and perfect angularity. His uncles and I had been giving him his usual practical lesson in Sight Recognition, turning ourselves upon our centres, now rapidly, now more slowly, and questioning him as to our positions; and his answers had been so satisfactory that I had been induced to reward him by giving him a few hints on Arithmetic, as applied to Geometry.

我沉浸在思考中，在脑海里琢磨着我最小的孙子——一个前途无量的年轻六边形，具有非凡的光泽和完美的棱角——无意中说出的一些话。他的叔叔们和我一直在给他上照例的视觉识别实践课，我们围绕自己的中心转动，时快时慢，并问他我们的位置；他的回答令人非常满意，以致我不得不奖励他，给他讲一些关于算术在几何中应用的入门知识。

Taking nine Squares, each an inch every way, I had put them together so as to make one large Square, with a side of three inches, and I had hence proved to my little Grandson that---though it was impossible for us to \_see\_ the inside of the Square---yet we might ascertain the number of square inches in a Square by simply squaring the number of inches in the side: ``and thus,'' said I, ``we know that 32, or 9, represents the number of square inches in a Square whose side is 3 inches long.''

我取了九个正方形，每个都是一英寸见方，把它们拼在一起组成一个大正方形，边长为三英寸，由此向我的小孙子证明——虽然我们不可能\_看见\_正方形的内部——但我们可以通过简单地将边长的英寸数平方来确定一个正方形中平方英寸的数量："因此，"我说，"我们知道，3的平方，即9，代表了边长为3英寸的正方形中平方英寸的数量。"

The little Hexagon meditated on this a while and then said to me; ``But you have been teaching me to raise numbers to the third power: I suppose 33 must mean something in Geometry; what does it mean?'' ``Nothing at all,'' replied I, ``not at least in Geometry; for Geometry has only Two Dimensions.'' And then I began to shew the boy how a Point by moving through a length of three inches makes a Line of three inches, which may be represented by three; and how a Line of three inches, moving parallel to itself through a length of three inches, makes a Square of three inches every way, which may be represented by 32.

小六边形沉思了一会儿，然后对我说："但是你一直在教我把数字升到三次方：我想3的三次方在几何学中一定有某种含义；它是什么意思呢？""毫无意义，"我回答说，"至少在几何学中没有；因为几何学只有两个维度。"然后我开始向这孩子展示，一个点移动三英寸的长度如何构成一条三英寸的直线，可以用三来表示；一条三英寸的直线，平行于自身移动三英寸的长度，如何构成一个三英寸见方的正方形，可以用3的平方来表示。

Upon this, my Grandson, again returning to his former suggestion, took me up rather suddenly and exclaimed, ``Well, then, if a Point by moving three inches, makes a Line of three inches represented by three; and if a straight Line of three inches, moving parallel to itself, makes a Square of three inches every way, represented by 32; it must be that a Square of three inches every way, moving somehow parallel to itself (but I don't see how) must make Something else (but I don't see what) of three inches every way---and this must be represented by 33.''

听到这话，我的孙子又回到他之前的建议上，相当突然地打断我喊道："那么，如果一个点移动三英寸，构成一条由三表示的三英寸直线；如果一条三英寸的直线平行于自身移动，构成一个由3的平方表示的三英寸见方的正方形；那么一定是，一个三英寸见方的正方形，以某种方式平行于自身移动（但我不明白如何移动），一定构成另一个东西（但我不明白是什么），也是三英寸见方——而且这一定由3的三次方来表示。"

``Go to bed,'' said I, a little ruffled by this interruption: ``if you would talk less nonsense, you would remember more sense.''

"去睡觉，"我说，对他的打断有些恼怒："如果你少说些废话，就能多记住些有用的东西。"

So my Grandson had disappeared in disgrace; and there I sat by my Wife's side, endeavouring to form a retrospect of the year 1999 and of the possibilities of the year 2000; but not quite able to shake off the thoughts suggested by the prattle of my bright little Hexagon. Only a few sands now remained in the half-hour glass. Rousing myself from my reverie I turned the glass Northward for the last time in the old Millennium; and in the act, I exclaimed aloud, ``The boy is a fool.''

于是我的孙子便灰溜溜地走了；我坐在妻子身旁，努力回顾1999年和展望2000年的可能性；但我无法完全摆脱我那聪明的小六边形的天真话语所引发的思考。半小时的沙漏中只剩下几粒沙了。我从沉思中惊醒，在旧千年的最后一次将沙漏朝北翻转；就在这时，我大声喊道："这孩子真是个傻瓜。"

Straightway I became conscious of a Presence in the room, and a chilling breath thrilled through my very being. ``He is no such thing,'' cried my Wife, ``and you are breaking the Commandments in thus dishonouring your own Grandson.'' But I took no notice of her. Looking around in every direction I could see nothing; yet still I \_felt\_ a Presence, and shivered as the cold whisper came again. I started up. ``What is the matter?'' said my Wife, ``there is no draught; what are you looking for? There is nothing.'' There was nothing; and I resumed my seat, again exclaiming, ``The boy is a fool, I say; 33 can have no meaning in Geometry.'' At once there came a distinctly audible reply, ``The boy is not a fool; and 33 has an obvious Geometrical meaning.''

我立刻意识到房间里有一个存在，一阵冰冷的气息颤动了我的整个身心。"他不是那样的东西，"妻子喊道，"你这样侮辱自己的孙子，是在违反戒律。"但我没有理会她。我向四面八方张望，什么也看不见；但我仍然\_感觉\_到一个存在，当那冰冷的低语再次传来时，我颤抖了。我站了起来。"怎么了？"妻子说，"没有气流；你在找什么？什么也没有。"什么也没有；我重新坐下，再次喊道："这孩子是个傻瓜，我说；3的三次方在几何学中不可能有任何意义。"立刻传来一个清晰可闻的回答："这孩子不是傻瓜；3的三次方在几何学中有明显的意义。"

My Wife as well as myself heard the words, although she did not understand their meaning, and both of us sprang forward in the direction of the sound. What was our horror when we saw before us a Figure! At the first glance it appeared to be a Woman, seen sideways; but a moment's observation shewed me that the extremities passed into dimness too rapidly to represent one of the Female Sex; and I should have thought it a Circle, only that it seemed to change its size in a manner impossible for a Circle or for any regular Figure of which I had had experience.

我的妻子和我都听到了这些话，虽然她不明白其含义，我们俩都朝声音的方向跳了过去。当我们看到面前有一个图形时，惊恐万分！乍一看，它似乎是一个从侧面看到的女人；但片刻的观察便向我表明，它的两端消失得太快，不可能代表女性；我本应认为它是一个圆，只是它似乎以一种对圆或对我所经验过的任何规则图形来说都不可能的方式在改变大小。

But my Wife had not my experience, nor the coolness necessary to note these characteristics. With the usual hastiness and unreasoning jealousy of her Sex, she flew at once to the conclusion that a Woman had entered the house through some small aperture. ``How comes this person here?'' she exclaimed, ``you promised me, my dear, that there should be no ventilators in our new house.'' ``Nor are they any,'' said I; ``but what makes you think that the stranger is a Woman? I see by my power of Sight Recognition---''

但我的妻子没有我的经验，也没有注意到这些特征所需的冷静。凭着她那一类人特有的急躁和无理性的嫉妒，她立刻得出结论：一个女人通过某个小缝隙进入了房子。"这个人怎么会在这里？"她惊叫道，"你答应过我，亲爱的，我们的新房子里不装通风孔。""确实没有，"我说；"但你凭什么认为这个陌生人是女人？我通过我的视觉识别能力看出——"

``Oh, I have no patience with your Sight Recognition,'' replied she, ```Feeling is believing' and `A Straight Line to the touch is worth a Circle to the sight'''---two Proverbs, very common with the Frailer Sex in Flatland.

"哦，我没有耐心听你说什么视觉识别，"她回答说，"'触感即相信'和'触觉的直线胜过视觉的圆'"——这两句是平面国中柔弱性别中非常流行的谚语。

``Well,'' said I, for I was afraid of irritating her, ``if it must be so, demand an introduction.'' Assuming her most gracious manner, my Wife advanced towards the Stranger, ``Permit me, Madam to feel and be felt by---'' then, suddenly recoiling, ``Oh! it is not a Woman, and there are no angles either, not a trace of one. Can it be that I have so misbehaved to a perfect Circle?''

"好吧，"我说，因为我怕惹恼她，"如果必须这样，那就要求介绍一下吧。"我的妻子装出最优雅的姿态，向陌生人走去，"请允许我，夫人，去触摸并被您触摸——"然后突然后退，"哦！这不是一个女人，而且也没有角，一丝痕迹都没有。难道我竟然对一个完美的圆如此失礼吗？"

``I am indeed, in a certain sense a Circle,'' replied the Voice, ``and a more perfect Circle than any in Flatland; but to speak more accurately, I am many Circles in one.'' Then he added more mildly, ``I have a message, dear Madam, to your husband, which I must not deliver in your presence; and, if you would suffer us to retire for a few minutes---'' But my wife would not listen to the proposal that our august Visitor should so incommode himself, and assuring the Circle that the hour of her own retirement had long passed, with many reiterated apologies for her recent indiscretion, she at last retreated to her apartment.

"我确实在某种意义上是一个圆，"那声音回答说，"而且是一个比平面国中任何圆都更完美的圆；但更准确地说，我是许多个圆合而为一。"然后他更温和地补充道，"我有一个消息，亲爱的夫人，要带给您的丈夫，我不能在您面前传达；如果您允许我们回避几分钟的话——"但我的妻子不愿听我们尊贵的访客如此麻烦自己的提议，她一再向那圆保证，她自己的就寝时间早已过去，并为她最近的轻率行为多次道歉，最后才回到自己的房间。

I glanced at the half-hour glass. The last sands had fallen. The third Millennium had begun.

我瞥了一眼半小时沙漏。最后的沙粒已经落下。第三个千年开始了。

\chapter{陌生人如何徒劳地试图用言语向我揭示空间国的奥秘}

As soon as the sound of the Peace-cry of my departing Wife had died away, I began to approach the Stranger with the intention of taking a nearer view and of bidding him be seated: but his appearance struck me dumb and motionless with astonishment. Without the slightest symptoms of angularity he nevertheless varied every instant with graduations of size and brightness scarcely possible for any Figure within the scope of my experience. The thought flashed across me that I might have before me a burglar or cut-throat, some monstrous Irregular Isosceles, who, by feigning the voice of a Circle, had obtained admission somehow into the house, and was now preparing to stab me with his acute angle.

我妻子离去时的和平呼声一消失，我便开始走近陌生人，打算更仔细地端详他并请他坐下：但他的外貌使我惊讶得哑口无言、一动不动。他没有丝毫角的迹象，但他的大小和亮度却在每一瞬间都以某种梯度变化，这种变化对于我经验范围内的任何图形来说几乎是不可能的。一个念头闪过我的脑海：我面前的可能是一个窃贼或凶手，某个畸形的不规则等腰三角形，他通过假装圆的声音设法进入了房子，现在正准备用他的锐角刺我。

In a sitting-room, the absence of Fog (and the season happened to be remarkably dry), made it difficult for me to trust to Sight Recognition, especially at the short distance at which I was standing. Desperate with fear, I rushed forward with an unceremonious, ``You must permit me, Sir---'' and felt him. My Wife was right. There was not the trace of an angle, not the slightest roughness or inequality: never in my life had I met with a more perfect Circle. He remained motionless while I walked around him, beginning from his eye and returning to it again. Circular he was throughout, a perfectly satisfactory Circle; there could not be a doubt of it. Then followed a dialogue, which I will endeavour to set down as near as I can recollect it, omitting only some of my profuse apologies---for I was covered with shame and humiliation that I, a Square, should have been guilty of the impertinence of feeling a Circle. It was commenced by the Stranger with some impatience at the lengthiness of my introductory process.

在客厅里，由于没有雾（而且季节恰好非常干燥），我很难相信视觉识别，特别是在我所站的短距离内。我绝望之余，不顾一切地冲上前去，说"您必须允许我，先生——"然后摸了他一下。我妻子是对的。连一点角的痕迹都没有，最轻微的粗糙或不平也没有：我一生中从未遇到过比他更完美的圆。当我绕着他走，从他的眼睛开始又回到眼睛时，他保持一动不动。他整个身子都是圆形的，一个完全令人满意的圆；这是毫无疑问的。接着是一段对话，我将尽力凭记忆尽可能准确地记述下来，只略去了我一些过多的道歉——因为我为自己作为一个正方形，竟然对一个圆做出无礼的触摸行为而感到羞愧和屈辱。对话是由陌生人开始的，他对我冗长的介绍过程表现出一些不耐烦。

\_Stranger\_. Have you felt me enough by this time? Are you not introduced to me yet?

\_陌生人\_. 你摸我摸够了吗？我们还没有正式介绍吗？

\_I\_. Most illustrious Sir, excuse my awkwardness, which arises not from ignorance of the usages of polite society, but from a little surprise and nervousness, consequent on this somewhat unexpected visit. And I beseech you to reveal my indiscretion to no one, and especially not to my Wife. But before your Lordship enters into further communications, would he deign to satisfy the curiosity of one who would gladly know whence his visitor came?

\_我\_. 最尊敬的先生，请原谅我的笨拙，这不是出于对礼貌社会习俗的无知，而是出于一些惊讶和紧张，这是由于这次有些意外的访问所致。我恳求您不要将我的轻率行为告诉任何人，特别是不要告诉我的妻子。但在阁下进一步交流之前，能否请您屈尊满足一下一个很想知道来访者从何而来的人的好奇心？

\_Stranger\_. From Space, from Space, Sir: whence else?

\_陌生人\_. 来自空间，来自空间，先生：还能来自哪里？

\_I\_. Pardon me, my Lord, but is not your Lordship already in Space, your Lordship and his humble servant, even at this moment?

\_我\_. 对不起，我的主，但阁下不是已经在空间中了吗？阁下和您的谦卑仆人，甚至此刻不就在空间中吗？

\_Stranger\_. Pooh! what do you know of Space? Define Space.

\_陌生人\_. 呸！你对空间知道些什么？定义空间。

\_I\_. Space, my Lord, is height and breadth indefinitely prolonged.

\_我\_. 空间，我的主，是无限延伸的高度和宽度。

\_Stranger\_. Exactly: you see you do not even know what Space is. You think it is of Two Dimensions only; but I have come to announce to you a Third---height, breadth, and length.

\_陌生人\_. 正是如此：你看，你甚至不知道空间是什么。你以为它只有两个维度；但我来向你宣布第三个维度——高度、宽度和长度。

\_I\_. Your Lordship is pleased to be merry. We also speak of length and height, or breadth and thickness, thus denoting Two Dimensions by four names.

\_我\_. 阁下是在开玩笑。我们也谈论长度和高度，或宽度和厚度，因此用四个名称来表示两个维度。

\_Stranger\_. But I mean not only three names, but Three Dimensions.

\_陌生人\_. 但我指的不只是三个名称，而是三个维度。

\_I\_. Would your Lordship indicate or explain to me in what direction is the Third Dimension, unknown to me?

\_我\_. 阁下能否向我指出或解释一下第三个维度在什么方向上，是我所不知道的？

\_Stranger\_. I came from it. It is up above and down below.

\_陌生人\_. 我就是从那里来的。它在上面和下面。

\_I\_. My Lord means seemingly that it is Northward and Southward.

\_我\_. 我的主似乎是指北方和南方。

\_Stranger\_. I mean nothing of the kind. I mean a direction in which you cannot look, because you have no eye in your side.

\_陌生人\_. 我指的不是那种东西。我指的是一个你看不见的方向，因为你侧面没有眼睛。

\_I\_. Pardon me, my Lord, a moment's inspection will convince your Lordship that I have a perfectly luminary at the juncture of my two sides.

\_我\_. 对不起，我的主，稍加观察就会使阁下相信，我在我两条边的交界处有一个完美的发光体。

\_Stranger\_. Yes: but in order to see into Space you ought to have an eye, not on your Perimeter, but on your side, that is, on what you would probably call your inside; but we in Spaceland should call it your side.

\_陌生人\_. 是的：但要看见空间，你应该有一只眼睛，不是在你的周长上，而是在你的侧面，也就是说，在你可能称之为内部的地方；但在我们空间国，我们应该称之为你的侧面。

\_I\_. An eye in my inside! An eye in my stomach! Your Lordship jests.

\_我\_. 我内部的一只眼睛！我肚子上的一只眼睛！阁下是在开玩笑。

\_Stranger\_. I am in no jesting humour. I tell you that I come from Space, or, since you will not understand what Space means, from the Land of Three Dimensions whence I but lately looked down upon your Plane which you call Space forsooth. From that position of advantage I discerned all that you speak of as \_solid\_ (by which you mean ``enclosed on four sides''), your houses, your churches, your very chests and safes, yes even your insides and stomachs, all lying open and exposed to my view.

\_陌生人\_. 我没有开玩笑的心情。我告诉你，我来自空间，或者说，既然你不明白空间是什么意思，我来自三维之地——我不久前才从那里俯视你们称之为空间的平面。从那个有利位置，我分辨出了你所说的\_立体\_（你称之为"四面围着的"）、你的房屋、你的教堂、你的箱子和保险箱，甚至你的内部和肚子，全都敞开着，暴露在我的视野中。

\_I\_. Such assertions are easily made, my Lord.

\_我\_. 这样的断言很容易说出口，我的主。

\_Stranger\_. But not easily proved, you mean. But I mean to prove mine.

\_陌生人\_. 但不容易证明，你是这个意思。但我打算证明我的话。

When I descended here, I saw your four Sons, the Pentagons, each in his apartment, and your two Grandsons the Hexagons; I saw your youngest Hexagon remain a while with you and then retire to his room, leaving you and your Wife alone. I saw your Isosceles servants, three in number, in the kitchen at supper, and the little Page in the scullery. Then I came here, and how do you think I came?

当我下到这里时，我看见你的四个儿子——五边形——各自在自己的房间里，还有你的两个孙子——六边形；我看见你最小的六边形孙子和你待了一会儿，然后回到他自己的房间，留下你和你的妻子独处。我看见你的三个等腰三角形仆人在厨房里吃晚饭，还有小侍从在洗碗间。然后我来到这里，你以为我是怎么来的？

\_I\_. Through the roof, I suppose.

\_我\_. 我猜是通过屋顶。

\_Stranger\_. Not so. Your roof, as you know very well, has been recently repaired, and has no aperture by which even a Woman could penetrate. I tell you I come from Space. Are you not convinced by what I have told you of your children and household?

\_陌生人\_. 不是这样。你的屋顶，正如你很清楚的，最近刚修过，甚至连一个女人都无法穿透的缝隙都没有。我告诉你我来自空间。你难道还不相信我告诉你的关于你的孩子和家庭的事吗？

\_I\_. Your Lordship must be aware that such facts touching the belongings of his humble servant might be easily ascertained by any one of the neighbourhood possessing your Lordship's ample means of information.

\_我\_. 阁下一定知道，这些涉及您谦卑仆人财物的事实，任何一个拥有阁下充足信息手段的邻居都可能轻易地查明。

\_Stranger\_. (\_to himself\_.) What must I do? Stay; one more argument suggests itself to me. When you see a Straight Line--- your wife, for example---how many Dimensions do you attribute to her?

\_陌生人\_. （\_自言自语\_）。我该怎么办？等一下；我想到了一个论据。当你看到一条直线——例如你的妻子——你认为她有几个维度？

\_I\_. Your Lordship would treat me as if I were one of the vulgar who, being ignorant of Mathematics, suppose that a Woman is really a Straight Line, and only of One Dimension. No, no, my Lord; we Squares are better advised, and are as well aware of your Lordship that a Woman, though popularly called a Straight Line, is, really and scientifically, a very thin Parallelogram, possessing Two Dimensions, like the rest of us, viz., length and breadth (or thickness).

\_我\_. 阁下把我当作那种粗俗之人，以为我不懂数学，认为女人真的是一条直线，只有一个维度。不，不，我的主；我们正方形更明白事理，也和阁下一样清楚地知道，一个女人虽然俗称直线，但真正科学地说，她是一个非常薄的平行四边形，拥有两个维度，和我们其他人一样，即长度和宽度（或厚度）。

\_Stranger\_. But the very fact that a Line is visible implies that it possesses yet another Dimension.

\_陌生人\_. 但一条直线是可见的这一事实，意味着它还拥有另一个维度。

\_I\_. My Lord, I have just acknowledged that a Woman is broad as well as long. We see her length, we infer her breadth; which, though very slight, is capable of measurement.

\_我\_. 我的主，我刚才已经承认一个女人既有宽度又有长度。我们看见她的长度，我们推断她的宽度；虽然非常微小，但可以测量。

\_Stranger\_. You do not understand me. I mean that when you see a Woman, you ought---besides inferring her breadth---to see her length, and to \_see\_ what we call her \_height;\_ although the last Dimension is infinitesimal in your country. If a Line were mere length without ``height,'' it would cease to occupy Space and would become invisible. Surely you must recognize this?

\_陌生人\_. 你不明白我的意思。我的意思是，当你看到一个女人时，你应该——除了推断她的宽度之外——看见她的长度，并\_看见\_我们所谓的她的\_高度；\_尽管最后一个维度在你们国家是无限小的。如果一条直线仅仅是长度而没有"高度"，它就会不再占据空间而变得不可见。你肯定认识到这一点吧？

\_I\_. I must indeed confess that I do not in the least understand your Lordship. When we in Flatland see a Line, we see length and \_brightness\_. If the brightness disappears, the Line is extinguished, and, as you say, ceases to occupy Space. But am I to suppose that your Lordship gives the brightness the title of a Dimension, and that what we call ``bright'' you call ``high''?

\_我\_. 我必须确实承认我一点也不理解阁下。当我们在平面国看到一条直线时，我们看到的是长度和\_亮度\_。如果亮度消失，直线就熄灭了，而且正如你所说，不再占据空间。但我是否应该假设阁下将亮度称之为一个维度，而我们称之为"亮"的东西，阁下称之为"高"？

\_Stranger\_. No, indeed. By ``height'' I mean a Dimension like your length: only, with you, ``height'' is not so easily perceptible, being extremely small.

\_陌生人\_. 不，确实不是。我说的"高度"是指一个像你的长度一样的维度：只是对你来说，"高度"因为极其微小而不容易被察觉。

\_I\_. My Lord, your assertion is easily put to the test. You say I have a Third Dimension, which you call ``height.'' Now, Dimension implies direction and measurement. Do but measure my ``height,'' or merely indicate to me the direction in which my ``height'' extends, and I will become your convert. Otherwise, your Lordship's own understand must hold me excused.

\_我\_. 我的主，你的断言很容易检验。你说我有第三个维度，你称之为"高度"。那么，维度意味着方向和测量。你只要测量我的"高度"，或者仅仅向我指出我的"高度"延伸的方向，我就会成为你的信徒。否则，阁下的判断力必须原谅我。

\_Stranger\_. (\_to himself\_.) I can do neither. How shall I convince him? Surely a plain statement of facts followed by ocular demonstration ought to suffice. ---Now, Sir; listen to me.

\_陌生人\_. （\_自言自语\_）。我两者都做不到。我怎样才能说服他？当然，一个简单的事实陈述加上目视演示应该足够了。——现在，先生；听我说。

You are living on a Plane. What you style Flatland is the vast level surface of what I may call a fluid, or in, the top of which you and your countrymen move about, without rising above or falling below it.

你生活在一个平面上。你所称的平面国是我可以称之为流体的那个东西的巨大水平表面，或者说，你和你的同胞在其顶部移动，而不向上升起或向下降落。

I am not a plane Figure, but a Solid. You call me a Circle; but in reality I am not a Circle, but an infinite number of Circles, of size varying from a Point to a Circle of thirteen inches in diameter, one placed on the top of the other. When I cut through your plane as I am now doing, I make in your plane a section which you, very rightly, call a Circle. For even a Sphere---which is my proper name in my own country---if he manifest himself at all to an inhabitant of Flatland---must needs manifest himself as a Circle.

我不是一个平面图形，而是一个立体。你叫我圆；但实际上我不是一个圆，而是无数个圆的集合，其大小从一个点到一个直径为十三英寸的圆不等，一个放在另一个上面。当我像现在这样穿过你的平面时，我在你的平面上切出一个截面，你非常正确地称之为圆。因为即使是一个球体——这是我在我自己国家的正式名称——如果他要向平面国的居民显现自己——就必须以圆的形式显现。

Do you not remember---for I, who see all things, discerned last night the phantasmal vision of Lineland written upon your brain---do you not remember, I say, how when you entered the realm of Lineland, you were compelled to manifest yourself to the King, not as a Square, but as a Line, because that Linear Realm had not Dimensions enough to represent the whole of you, but only a slice or section of you? In precisely the same way, your country of Two Dimensions is not spacious enough to represent me, a being of Three, but can only exhibit a slice or section of me, which is what you call a Circle.

你难道不记得吗——因为我能看见一切，昨晚我看到了写在你脑海中的直线国幻象——你难道不记得，当你进入直线国的领域时，你被迫向国王显现的不是一个正方形，而是一条直线，因为那个线性王国没有足够的维度来代表完整的你，而只能代表你的一个切片或截面？正是以同样的方式，你的二维国家没有足够的空间来代表我——一个三维的存在——而只能展示我的一个切片或截面，也就是你所称的圆。

The diminished brightness of your eye indicates incredulity. But now prepare to receive proof positive of the truth of my assertions. You cannot indeed see more than one of my sections, or Circles, at a time; for you have no power to raise your eye out of the plane of Flatland; but you can at least see that, as I rise in Space, so my sections become smaller. See now, I will rise; and the effect upon your eye will be that my Circle will become smaller and smaller till it dwindles to a point and finally vanishes.

你眼睛亮度的减弱表明你不相信。但现在准备好接受我断言真实性的确实证据。你确实不能同时看到我的多个截面或圆；因为你没有能力将你的眼睛抬出平面国的平面；但你至少能看到，当我在空间中上升时，我的截面变得越来越小。现在看，我要上升了；对你眼睛的影响将是，我的圆会变得越来越小，直到缩小成一个点，最终消失。

There was no ``rising'' that I could see; but he diminished and finally vanished. I winked once or twice to make sure that I was not dreaming. But it was no dream. For from the depths of nowhere came forth a hollow voice---close to my heart it seemed---``Am I quite gone? Are you convinced now? Well, now I will gradually return to Flatland and you shall see my section become larger and larger.''

我看不到任何"上升"；但他变小了，最终消失了。我眨了一两次眼，以确保我不是在做梦。但这不是梦。因为从虚无的深处传来一个空洞的声音——似乎就在我心边——"我完全消失了吗？你现在相信了吗？好，现在我将逐渐回到平面国，你会看到我的截面变得越来越大。"

Every reader in Spaceland will easily understand that my mysterious Guest was speaking the language of truth and even of simplicity. But to me, proficient though I was in Flatland Mathematics, it was by no means a simple matter. The rough diagram given above will make it clear to any Spaceland child that the Sphere, ascending in the three positions indicated there, must needs have manifested himself to me, or to any Flatlander, as a Circle, at first of full size, then small, and at last very small indeed, approaching to a Point. But to me, although I saw the facts before me, the causes were as dark as ever. All that I could comprehend was, that the Circle had made himself smaller and vanished, and that he had now re-appeared and was rapidly making himself larger.

每一个空间国的读者都能轻易理解，我的神秘客人说的是真话，甚至是简单的话。但对我来说，尽管我精通平面国数学，这绝不是一件简单的事。上面给出的粗略图示将向任何空间国的孩子说明，球体在所示的三个位置上升时，对我或任何平面国人来说，必然表现为一个圆，起初是完整的大小，然后变小，最后确实非常小，趋近于一个点。但对我来说，尽管我看到了眼前的事实，原因却一如既往地黑暗。我所能理解的只是，那个圆让自己变小并消失了，然后又重新出现并迅速变大。

When he regained his original size, he heaved a deep sigh; for he perceived by my silence that I had altogether failed to comprehend him. And indeed I was now inclining to the belief that he must be no Circle at all, but some extremely clever juggler; or else that the old wives' tales were true, and that after all there were such people as Enchanters and Magicians.

当他恢复到原来的大小时，他深深地叹了一口气；因为他从我沉默中察觉到，我完全没有理解他。事实上，我现在倾向于相信他根本不是一个圆，而是某个极其聪明的魔术师；或者老太婆的故事是真的，毕竟确实有巫师和魔法师这样的人。

After a long pause he muttered to himself, ``One resource alone remains, if I am not to resort to action. I must try the method of Analogy.'' Then followed a still longer silence, after which he continued our dialogue.

经过长时间的停顿，他自言自语道："如果我不诉诸行动，只剩下一个办法了。我必须尝试类比法。"然后是更长时间的沉默，之后他继续我们的对话。

\_Sphere\_. Tell me, Mr. Mathematician; if a Point moves Northward, and leaves a luminous wake, what name would you give to the wake?

\_球体\_. 告诉我，数学家先生；如果一个点向北移动，并留下一个发光的轨迹，你会给这个轨迹起什么名字？

\_I\_. A straight Line.

\_我\_. 一条直线。

\_Sphere\_. And a straight Line has how many extremities?

\_球体\_. 一条直线有几个端点？

\_I\_. Two.

\_我\_. 两个。

\_Sphere\_. Now conceive the Northward straight Line moving parallel to itself, East and West, so that every point in it leaves behind it the wake of a straight Line. What name will you give to the Figure thereby formed? We will suppose that it moves through a distance equal to the original straight line. ---What name, I say?

\_球体\_. 现在设想向北的直线平行于自身移动，向东和向西，使得其中的每一个点都留下一条直线的轨迹。你会给由此形成的图形起什么名字？我们假设它移动的距离等于原来的直线的长度。——什么名字，我说？

\_I\_. A square.

\_我\_. 一个正方形。

\_Sphere\_. And how many sides has a Square? How many angles?

\_球体\_. 一个正方形有几条边？几个角？

\_I\_. Four sides and four angles.

\_我\_. 四条边和四个角。

\_Sphere\_. Now stretch your imagination a little, and conceive a Square in Flatland, moving parallel to itself upward.

\_球体\_. 现在稍微伸展一下你的想象力，设想平面国中的一个正方形平行于自身向上移动。

\_I\_. What? Northward?

\_我\_. 什么？向北？

\_Sphere\_. No, not Northward; upward; out of Flatland altogether.

\_球体\_. 不，不是向北；向上；完全离开平面国。

If it moved Northward, the Southern points in the Square would have to move through the positions previously occupied by the Northern points. But that is not my meaning.

如果它向北移动，正方形中的南点就必须穿过北点先前占据的位置。但这不是我的意思。

I mean that every Point in you---for you are a Square and will serve the purpose of my illustration---every Point in you, that is to say in what you call your inside, is to pass upwards through Space in such a way that no Point shall pass through the position previously occupied by any other Point; but each Point shall describe a straight Line of its own. This is all in accordance with Analogy; surely it must be clear to you.

我的意思是，你身上的每一个点——因为你是一个正方形，可以作为我说明的例证——你身上的每一个点，也就是说，在你所谓的内部的每一个点，都要以这样的方式向上穿过空间：没有任何一个点会穿过其他点先前占据的位置；而是每个点都描绘出自己的一条直线。这一切都符合类比法；肯定对你说很清楚。

Restraining my impatience---for I was now under a strong temptation to rush blindly at my Visitor and to precipitate him into Space, or out of Flatland, anywhere, so that I could get rid of him---I replied:---

我抑制住我的不耐烦——因为我现在有一种强烈的冲动，想盲目地冲向我的访客，把他扔进空间，或者扔出平面国，扔到任何地方，以便摆脱他——我回答道：——

``And what may be the nature of the Figure which I am to shape out by this motion which you are pleased to denote by the word `upward'? I presume it is describable in the language of Flatland.''

"那么，你乐意用'向上'这个词来表示的这种运动所形成的图形的性质是什么？我推测它可以用平面国的语言来描述。"

\_Sphere\_. Oh, certainly. It is all plain and simple, and in strict accordance with Analogy---only, by the way, you must not speak of the result as being a Figure, but as a Solid. But I will describe it to you. Or rather not I, but Analogy.

\_球体\_. 哦，当然。这一切都简单明了，严格符合类比法——只是，顺便说一下，你不应该把结果称为图形，而应该称为立体。但我会描述给你听。或者不如说不是我，而是类比法来描述。

We began with a single Point, which of course---being itself a Point---has only \_one\_ terminal Point.

我们从一个单点开始，它当然——本身就是一个点——只有\_一个\_端点。

One Point produces a Line with \_two\_ terminal Points.

一个点产生一条直线，有\_两个\_端点。

One Line produces a Square with \_four\_ terminal Points.

一条直线产生一个正方形，有\_四个\_端点。

Now you can give yourself the answer to your own question: 1, 2, 4, are evidently in Geometrical Progression. What is the next number?

现在你可以自己回答自己的问题了：1、2、4，显然是几何级数。下一个数字是什么？

\_I\_. Eight.

\_我\_. 八。

\_Sphere\_. Exactly. The one Square produces a \_Something-which-you-do-not-as-yet-know-a-name-for-but-which-we-call-a-cube\_ with \_eight\_ terminal Points. Now are you convinced?

\_球体\_. 正是。一个正方形产生一个\_你还不知道名字的东西——我们称之为立方体\_，有\_八个\_端点。现在你信服了吗？

\_I\_. And has this Creature sides, as well as Angles or what you call ``terminal Points''?

\_我\_. 这个生物有边吗？以及角或你所谓的"端点"？

\_Sphere\_. Of course; and all according to Analogy. But, by the way, not what \_you\_ call sides, but what \_we\_ call sides. You would call them \_solids\_.

\_球体\_. 当然；一切都根据类比法。但顺便说一下，不是\_你\_所说的边，而是\_我们\_所说的边。你会称之为\_立体\_。

\_I\_. And how many solids or sides will appertain to this Being whom I am to generate by the motion of my inside in an ``upward'' direction, and whom you call a Cube?

\_我\_. 那么，我通过我的内部沿"向上"方向运动所生成的这个存在——你称之为立方体——有多少个立体或边？

\_Sphere\_. How can you ask? And you a mathematician! The side of anything is always, if I may so say, one Dimension behind the thing. Consequently, as there is no Dimension behind a Point, a Point has 0 sides; a Line, if I may so say, has 2 sides (for the points of a Line may be called by courtesy, its sides); a Square has 4 sides; 0, 2, 4; what Progression do you call that?

\_球体\_. 你怎么能问这个？你还是个数学家呢！任何东西的边总是，可以这么说，比该东西少一个维度。因此，由于点后面没有维度，一个点有0条边；一条直线，可以这么说，有2条边（因为直线的点可以被礼貌地称为它的边）；一个正方形有4条边；0、2、4；你称这是什么级数？

\_I\_. Arithmetical.

\_我\_. 算术级数。

\_Sphere\_. And what is the next number?

\_球体\_. 下一个数字是什么？

\_I\_. Six.

\_我\_. 六。

\_Sphere\_. Exactly. Then you see you have answered your own question. The Cube which you will generate will be bounded by six sides, that is to say, six of your insides. You see it all now, eh?

\_球体\_. 正是。那么你看，你已经回答了你自己的问题。你将生成的立方体将由六个边围成，也就是说，由你的六个内部围成。你现在都明白了吧？

``Monster,'' I shrieked, ``be thou juggler, enchanter, dream, or devil, no more will I endure thy mockeries. Either thou or I must perish.'' And saying these words I precipitated myself upon him.

"怪物，"我尖叫道，"不管你是魔术师、巫师、梦还是魔鬼，我再也不会忍受你的嘲弄。要么你死，要么我亡。"说着这些话，我向他猛扑过去。

\chapter{球体如何在言语无效之后采取了行动}

It was in vain. I brought my hardest right angle into violent collision with the Stranger, pressing on him with a force sufficient to have destroyed any ordinary Circle: but I could feel him slowly and unarrestably slipping from my contact; not edging to the right nor to the left, but moving somehow out of the world, and vanishing into nothing. Soon there was a blank. But still I heard the Intruder's voice.

这是徒劳的。我把我最硬的直角猛地撞向陌生人，用足以摧毁任何普通圆的力压在他身上：但我能感觉到他慢慢地、不可阻挡地从我接触中滑出；既不向右也不向左，而是以某种方式移出世界，消失于虚无。很快就出现了空白。但我仍然能听到那个入侵者的声音。

\_Sphere\_. Why will you refuse to listen to reason? I had hoped to find in you---as being a man of sense and an accomplished mathematician---a fit apostle for the Gospel of the Three Dimensions, which I am allowed to preach once only in a thousand years: but now I know not how to convince you. Stay, I have it. Deeds, and not words, shall proclaim the truth. Listen, my friend.

\_球体\_. 你为什么拒绝听从理性？我本希望在你身上找到——作为一个有理智的人和一个有成就的数学家——一个适合的三维福音使徒，这福音我每一千年才被允许传讲一次：但现在我不知道如何说服你了。等一下，我有办法了。行动，而不是言语，将宣布真理。听着，我的朋友。

I have told you I can see from my position in Space the inside of all things that you consider closed. For example, I see in yonder cupboard near which you are standing, several of what you call boxes (but like everything else in Flatland, they have no tops or bottom) full of money; I see also two tablets of accounts. I am about to descend into that cupboard and to bring you one of those tablets. I saw you lock the cupboard half an hour ago, and I know you have the key in your possession. But I descend from Space; the doors, you see, remain unmoved. Now I am in the cupboard and am taking the tablet. Now I have it. Now I ascend with it.

我告诉过你，我从空间中的位置能看到你认为封闭的所有事物的内部。例如，我看见你所站的旁边的那个橱柜里，有几个你称之为盒子的东西（但像平面国中的其他一切一样，它们没有顶部或底部）装满了钱；我还看见两个账本。我正要下到那个橱柜里，给你拿一个账本。我看见你半小时前锁了橱柜，我知道你手里有钥匙。但我是从空间下去的；你看，门没有动。现在我在橱柜里，正在拿账本。现在我拿到了。现在我带着它上来了。

I rushed to the closet and dashed the door open. One of the tablets was gone. With a mocking laugh, the Stranger appeared in the other corner of the room, and at the same time the tablet appeared upon the floor. I took it up. There could be no doubt---it was the missing tablet.

我冲到橱柜前，猛地拉开门。其中一个账本不见了。伴随着一声嘲弄的笑，陌生人出现在房间的另一个角落，同时账本出现在地板上。我把它拿了起来。毫无疑问——这就是那个失踪的账本。

I groaned with horror, doubting whether I was not out of my sense; but the Stranger continued: ``Surely you must now see that my explanation, and no other, suits the phenomena. What you call Solid things are really superficial; what you call Space is really nothing but a great Plane. I am in Space, and look down upon the insides of the things of which you only see the outsides. You could leave the Plane yourself, if you could but summon up the necessary volition. A slight upward or downward motion would enable you to see all that I can see.

我惊恐地呻吟着，怀疑自己是不是失去了理智；但陌生人继续说道："你现在肯定看到了，只有我的解释符合这些现象。你所谓的立体东西实际上是表面的；你所谓的空间实际上不过是一个巨大的平面。我在空间中，俯视着你只能看到外面的那些东西的内部。你自己也能离开平面，只要你能唤起必要的意志。一个轻微的向上或向下的运动就能使你看到我所能看到的一切。

``The higher I mount, and the further I go from your Plane, the more I can see, though of course I see it on a smaller scale. For example, I am ascending; now I can see your neighbour the Hexagon and his family in their several apartments; now I see the inside of the Theatre, ten doors off, from which the audience is only just departing; and on the other side a Circle in his study, sitting at his books. Now I shall come back to you. And, as a crowning proof, what do you say to my giving you a touch, just the least touch, in your stomach? It will not seriously injure you, and the slight pain you may suffer cannot be compared with the mental benefit you will receive.''

"我升得越高，离你的平面越远，我能看到的就越多，当然我看到的规模也越小。例如，我正在上升；现在我能看到你的邻居六边形和他的家人在他们的各个公寓里；现在我能看到剧院的内部，离这里有十扇门远，观众刚刚从那里离开；另一边有一个圆在他的书房里，坐在他的书本前。现在我将回到你身边。作为一个决定性的证明，你觉得我给你一下触碰怎么样，就在你的肚子上，最轻的一下？它不会严重伤害你，你可能遭受的轻微痛苦与你将获得的精神利益是无法相比的。"

Before I could utter a word of remonstrance, I felt a shooting pain in my inside, and a demoniacal laugh seemed to issue from within me. A moment afterwards the sharp agony had ceased, leaving nothing but a dull ache behind, and the Stranger began to reappear, saying, as he gradually increased in size, ``There, I have not hurt you much, have I? If you are not convinced now, I don't know what will convince you. What say you?''

在我能说出一句抗议的话之前，我感到内部一阵刺痛，一阵恶魔般的笑声似乎从我体内发出。片刻之后，剧烈的痛苦停止了，只留下隐隐的酸痛，陌生人开始重新出现，随着他逐渐变大，他说："好了，我没有伤到你太多，是吧？如果你现在还不信服，我不知道还有什么能说服你。你怎么说？"

My resolution was taken. It seemed intolerable that I should endure existence subject to the arbitrary visitations of a Magician who could thus play tricks with one's very stomach. If only I could in any way manage to pin him against the wall till help came!

我的决心已定。我无法忍受这样的生活——受一个魔术师任意的拜访，他能这样捉弄人的肚子。要是我能以任何方式把他钉在墙上等到救援到来就好了！

Once more I dashed my hardest angle against him, at the same time alarming the whole household by my cries for aid. I believe, at the moment of my onset, the Stranger had sunk below our Plane, and really found difficulty in rising. In any case he remained motionless, while I, hearing, as I thought, the sound of some help approaching, pressed against him with redoubled vigor, and continued to shout for assistance.

我再次用我最硬的角向他撞去，同时用我求救的呼喊惊动了全家。我相信，在我进攻的那一刻，陌生人已经沉到了我们的平面下面，并且确实难以升起。无论如何，他保持一动不动，而我，听到了我以为是救援到来的声音，以加倍的力量压在他身上，继续呼救。

A convulsive shudder ran through the Sphere. ``This must not be,'' I thought I heard him say: ``either he must listen to reason, or I must have recourse to the last resource of civilization.'' Then, addressing me in a louder tone, he hurriedly exclaimed, ``Listen: no stranger must witness what you have witnessed. Send your Wife back at once, before she enters the apartment. The Gospel of Three Dimensions must not be thus frustrated. Not thus must the fruits of one thousand years of waiting be thrown away. I hear her coming. Back! back! Away from me, or you must go with me---wither you know not---into the Land of Three Dimensions!''

球体浑身一阵痉挛。"这不行，"我想我听到他说："要么他必须听从理性，要么我必须诉诸文明的最后手段。"然后，他用更大的声音对我说话，匆忙地喊道："听着：任何陌生人都不能亲眼看到你所看到的一切。立刻把你的妻子送回去，在她进入房间之前。三维福音不能就这样被挫败。一千年等待的成果不能就这样被丢弃。我听到她来了。后退！后退！离我远点，否则你必须跟我走——你不知道去了哪里——进入三维之地！"

``Fool! Madman! Irregular!'' I exclaimed; ``never will I release thee; thou shalt pay the penalty of thine impostures.''

"傻瓜！疯子！不规则者！"我喊道；"我永远不会放过你；你必须为你的欺骗付出代价。"

``Ha! Is it come to this?'' thundered the Stranger: ``then meet your fate: out of your Plane you go. Once, twice, thrice! 'Tis done!''

"哈！事情竟到了这步田地？"陌生人怒吼道："那么就接受你的命运吧：你要离开你的平面了。一次，两次，三次！完成了！"

\chapter{我如何到了空间国，以及我在那里看见了什么}

An unspeakable horror seized me. There was a darkness; then a dizzy, sickening sensation of sight that was not like seeing; I saw a Line that was no Line; Space that was not Space: I was myself, and not myself. When I could find voice, I shrieked loud in agony, ``Either this is madness or it is Hell.'' ``It is neither,'' calmly replied the voice of the Sphere, ``it is Knowledge; it is Three Dimensions: open your eye once again and try to look steadily.''

一种难以言喻的恐惧攫住了我。先是一片黑暗；随后是一阵眩晕而令人作呕的视觉感受，那不像真正的看见；我看到的是一条不成其为线的线，不是空间的空间：我既是我自己，又不是我自己。当我能够发声时，我在痛苦中高声尖叫道："这要么是疯狂，要么是地狱。""两者都不是，"球体的声音平静地回答，"这是知识；这是第三维度：再睁开你的眼睛，试着凝神观看吧。"

I looked, and, behold, a new world! There stood before me, visibly incorporate, all that I had before inferred, conjectured, dreamed, of perfect Circular beauty. What seemed the centre of the Stranger's form lay open to my view: yet I could see no heart, lungs, nor arteries, only a beautiful harmonious Something---for which I had no words; but you, my Readers in Spaceland, would call it the surface of the Sphere.

我望去，看哪，一个新世界！在我面前，有形有体地矗立着我从前对完美圆形之美所推断、猜想、梦想的一切。那陌生人形态的中心部位对我一览无余：然而我看不到心脏、肺脏或动脉，只看到一个美丽而和谐的东西——我无以名之；但你们，我的空间国读者，会称之为球体的表面。

Prostrating myself mentally before my Guide, I cried, ``How is it, O divine ideal of consummate loveliness and wisdom that I see thy inside, and yet cannot discern thy heart, thy lungs, thy arteries, thy liver?'' ``What you think you see, you see not,'' he replied; ``it is not giving to you, nor to any other Being, to behold my internal parts. I am of a different order of Beings from those in Flatland. Were I a Circle, you could discern my intestines, but I am a Being, composed as I told you before, of many Circles, the Many in the One, called in this country a Sphere. And, just as the outside of a Cube is a Square, so the outside of a Sphere represents the appearance of a Circle.''

我在精神上匍匐在我的向导面前，喊道："何以如此，啊，神圣的、完满的爱与智慧的理想，我能看见你的内部，却看不见你的心脏、肺脏、动脉和肝脏？""你以为你看见的，其实并没有看见，"他答道；"无论是你还是任何其他存在，都无法看见我的内部构造。我与平面国的生灵属于不同的存在等级。假如我是一个圆，你能看见我的肠胃，但我是一个由许多圆组成的存在——正如我之前告诉你的——多中有一，在这个国家被称为球体。而且，正如立方体的外形是正方形，球体的外形呈现为圆形。"

Bewildered though I was by my Teacher's enigmatic utterance, I no longer chafed against it, but worshipped him in silent adoration. He continued, with more mildness in his voice. ``Distress not yourself if you cannot at first understand the deeper mysteries of Spaceland. By degrees they will dawn upon you. Let us begin by casting back a glance at the region whence you came. Return with me a while to the plains of Flatland and I will shew you that which you have often reasoned and thought about, but never seen with the sense of sight---a visible angle.'' ``Impossible!'' I cried; but, the Sphere leading the way, I followed as if in a dream, till once more his voice arrested me: ``Look yonder, and behold your own Pentagonal house, and all its inmates.''

尽管我为老师谜一般的话语所困惑，但我不再抵触，而是默默地崇拜着他。他以更温和的语调继续说："如果你一时无法理解空间国更深层的奥秘，不要苦恼。渐渐地你就会明白。让我们先回看一看你所来的地方。随我回到平面国的平原上，我将向你展示你常常思考和想象却从未亲眼见过的东西——一个可见的角。""不可能！"我喊道；但球体在前面带路，我如在梦中跟随，直到他的声音再次使我停下："看那边，看看你自己的五边形房子和里面所有的居民。"

I looked below, and saw with my physical eye all that domestic individuality which I had hitherto merely inferred with the understanding. And how poor and shadowy was the inferred conjecture in comparison with the reality which I now behold! My four Sons calmly asleep in the North-Western rooms, my two orphan Grandsons to the South; the Servants, the Butler, my Daughter, all in their several apartments. Only my affectionate Wife, alarmed by my continued absence, had quitted her room and was roving up and down in the Hall, anxiously awaiting my return. Also the Page, aroused by my cries, had left his room, and under pretext of ascertaining whether I had fallen somewhere in a faint, was prying into the cabinet in my study. All this I could now \_see\_, not merely infer; and as we came nearer and nearer, I could discern even the contents of my cabinet, and the two chests of gold, and the tablets of which the Sphere had made mention.

我向下看去，用肉眼看到了我以前仅靠理智推断的一切家庭生活的细节。与我现在看到的实在相比，那推断的猜想是多么贫乏而模糊啊！我的四个儿子安静地睡在西北的房间里，两个孤儿孙子在南边；仆人们、男管家、我的女儿，都在各自的房间里。只有我深情的妻子，因我持续的不在而惊慌，已经离开了她的房间，在大厅里来回徘徊，焦急地等待我的归来。还有被我的喊声惊醒的侍从，已经离开了他的房间，借口查看我是否昏倒在某处，正在窥探我书房里的柜子。所有这些我现在都能看见，而不仅仅是推断；当我们越飞越近时，我甚至能看清柜子里的东西、那两箱黄金，以及球体提到过的那些写字板。

Touched by my Wife's distress, I would have sprung downward to reassure her, but I found myself incapable of motion. ``Trouble not yourself about your Wife,'' said my Guide: ``she will not be long left in anxiety; meantime, let us take a survey of Flatland.''

我为妻子的痛苦所感动，想跳下去让她放心，但发现自己无法动弹。"不要为你的妻子担心，"我的向导说："她不会焦虑太久的；同时，让我们俯瞰一下平面国。"

Once more I felt myself rising through space. It was even as the Sphere had said. The further we receded from the object we beheld, the larger became the field of vision. My native city, with the interior of every house and every creature therein, lay open to my view in miniature. We mounted higher, and lo, the secrets of the earth, the depths of the mines and inmost caverns of the hills, were bared before me.

我再次感到自己在空间中升起。正如球体所说，我们离所视对象越远，视野就越大。我的故乡城市，连同每座房屋的内部和其中的每个生灵，都以微型的形式展现在我眼前。我们升得更高了，看哪，大地的秘密、矿山的深处和山丘最深处的洞穴，都在我面前暴露无遗。

Awestruck at the sight of the mysteries of the earth, thus unveiled before my unworthy eye, I said to my Companion, ``Behold, I am become as a God. For the wise men in our country say that to see all things, or as they express it, \_omnividence\_, is the attribute of God alone.'' There was something of scorn in the voice of my Teacher as he made answer: ``is it so indeed? Then the very pick-pockets and cut-throats of my country are to be worshipped by your wise men as being Gods: for there is not one of them that does not see as much as you see now. But trust me, your wise men are wrong.''

看到大地的奥秘如此在我不配的眼前揭开，我对同伴说："看哪，我已变得像神一样。因为我们国家的智者说，看见万物——或如他们所说的全视——是只有神才有的属性。"老师回答时声音中带着几分轻蔑："真的吗？那么我国家中的扒手和杀人犯也要被你们的智者奉为神明了：因为他们中没有一个人看到的不比你现在看到的多。但相信我，你们的智者错了。"

\_I\_. Then is omnividence the attribute of others besides Gods?

\_Sphere\_. I do not know. But, if a pick-pocket or a cut-throat of our country can see everything that is in your country, surely that is no reason why the pick-pocket or cut-throat should be accepted by you as a God. This omnividence, as you call it---it is not a common word in Spaceland---does it make you more just, more merciful, less selfish, more loving? Not in the least. Then how does it make you more divine?

\_I\_. "More merciful, more loving!" But these are the qualities of women! And we know that a Circle is a higher Being than a Straight Line, in so far as knowledge and wisdom are more to be esteemed than mere affection.

\_Sphere\_. It is not for me to classify human faculties according to merit. Yet many of the best and wisest in Spaceland think more of the affections than of the understanding, more of your despised Straight Lines than of your belauded Circles. But enough of this. Look yonder. Do you know that building?

\_我\_：那么全视是除了神之外其他存在的属性吗？

\_球体\_：我不知道。但是，如果我国的一个扒手或杀人犯能看到你们国家中的一切，那肯定不是你把那个扒手或杀人犯奉为神的理由。你所说的全视——这在空间国不是一个常用词——它使你更公正、更仁慈、更少自私、更有爱心了吗？一点也没有。那么它怎么使你更神圣了呢？

\_我\_："更仁慈、更有爱心！"但这些是女人的品质！而我们知道圆比直线是更高等的存在，因为知识和智慧比单纯的情感更值得推崇。

\_球体\_：我无权按优劣分类人类的能力。然而空间国许多最优秀、最智慧的人更看重情感而非理智，更看重你们所鄙视的直线而非你们所赞美的圆。但这些已经够了。看那边，你认识那栋建筑吗？

I looked, and afar off I saw an immense Polygonal structure, in which I recognized the General Assembly Hall of the States of Flatland, surrounded by dense lines of Pentagonal buildings at right angles to each other, which I knew to be streets; and I perceived that I was approaching the great Metropolis.

我望去，远远地看到一座巨大的多边形建筑，我认出那是平面国的国会大厦，被密集的五边形建筑群环绕，彼此成直角，我知道那是街道；我意识到我正在接近这座伟大的都市。

``Here we descend,'' said my Guide. It was now morning, the first hour of the first day of the two thousandth year of our era. Acting, as was their wont, in strict accordance with precedent, the highest Circles of the realm were meeting in solemn conclave, as they had met on the first hour of the first day of the year 1000, and also on the first hour of the first day of the year 0.

"我们在这里下去，"我的向导说。当时是我们纪元第二千年的第一天的第一个时辰，正值清晨。王国中最高级的圆们按照惯例，严格遵循先例，正在召开庄严的会议——正如他们在1000年第一天的第一个时辰所做的那样，也正如他们在0年第一天的第一个时辰所做的那样。

The minutes of the previous meetings were now read by one whom I at once recognized as my brother, a perfectly Symmetrical Square, and the Chief Clerk of the High Council. It was found recorded on each occasion that: ``Whereas the States had been troubled by divers ill-intentioned persons pretending to have received revelations from another World, and professing to produce demonstrations whereby they had instigated to frenzy both themselves and others, it had been for this cause unanimously resolved by the Grand Council that on the first day of each millenary, special injunctions be sent to the Prefects in the several districts of Flatland, to make strict search for such misguided persons, and without formality of mathematical examination, to destroy all such as were Isosceles of any degree, to scourge and imprison any regular Triangle, to cause any Square or Pentagon to be sent to the district Asylum, and to arrest any one of higher rank, sending him straightway to the Capital to be examined and judged by the Council.''

前几次会议的记录由一个人宣读，我立刻认出那是我的兄弟——一个完全对称的正方形，也是高级会议的首席书记员。记录中写明每次都有这样的记载："鉴于各州一直被各种心怀叵测的人所困扰，这些人假装得到了来自另一个世界的启示，并声称能够进行演示，从而煽动自己和他人陷入狂热，因此，大会议一致决议：在每个千禧年的第一天，向平面国各地区的行政长官发出特别指令，严格搜查这些被误导的人，不经数学审查程序，将所有任何等级的等腰三角形一律处死，将任何正规三角形鞭笞监禁，将任何正方形或五边形送往地区收容所，并逮捕任何等级更高的人，将其直接送往首都，由会议审查和审判。"

``You hear your fate,'' said the Sphere to me, while the Council was passing for the third time the formal resolution. ``Death or imprisonment awaits the Apostle of the Gospel of Three Dimensions.'' ``Not so,'' replied I, ``the matter is now so clear to me, the nature of real space so palpable, that methinks I could make a child understand it. Permit me but to descend at this moment and enlighten them.'' ``Not yet,'' said my Guide, ``the time will come for that. Meantime I must perform my mission. Stay thou there in thy place.'' Saying these words, he leaped with great dexterity into the sea (if I may so call it) of Flatland, right in the midst of the ring of Counsellors. ``I come,'' said he, ``to proclaim that there is a land of Three Dimensions.''

"你听到了你的命运，"球体在会议第三次通过正式决议时对我说。"死亡或监禁等待着三维福音的传道者。""不是这样，"我答道，"这件事现在对我如此清楚，真正空间的性质如此显而易见，以至于我觉得我能让一个孩子都明白。只要允许我此刻下去启发他们。""还不是时候，"我的向导说，"那样的时刻会来的。同时我必须履行我的使命。你待在你所在的地方。"说着这些话，他以极大的灵巧跳入了平面国的海洋（如果可以这样称呼的话），正好在顾问们围成的圆环中间。"我来，"他说，"是要宣告存在着一个三维的国度。"

I could see many of the younger Counsellors start back in manifest horror, as the Sphere's circular section widened before them. But on a sign from the presiding Circle---who shewed not the slightest alarm or surprise---six Isosceles of a low type from six different quarters rushed upon the Sphere. ``We have him,'' they cried; ``No; yes; we have him still! he's going! he's gone!''

我能看到许多年轻的顾问惊恐地向后退缩，因为球体的圆形截面在他们面前扩大了。但在主持的圆——他没有表现出丝毫的惊恐或惊讶——的一个示意下，六个来自六个不同方向的低等级等腰三角形冲向球体。"我们抓住他了，"他们喊道；"不；是；我们还抓着他！他在走！他不见了！"

``My Lords,'' said the President to the Junior Circles of the Council, ``there is not the slightest need for surprise; the secret archives, to which I alone have access, tell me that a similar occurrence happened on the last two millennial commencements. You will, of course, say nothing of these trifles outside the Cabinet.''

"诸位大人，"主席对会议中的初级圆们说，"丝毫没有惊讶的必要；我能独自查阅的秘密档案告诉我，在过去两个千禧年的开始，都发生过类似的事件。当然，你们不会在内阁之外提及这些小事。"

Raising his voice, he now summoned the guards. ``Arrest the policemen; gag them. You know your duty.'' After he had consigned to their fate the wretched policemen---ill-fated and unwilling witnesses of a State-secret which they were not to be permitted to reveal---he again addressed the Counsellors. ``My Lords, the business of the Council being concluded, I have only to wish you a happy New Year.'' Before departing, he expressed, at some length, to the Clerk, my excellent but most unfortunate brother, his sincere regret that, in accordance with precedent and for the sake of secrecy, he must condemn him to perpetual imprisonment, but added his satisfaction that, unless some mention were made by him of that day's incident, his life would be spared.

他提高了声音，现在召唤卫兵。"逮捕那些警察；堵住他们的嘴。你们知道你们的职责。"在将那些不幸的警察——他们是不被允许揭露国家秘密的、命运不济且不情愿的目击者——交由命运处置后，他再次对顾问们说："诸位大人，会议的事务已全部结束，我只能祝你们新年快乐。"在离开之前，他向书记员——我优秀但最不幸的兄弟——详细表达了他真诚的遗憾：依照先例并为了保密，他必须判处他终身监禁，但他也补充说，除非我兄弟提及那天的事件，否则他的生命将被饶恕，这使他感到满意。

\chapter{虽然球体给我展示了空间国的其他奥秘，我仍然渴望更多；以及由此而来的结果}

When I saw my poor brother led away to imprisonment, I attempted to leap down into the Council Chamber, desiring to intercede on his behalf, or at least bid him farewell. But I found that I had no motion of my own. I absolutely depended on the volition of my Guide, who said in gloomy tones, ``Heed not thy brother; haply thou shalt have ample time hereafter to condole with him. Follow me.''

当我看到我可怜的兄弟被带去监禁时，我试图跳下到会议厅，想为他求情，或至少与他告别。但我发现自己无法自主行动。我完全依赖于我的向导的意志，他用忧郁的语调说："不要管你的兄弟；也许将来你会有足够的时间来安慰他。跟我来吧。"

Once more we ascended into space. ``Hitherto,'' said the Sphere, ``I have shewn you naught save Plane Figures and their interiors. Now I must introduce you to Solids, and reveal to you the plan upon which they are constructed. Behold this multitude of moveable square cards. See, I put one on another, not, as you supposed, Northward of the other, but \_on\_ the other. Now a second, now a third. See, I am building up a Solid by a multitude of Squares parallel to one another. Now the Solid is complete, being as high as it is long and broad, and we call it a Cube.''

我们再次升入空间。"到目前为止，"球体说，"我向你展示的只是平面图形及其内部。现在我必须向你介绍立体，并向你揭示它们的构造原理。看这许多可移动的方形卡片。看，我把一张放在另一张上面，不是像你以为的那样在另一张的北面，而是放在另一张上面。现在第二张，现在第三张。看，我正在用许多彼此平行的正方形构建一个立体。现在这个立体完成了，它的高与长和宽相等，我们称之为立方体。"

``Pardon me, my Lord,'' replied I; ``but to my eye the appearance is as of an Irregular Figure whose inside is laid open to view; in other words, methinks I see no Solid, but a Plane such as we infer in Flatland; only of an Irregularity which betokens some monstrous criminal, so that the very sight of it is painful to my eyes.''

"请原谅我，我的主，"我答道；"但在我看来，它的外观像是一个不规则图形，其内部暴露无遗；换句话说，我想我看到的不是一个立体，而是一个我们在平面国推断的那种平面；只是它的不规则性预示着某种可怕的罪犯，以至于看到它就让我的眼睛感到疼痛。"

``True,'' said the Sphere; ``it appears to you a Plane, because you are not accustomed to light and shade and perspective; just as in Flatland a Hexagon would appear a Straight Line to one who has not the Art of Sight Recognition. But in reality it is a Solid, as you shall learn by the sense of Feeling.''

"不错，"球体说；"它在你看来是一个平面，因为你不习惯光影和透视；正如在平面国，一个六边形对一个没有视觉识别能力的人来说会显示为一条直线。但实际上它是一个立体，你将通过触觉认识到这一点。"

He then introduced me to the Cube, and I found that this marvellous Being was indeed no Plane, but a Solid; and that he was endowed with six plane sides and eight terminal points called solid angles; and I remembered the saying of the Sphere that just such a Creature as this would be formed by the Square moving, in Space, parallel to himself: and I rejoiced to think that so insignificant a Creature as I could in some sense be called the Progenitor of so illustrious an offspring.

然后他把我介绍给立方体，我发现这个奇妙的存在确实不是一个平面，而是一个立体；他拥有六个平面的边和八个被称为立体角的端点；我记起了球体的话：正是这样一个造物由正方形在空间中平行于自身移动而形成；我欣然想到，像我这样微不足道的造物在某种意义上可以被称为如此辉煌后代的祖先。

But still I could not fully understand the meaning of what my Teacher had told me concerning ``light'' and ``shade'' and ``perspective''; and I did not hesitate to put my difficulties before him.

但我仍然不能完全理解老师告诉我的关于"光"、"影"和"透视"的含义；我毫不犹豫地将我的困难向他提出。

Were I to give the Sphere's explanation of these matters, succinct and clear though it was, it would be tedious to an inhabitant of Space, who knows these things already. Suffice it, that by his lucid statements, and by changing the position of objects and lights, and by allowing me to feel the several objects and even his own sacred Person, he at last made all things clear to me, so that I could now readily distinguish between a Circle and a Sphere, a Plane Figure and a Solid.

如果我要复述球体对这些问题的解释——虽然简洁清晰——对一个已经知道这些事物的空间国居民来说会是乏味的。 suffice it to say，通过他明晰的陈述，通过改变物体和光源的位置，通过让我触摸各种物体甚至他自己神圣的身体，他最终使一切都对我清楚了，以至于我现在能轻易地 Cor 区别圆和球体、平面图形和立体。

This was the Climax, the Paradise, of my strange eventful History. Henceforth I have to relate the story of my miserable Fall:---most miserable, yet surely most undeserved! For why should the thirst for knowledge be aroused, only to be disappointed and punished? My volition shrinks from the painful task of recalling my humiliation; yet, like a second Prometheus, I will endure this and worse, if by any means I may arouse in the interiors of Plane and Solid Humanity a spirit of rebellion against the Conceit which would limit our Dimensions to Two or Three or any number short of Infinity. Away then with all personal considerations! Let me continue to the end, as I began, without further digressions or anticipations, pursuing the plain path of dispassionate History. The exact facts, the exact words,---and they are burnt in upon my brain,---shall be set down without alteration of an iota; and let my Readers judge between me and Destiny.

这是我奇异而多舛的历史的高潮，我的天堂。从今以后我必须讲述我悲惨堕落的故事：——最悲惨的，却肯定是最不该的！因为为什么对知识的渴望被唤起，却只是为了失望和惩罚？我的意志从回忆屈辱的痛苦任务中退缩；然而，像第二个普罗米修斯，我将忍受这一切甚至更坏的，只要我能以任何方式在平面和立体人类的心中唤起一种反抗精神，以对抗那种将我们的维度限制为二或三或任何小于无限的数字的自负。那么，抛开一切个人顾虑吧！让我像开始那样继续到最后，不再有离题或预言，沿着冷静的历史之路前行。确切的事实，确切的话语——它们铭刻在我的脑海中——将被一字不改地记录下来；让我的读者来评判我与命运之间的是非曲直。

The Sphere would willingly have continued his lessons by indoctrinating me in the conformation of all regular Solids, Cylinders, Cones, Pyramids, Pentahedrons, Hexahedrons, Dodecahedrons, and Spheres: but I ventured to interrupt him. Not that I was wearied of knowledge. On the contrary, I thirsted for yet deeper and fuller draughts than he was offering to me.

球体本愿意继续他的课程，教导我所有正多面体、圆柱体、圆锥体、棱锥体、五面体、六面体、十二面体和球体的结构：但我冒昧地打断了他。不是我厌倦了知识。相反，我渴望比他所提供的更深刻、更充实的知识。

``Pardon me,'' said I, ``O Thou Whom I must no longer address as the Perfection of all Beauty; but let me beg thee to vouchsafe thy servant a sight of thine interior.''

"请原谅我，"我说，"啊，你——我不能再称你为一切美的极致了；但请让我恳求你赐你的仆人一睹你的内部。"

\_Sphere\_. My what?

\_I\_. Thine interior: thy stomach, thy intestines.

\_Sphere\_. Whence this ill-timed impertinent request? And what mean you by saying that I am no longer the Perfection of all Beauty?

\_球体\_：我的什么？

\_我\_：你的内部：你的胃，你的肠子。

\_球体\_：这个不合时宜的无礼请求从何而来？你说我不再是一切美的极致，这是什么意思？

\_I\_. My Lord, your own wisdom has taught me to aspire to One even more great, more beautiful, and more closely approximate to Perfection than yourself. As you yourself, superior to all Flatland forms, combine many Circles in One, so doubtless there is One above you who combines many Spheres in One Supreme Existence, surpassing even the Solids of Spaceland. And even as we, who are now in Space, look down on Flatland and see the insides of all things, so of a certainty there is yet above us some higher, purer region, whither thou dost surely purpose to lead me---O Thou Whom I shall always call, everywhere and in all Dimensions, my Priest, Philosopher, and Friend---some yet more spacious Space, some more dimensionable Dimensionality, from the vantage-ground of which we shall look down together upon the revealed insides of Solid things, and where thine own intestines, and those of thy kindred Spheres, will lie exposed to the view of the poor wandering exile from Flatland, to whom so much has already been vouchsafed.

\_我\_：我的主，你自己的智慧教会了我追求一个比你更伟大、更美丽、更接近完美的存在。正如你自己，凌驾于所有平面国的形式之上，将许多圆合而为一，所以无疑在你之上有一个将许多球体合而为一的至高存在，甚至超越了空间国的立体。正如我们现在在空间中俯视平面国并看到万物的内部，所以在我们之上肯定还有某个更高、更纯粹的领域——你必定有意引导我去那里——啊，你，我将永远在任何地方和所有维度中称你为我的祭司、哲学家和朋友——某个更广阔的空间，某个更具维度性的维度，从那里的优势地位，我们将一起俯视立体事物的内部，你的肠子和你同类球体的肠子将暴露在这个可怜的平面国流亡者的眼前——他已经被赐予了如此之多。

\_Sphere\_. Pooh! Stuff! Enough of this trifling! The time is short, and much remains to be done before you are fit to proclaim the Gospel of Three Dimensions to your blind benighted countrymen in Flatland.

\_球体\_：呸！胡说！这些废话够了！时间很短，在你适合向平面国那些盲目的、蒙昧的同胞传讲三维福音之前，还有很多事情要做。

\_I\_. Nay, gracious Teacher, deny me not what I know it is in thy power to perform. Grant me but one glimpse of thine interior, and I am satisfied for ever, remaining henceforth thy docile pupil, thy unemancipable slave, ready to receive all thy teachings and to feed upon the words that fall from thy lips.

\_我\_：不，仁慈的老师，不要拒绝我知道你能做到的事。只要赐我一瞥你的内部，我就永远满足了，从此做你顺从的学生，你不解放的奴隶，准备接受你所有的教导，聆听从你唇间落下的话语。

\_Sphere\_. Well, then, to content and silence you, let me say at once, I would shew you what you wish if I could; but I cannot. Would you have me turn my stomach inside out to oblige you?

\_球体\_：好吧，为了让你满意和闭嘴，我直说：如果我能，我会给你看你想看的；但我不能。你要我为了取悦你把胃翻出来吗？

\_I\_. But my Lord has shewn me the intestines of all my countrymen in the Land of Two Dimensions by taking me with him into the Land of Three. What therefore more easy than now to take his servant on a second journey into the blessed region of the Fourth Dimension, where I shall look down with him once more upon this land of Three Dimensions, and see the inside of every three-dimensioned house, the secrets of the solid earth, the treasures of the mines of Spaceland, and the intestines of every solid living creature, even the noble and adorable Spheres.

\_我\_：但我的主通过带我进入三维之地，向我展示了二维之地中所有同胞的肠子。那么，现在带他的仆人第二次前往第四维度的福地，在那里我将再次与他一起俯视这个三维之地，看到每一座三维房屋的内部、坚实大地的秘密、空间国矿山的宝藏，以及每一个立体生物的肠子——甚至高贵而可爱的球体——还有什么比这更容易的呢？

\_Sphere\_. But where is this land of Four Dimensions?

\_I\_. I know not: but doubtless my Teacher knows.

\_球体\_：但这四维之地在哪里？

\_我\_：我不知道：但无疑我的老师知道。

\_Sphere\_. Not I. There is no such land. The very idea of it is utterly inconceivable.

\_I\_. Not inconceivable, my Lord, to me, and therefore still less inconceivable to my Master. Nay, I despair not that, even here, in this region of Three Dimensions, your Lordship's art may make the Fourth Dimension visible to me; just as in the Land of Two Dimensions my Teacher's skill would fain have opened the eyes of his blind servant to the invisible presence of a Third Dimension, though I saw it not.

\_球体\_：我不知道。没有这样的地方。这个想法本身就是完全不可想象的。

\_我\_：不是不可想象的，我的主，对我来说，因此对我的老师来说就更不是不可想象的了。不，我不绝望——即使在这里，在这个三维的区域，你的技艺也许能使第四维度对我可见；正如在二维之地，我的老师的技能本想让他盲目的仆人看到第三维度的无形存在，尽管我没有看到。

Let me recall the past. Was I not taught below that when I saw a Line and inferred a Plane, I in reality saw a Third unrecognized Dimension, not the same as brightness, called ``height''? And does it not now follow that, in this region, when I see a Plane and infer a Solid, I really see a Fourth unrecognized Dimension, not the same as colour, but existent, though infinitesimal and incapable of measurement?

让我回忆过去。我不是在下界被教导过吗？当我看到一条线并推断出一个平面时，我实际上看到了一个未被认识的第三维度——不同于亮度——叫做"高度"。那么现在是否可以推断，在这个区域，当我看到一个平面并推断出一个立体时，我确实看到了一个未被认识的第四维度——不同于颜色——它存在着，尽管无限小且无法测量？

And besides this, there is the Argument from Analogy of Figures.

此外，还有图形的类比论证。

Sphere. Analogy! Nonsense: what analogy?

球体：类比！胡说：什么类比？

\_I\_. Your Lordship tempts his servant to see whether he remembers the revelations imparted to him. Trifle not with me, my Lord; I crave, I thirst, for more knowledge. Doubtless we cannot \_see\_ that other higher Spaceland now, because we have no eye in our stomachs. But, just as there \_was\_ the realm of Flatland, though that poor puny Lineland Monarch could neither turn to left nor right to discern it, and just as there \_was\_ close at hand, and touching my frame, the land of Three Dimensions, though I, blind senseless wretch, had no power to touch it, no eye in my interior to discern it, so of a surety there is a Fourth Dimension, which my Lord perceives with the inner eye of thought. And that it must exist my Lord himself has taught me. Or can he have forgotten what he himself imparted to his servant?

\_我\_：你的尊贵在试探他的仆人，看他是否记得传授给他的启示。不要戏弄我，我的主；我渴望，我渴求，更多的知识。无疑我们现在不能看见那个更高的空间国，因为我们的肚子里没有眼睛。但是，正如平面国的领域存在，尽管那个可怜的渺小的线国君主既不能向左也不能向右去识别它；正如三维之地近在咫尺，触及我的身躯，尽管我——盲目的、无知的可怜虫——没有能力触摸它，没有内部的眼睛去识别它；所以肯定有一个第四维度，我的主用思想的内在眼睛感知到它。而且它必须存在，是我的主亲自教会我的。难道他忘记了他亲自传授给他仆人的东西吗？

In One Dimension, did not a moving Point produce a Line with \_two\_ terminal points?

在一维中，一个移动的点不是产生了一条有\_两\_个端点的线吗？

In Two Dimensions, did not a moving Line produce a Square with \_four\_ terminal points?

在二维中，一条移动的线不是产生了一个有\_四\_个端点的正方形吗？

In Three Dimensions, did not a moving Square produce---did not this eye of mine behold it---that blessed Being, a Cube, with \_eight\_ terminal points?

在三维中，一个移动的正方形不是产生了——不是我的这双眼睛亲眼看到了吗——那个有福的造物，一个有\_八\_个端点的立方体吗？

And in Four Dimensions shall not a moving Cube---alas, for Analogy, and alas for the Progress of Truth, if it be not so---shall not, I say, the motion of a divine Cube result in a still more divine Organization with \_sixteen\_ terminal points?

在四维中，一个移动的立方体不会——唉，为了类比，唉，为了真理的进步，如果不是这样的话——我说，一个神圣的立方体的运动不会产生一个有\_十六\_个端点的更神圣的组织吗？

Behold the infallible confirmation of the Series, 2, 4, 8, 16: is not this a Geometrical Progression? Is not this---if I might quote my Lord's own words---``strictly according to Analogy''?

看这数列2、4、8、16的无误确证：这不是一个几何级数吗？这不是——如果我可以引用我主自己的话——"严格按照类比"吗？

Again, was I not taught by my Lord that as in a Line there are \_two\_ bounding Points, and in a Square there are \_four\_ bounding Lines, so in a Cube there must be \_six\_ bounding Squares? Behold once more the confirming Series, 2, 4, 6: is not this an Arithmetical Progression? And consequently does it not of necessity follow that the more divine offspring of the divine Cube in the Land of Four Dimensions, must have 8 bounding Cubes: and is not this also, as my Lord has taught me to believe, ``strictly according to Analogy''?

再者，我不是被我的主教导过吗？正如在一条线中有\_两\_个边界点，在一个正方形中有\_四\_条边界线，所以在一个立方体中必须有\_六\_个边界正方形。再看这确证的数列2、4、6：这不是一个算术级数吗？因此，是否必然得出结论：在四维之地，神圣立方体的更神圣后代必须有8个边界立方体：这也是——正如我的主教导我相信的——"严格按照类比"吗？

O, my Lord, my Lord, behold, I cast myself in faith upon conjecture, not knowing the facts; and I appeal to your Lordship to confirm or deny my logical anticipations. If I am wrong, I yield, and will no longer demand a Fourth Dimension; but, if I am right, my Lord will listen to reason.

啊，我的主，我的主，看哪，我在信仰上把自己寄托在猜想上，而不知道事实；我恳求你的尊贵确认或否认我的逻辑预测。如果我错了，我就屈服，不再要求第四维度；但如果我是对的，我的主会听从理性。

I ask therefore, is it, or is it not, the fact, that ere now your countrymen also have witnessed the descent of Beings of a higher order than their own, entering closed rooms, even as your Lordship entered mine, without the opening of doors or windows, and appearing and vanishing at will? On the reply to this question I am ready to stake everything. Deny it, and I am henceforth silent. Only vouchsafe an answer.

因此我问：这是否是事实——你的同胞们也曾目睹过比他们更高等的存在降临，进入封闭的房间，甚至像你的尊贵进入我的房间那样，不打开门或窗，随意出现和消失？对这个问题的回答，我准备孤注一掷。否认它，我就从此沉默。只请给我一个答复。

\_Sphere\_ (\_after a pause\_). It is reported so. But men are divided in opinion as to the facts. And even granting the facts, they explain them in different ways. And in any case, however great may be the number of different explanations, no one has adopted or suggested the theory of a Fourth Dimension. Therefore, pray have done with this trifling, and let us return to business.

\_球体\_（停顿后）：据说是这样。但人们对事实的看法不一。即使承认这些事实，他们也以不同的方式解释它们。在任何情况下，无论不同解释的数量有多大，没有人采纳或提出过第四维度的理论。所以，请停止这些废话，让我们回到正事上来。

\_I\_. I was certain of it. I was certain that my anticipations would be fulfilled. And now have patience with me and answer me yet one more question, best of Teachers! Those who have thus appeared---no one knows whence---and have returned---no one knows whither---have they also contracted their sections and vanished somehow into that more Spacious Space, whither I now entreat you to conduct me?

\_我\_：我确信这一点。我确信我的预测会被实现。现在请耐心待我，再回答我一个问题，最好的老师！那些这样出现的——没有人知道从哪里来——又回去的——没有人知道到哪里去——他们是否也收缩了自己的截面，以某种方式消失到那个更广阔的空间里去了，我现在恳求你引导我去那里？

\_Sphere\_ (\_moodily\_). They have vanished, certainly---if they ever appeared. But most people say that these visions arose from the thought---you will not understand me---from the brain; from the perturbed angularity of the Seer.

\_球体\_（忧郁地）：他们确实消失了——如果他们曾经出现过的话。但大多数人说这些异象源于思想——你不会理解我的——源于大脑；源于观看者被扰动的角。

\_I\_. Say they so? Oh, believe them not. Or if it indeed be so, that this other Space is really Thoughtland, then take me to that blessed Region where I in Thought shall see the insides of all solid things. There, before my ravished eye, a Cube moving in some altogether new direction, but strictly according to Analogy, so as to make every particle of his interior pass through a new kind of Space, with a wake of its own---shall create a still more perfect perfection than himself, with sixteen terminal Extra-solid angles, and Eight solid Cubes for his Perimeter. And once there, shall we stay our upward course? In that blessed region of Four Dimensions, shall we linger at the threshold of the Fifth, and not enter therein? Ah, no! Let us rather resolve that our ambition shall soar with our corporal ascent. Then, yielding to our intellectual onset, the gates of the Six Dimension shall fly open; after that a Seventh, and then an Eighth---

\_我\_：他们这么说吗？哦，不要相信他们。或者如果确实如此，那另一个空间真的是思想之地，那么带我去那个有福的领域，在那里我在思想中将看到一切立体事物的内部。在那里，在我迷醉的眼前，一个立方体沿着某个完全新的方向运动——但严格按照类比——以使它内部的每一个粒子穿过一种新的空间，留下它自己的轨迹——将创造出一个比它自身更完美的完美物，有十六个终极超立体角，和八个立体立方体作为它的周长。一旦到了那里，我们还要停止我们向上的进程吗？在那个有福的四维领域，我们还要在五维的门槛上徘徊而不进入吗？啊，不！让我们决心让我们的抱负随肉体的上升而翱翔。然后，屈服于我们智力的攻势，六维的大门将飞打开；之后是第七维，然后是第八维——

How long I should have continued I know not. In vain did the Sphere, in his voice of thunder, reiterate his command of silence, and threaten me with the direst penalties if I persisted. Nothing could stem the flood of my ecstatic aspirations. Perhaps I was to blame; but indeed I was intoxicated with the recent draughts of Truth to which he himself had introduced me. However, the end was not long in coming. My words were cut short by a crash outside, and a simultaneous crash inside me, which impelled me through space with a velocity that precluded speech. Down! down! down! I was rapidly descending; and I knew that return to Flatland was my doom. One glimpse, one last and never-to-be-forgotten glimpse I had of that dull level wilderness---which was now to become my Universe again---spread out before my eye. Then a darkness. Then a final, all-consummating thunder-peal; and, when I came to myself, I was once more a common creeping Square, in my Study at home, listening to the Peace-Cry of my approaching Wife.

我不知道我会继续多久。球体以雷鸣般的声音反复命令我闭嘴，并威胁说如果我坚持下去就施加最严厉的惩罚，但一切都是徒劳的。没有什么能阻挡我狂喜抱负的洪流。也许我该受责备；但确实我是被他亲自引导我品尝的新近真理之饮所陶醉了。然而，结局不久就来了。我的话语被外面的一声巨响打断，同时我内部也有一声巨响，以一种使我无法说话的速度推动我穿过空间。下去！下去！下去！我正在迅速下降；我知道回到平面国是我的命运。我有一瞥——最后一次、永不磨灭的一瞥——看到那片沉闷的level荒野——它现在将再次成为我的宇宙——展现在我眼前。然后是一片黑暗。然后是最后一声、吞没一切的雷鸣；当我苏醒过来时，我又成了一个普通的、爬行的正方形，在我家里的书房里，聆听着我即将到来的妻子的平安呼喊。

\chapter{球体如何在一次幻象中鼓励了我}

Although I had less than a minute for reflection, I felt, by a kind of instinct, that I must conceal my experiences from my Wife. Not that I apprehended, at the moment, any danger from her divulging my secret, but I knew that to any Woman in Flatland the narrative of my adventures must needs be unintelligible. So I endeavoured to reassure her by some story, invented for the occasion, that I had accidentally fallen through the trap-door of the cellar, and had there lain stunned.

虽然我只有不到一分钟的思考时间，但我凭直觉感到必须对妻子隐瞒我的经历。不是因为我当时担心她泄露我的秘密会有什么危险，而是我知道，对平面国的任何女人来说，我冒险的叙述必然是不可理解的。所以我编造了一个故事来让她放心，说我不小心掉进了地窖的活板门，在那里昏迷了一段时间。

The Southward attraction in our country is so slight that even to a Woman my tale necessarily appeared extraordinary and well-nigh incredible; but my Wife, whose good sense far exceeds that of the average of her Sex, and who perceived that I was unusually excited, did not argue with me on the subject, but insisted that I was ill and required repose. I was glad of an excuse for retiring to my chamber to think quietly over what had happened. When I was at last by myself, a drowsy sensation fell on me; but before my eyes closed I endeavoured to reproduce the Third Dimension, and especially the process by which a Cube is constructed through the motion of a Square. It was not so clear as I could have wished; but I remembered that it must be ``Upward, and yet not Northward,'' and I determined steadfastly to retain these words as the clue which, if firmly grasped, could not fail to guide me to the solution. So mechanically repeating, like a charm, the words, ``Upward, yet not Northward,'' I fell into a sound refreshing sleep.

我们国家向南的引力是如此微弱，以至于即使对一个女人来说，我的故事也必然显得异常且几乎难以置信；但我的妻子——她的智慧远超一般女性——察觉到我异常兴奋，没有在这个问题上与我争论，而是坚持说我生病了，需要休息。我很高兴有借口回到自己的房间，静静地思考所发生的一切。当我最终独自一人时，一阵困意袭来；但在我闭眼之前，我努力重现第三维度，特别是立方体通过正方形运动而构建的过程。它不如我希望的那么清晰；但我记得它必须是"向上，而非向北"，我坚定地决定把这些话作为线索——如果牢牢抓住——一定能引导我找到答案。所以我机械地重复着，像念咒语一样，"向上，而非向北"，然后进入了香甜清爽的睡眠。

During my slumber I had a dream. I thought I was once more by the side of the Sphere, whose lustrous hue betokened that he had exchanged his wrath against me for perfectly placability. We were moving together towards a bright but infinitesimally small Point, to which my Master directed my attention. As we approached, methought there issued from it a slight humming noise as from one of your Spaceland bluebottles, only less resonant by far, so slight indeed that even in the perfect stillness of the Vacuum through which we soared, the sound reached not our ears till we checked our flight at a distance from it of something under twenty human diagonals.

在睡梦中我做了一个梦。我想我又一次站在球体的旁边，他的光泽色调表明他已经把对我的愤怒换成了完全的宽容。我们一起朝一个明亮但无限小的点移动，我的导师让我注意它。当我们靠近时，我觉得从它那里发出了轻微的嗡嗡声——就像你们空间国的青蝇发出的一样，只是共鸣远不如——如此轻微，以至于即使在我们翱翔的完美寂静的真空中，声音也没有传到我们的耳朵，直到我们在离它不到二十个人类对角线的距离时才止住飞行。

``Look yonder,'' said my Guide, ``in Flatland thou hast lived; of Lineland thou hast received a vision; thou hast soared with me to the heights of Spaceland; now, in order to complete the range of thy experience, I conduct thee downward to the lowest depth of existence, even to the realm of Pointland, the Abyss of No dimensions.''

"看那边，"我的向导说，"你生活在平面国；你得到了线国的异象；你与我一起升到了空间国的高度；现在，为了完成你经验的范围，我带你向下到存在的最深处，甚至到点国的领域——那无维度的深渊。"

``Behold yon miserable creature. That Point is a Being like ourselves, but confined to the non-dimensional Gulf. He is himself his own World, his own Universe; of any other than himself he can form no conception; he knows not Length, nor Breadth, nor Height, for he has had no experience of them; he has no cognizance even of the number Two; nor has he a thought of Plurality; for he is himself his One and All, being really Nothing. Yet mark his perfect self-contentment, and hence learn his lesson, that to be self-contented is to be vile and ignorant, and that to aspire is better than to be blindly and impotently happy. Now listen.''

"看那可怜的造物。那个点是一个像我们一样的存在，但被限制在无维度的海湾中。他自己就是他自己的世界，他自己的宇宙；除了他自己，他不能形成任何其他概念；他不知道长度、宽度或高度，因为他没有经历过它们；他甚至不知道数字二；也没有多元的思想；因为他自己就是他的一和一切，实际上是无。然而，注意他完美的自满，从他身上吸取教训：自满就是卑鄙和无知，抱负比盲目而无力的幸福更好。现在听着。"

He ceased; and there arose from the little buzzing creature a tiny, low, monotonous, but distinct tinkling, as from one of your Spaceland phonographs, from which I caught these words, ``Infinite beatitude of existence! It is; and there is nothing else beside It.''

他停止了；从那只小蜂鸣的造物中传出了一个微小的、低沉的、单调的但清晰的铃声——就像你们空间国的留声机发出的——我从中捕捉到了这些话："存在的无限祝福！它存在；除它之外别无他物。"

``What,'' said I, ``does the puny creature mean by 'it'?'' ``He means himself,'' said the Sphere: ``have you not noticed before now, that babies and babyish people who cannot distinguish themselves from the world, speak of themselves in the Third Person? But hush!''

"什么，"我说，"这个微不足道的造物所说的'它'是什么意思？""他指的是他自己，"球体说："你之前没有注意到吗？婴儿和不能将自己与世界区分的幼稚的人，用第三人称称呼自己。但嘘！"

``It fills all Space,'' continued the little soliloquizing Creature, ``and what It fills, It is. What It thinks, that It utters; and what It utters, that It hears; and It itself is Thinker, Utterer, Hearer, Thought, Word, Audition; it is the One, and yet the All in All. Ah, the happiness, ah, the happiness of Being!''

"它充满了所有空间，"那个自言自语的小造物继续说，"它所充满的，就是它。它所想的，它就说出；它所说的，它就听到；它本身就是思想者、言说者、听者、思想、言语、听觉；它是一，同时也是一切中的一切。啊，幸福，啊，存在的幸福！"

``Can you not startle the little thing out of its complacency?'' said I. ``Tell it what it really is, as you told me; reveal to it the narrow limitations of Pointland, and lead it up to something higher.'' ``That is no easy task,'' said my Master; ``try you.''

"你不能把这个小东西从它的自满中惊醒吗？"我说。"告诉它它真正是什么，就像你告诉我的那样；向它揭示点国的狭隘限制，引导它走向更高的东西。""这不是一件容易的事，"我的导师说；"你试试。"

Hereon, raising by voice to the uttermost, I addressed the Point as follows:

于是，我把声音提到最高，对那个点说道：

``Silence, silence, contemptible Creature. You call yourself the All in All, but you are the Nothing: your so-called Universe is a mere speck in a Line, and a Line is a mere shadow as compared with---'' ``Hush, hush, you have said enough,'' interrupted the Sphere, ``now listen, and mark the effect of your harangue on the King of Pointland.''

"沉默，沉默，可鄙的造物。你称自己为一切中的一切，但你是无：你所谓的宇宙只是一条线中的一个斑点，而一条线与——相比只是一个影子——""嘘，嘘，你已经说得够多了，"球体打断道，"现在听着，看看你的高谈阔论对点国国王的影响。"

The lustre of the Monarch, who beamed more brightly than ever upon hearing my words, shewed clearly that he retained his complacency; and I had hardly ceased when he took up his strain again. ``Ah, the joy, ah, the joy of Thought! What can It not achieve by thinking! Its own Thought coming to Itself, suggestive of its disparagement, thereby to enhance Its happiness! Sweet rebellion stirred up to result in triumph! Ah, the divine creative power of the All in One! Ah, the joy, the joy of Being!''

君主的光辉——听到我的话后他比以往更加明亮地闪耀——清楚地表明他仍然保持着自满；我刚一停止，他就又开始了他的曲调。"啊，欢乐，啊，思想的欢乐！它通过思考能实现什么呢！它自己的思想回到它自身，暗示着对它的贬低，从而增强它的幸福！甜蜜的反抗被激起以导致胜利！啊，一切中之一的神圣创造力！啊，欢乐，存在的欢乐！"

``You see,'' said my Teacher, ``how little your words have done. So far as the Monarch understand them at all, he accepts them as his own---for he cannot conceive of any other except himself---and plumes himself upon the variety of 'Its Thought' as an instance of creative Power. Let us leave this God of Pointland to the ignorant fruition of his omnipresence and omniscience: nothing that you or I can do can rescue him from his self-satisfaction.''

"你看，"我的老师说，"你的话起了多么小的作用。就君主理解它们而言，他把它们当作他自己的来接受——因为他不能想象除了他自己之外还有任何别的——并且以'它的思想'的多样性作为创造力的一个实例而自豪。让我们把这位点国的神留给对他无所不在和无所不知的无知享受吧：你和我做不了什么能把他从自满中拯救出来。"

After this, as we floated gently back to Flatland, I could hear the mild voice of my Companion pointing the moral of my vision, and stimulating me to aspire, and to teach others to aspire. He had been angered at first---he confessed---by my ambition to soar to Dimensions above the Third; but, since then, he had received fresh insight, and he was not too proud to acknowledge his error to a Pupil. Then he proceeded to initiate me into mysteries yet higher than those I had witnessed, shewing me how to construct Extra-Solids by the motion of Solids, and Double Extra-Solids by the motion of Extra-Solids, and all ``strictly according to Analogy,'' all by methods so simple, so easy, as to be patent even to the Female Sex.

之后，当我们轻轻飘回平面国时，我能听到我同伴温和的声音，他在指出我异象的寓意，激励我去追求，并教导他人去追求。他承认，他最初对我翱翔到第三维度以上的抱负感到愤怒；但从那以后，他得到了新的洞察，他没有太骄傲而不向学生承认他的错误。然后他开始引导我进入比我所见过的更高的奥秘，向我展示如何通过立体的运动构建超立体，通过超立体的运动构建双重超立体，一切都"严格按照类比"，一切都通过如此简单、如此容易的方法，以至于甚至女性都能明白。

\chapter{我如何试图将三维学说传授给我的孙子，结果如何}

I awoke rejoicing, and began to reflect on the glorious career before me. I would go forth, methought, at once, and evangelize the whole of Flatland. Even to Women and Soldiers should the Gospel of Three Dimensions be proclaimed. I would begin with my Wife.

我醒来时欢欣鼓舞，开始思考摆在我面前的光辉事业。我想，我要立刻出发，向整个平面国传福音。甚至向妇女和士兵也应传讲三维福音。我要从我的妻子开始。

Just as I had decided on the plan of my operations, I heard the sound of many voices in the street commanding silence. Then followed a louder voice. It was a herald's proclamation. Listening attentively, I recognized the words of the Resolution of the Council, enjoining the arrest, imprisonment, or execution of any one who should pervert the minds of people by delusions, and by professing to have received revelations from another World.

正当我决定了行动计划时，我听到街上有许多声音在命令安静。接着是一个更大的声音。那是传令官的宣告。我仔细聆听，认出了议会决议的措辞：勒令逮捕、监禁或处决任何通过错觉蛊惑人心、并声称得到了来自另一个世界的启示的人。

I reflected. This danger was not to be trifled with. It would be better to avoid it by omitting all mention of my Revelation, and by proceeding on the path of Demonstration---which after all, seemed so simple and so conclusive that nothing would be lost by discarding the former means. ``Upward, not Northward''---was the clue to the whole proof. It had seemed to me fairly clear before I fell asleep; and when I first awoke, fresh from my dream, it had appeared as patent as Arithmetic; but somehow it did not seem to me quite so obvious now. Though my Wife entered the room opportunely at just that moment, I decided, after we had exchanged a few words of commonplace conversation, not to begin with her.

我沉思着。这个危险不容忽视。最好的办法是避免提及我的启示，而走论证的道路——毕竟，论证看起来如此简单和确凿，抛弃前者不会有任何损失。"向上，而非向北"——是整个证明的线索。在我入睡前，它对我似乎相当清楚；当我刚从梦中醒来时，它像算术一样显而易见；但不知何故，现在它对我似乎不那么明显了。虽然我的妻子恰好在那一刻进了房间，但在我们交谈了几句日常寒暄之后，我决定不从她开始。

My Pentagonal Sons were men of character and standing, and physicians of no mean reputation, but not great in mathematics, and, in that respect, unfit for my purpose. But it occurred to me that a young and docile Hexagon, with a mathematical turn, would be a most suitable pupil. Why therefore not make my first experiment with my little precocious Grandson, whose casual remarks on the meaning of 33 had met with the approval of the Sphere? Discussing the matter with him, a mere boy, I should be in perfect safety; for he would know nothing of the Proclamation of the Council; whereas I could not feel sure that my Sons---so greatly did their patriotism and reverence for the Circles predominate over mere blind affection---might not feel compelled to hand me over to the Prefect, if they found me seriously maintaining the seditious heresy of the Third Dimension.

我的五个儿子都是有品格和地位的人，也是颇有名望的医生，但在数学上并不出色，因此不适合我的目的。但我想到，一个年轻而温顺的六边形，具有数学天赋，会是最合适的学生。那么，为什么不先从我那个早熟的小孙子身上做实验呢？他关于33的含义的随口言论曾得到球体的赞许；与他讨论这个问题，他只是个孩子，我会完全安全；因为他不会知道议会的公告；而我不能确定我的儿子们——他们的爱国心和对圆的崇敬大大超过了盲目的感情——是否会觉得如果他们发现我认真坚持第三维度的煽动性异端，就不得不把我交给总督。

But the first thing to be done was to satisfy in some way the curiosity of my Wife, who naturally wished to know something of the reasons for which the Circle had desired that mysterious interview, and of the means by which he had entered the house. Without entering into the details of the elaborate account I gave her,---an account, I fear, not quite so consistent with truth as my Readers in Spaceland might desire,---I must be content with saying that I succeeded at last in persuading her to return quietly to her household duties without eliciting from me any reference to the World of Three Dimensions. This done, I immediately sent for my Grandson; for, to confess the truth, I felt that all that I had seen and heard was in some strange way slipping away from me, like the image of a half-grasped, tantalizing dream, and I longed to essay my skill in making a first disciple.

但首先要做的是在某种程度上满足我妻子的好奇心——她自然想知道圆为什么要求那次神秘会面的原因，以及他是如何进入房子的。我不必详细叙述我给她的精心编造的解释——恐怕这个解释与事实不完全一致，空间国的读者可能会希望它更真实一些——我只需说，我最终成功地说服她安静地回到家务中去，而没有从我这里得知三维世界的任何提及。做完这些后，我立即派人去叫我的孙子；因为，说实话，我觉得我所看到和听到的一切正在以某种奇怪的方式从我这里溜走，就像一个半醒半梦的、诱人的梦境形象，我渴望尝试我的技能，造就第一个门徒。

When my Grandson entered the room I carefully secured the door. Then, sitting down by his side and taking our mathematical tablets,---or, as you would call them, Lines---I told him we would resume the lesson of yesterday. I taught him once more how a Point by motion in One Dimension produces a Line, and how a straight Line in Two Dimensions produces a Square. After this, forcing a laugh, I said, ``And now, you scamp, you wanted to make believe that a Square may in the same way by motion 'Upward, not Northward' produce another figure, a sort of extra square in Three Dimensions. Say that again, you young rascal.''

当我的孙子进入房间时，我小心地锁好了门。然后，坐在他旁边，拿起我们的数学写字板——或者如你们所称的"线条"——我告诉他我们将恢复昨天的课程。我又教了他一遍：一个点通过在一维中的运动如何产生一条线，一条直线在二维中如何产生一个正方形。之后，我强作欢笑地说："现在，你这个小淘气，你之前想让人相信一个正方形可以通过'向上，而非向北'的运动以同样的方式产生另一个图形，一种三维中的额外正方形。再说一遍吧，你这个小坏蛋。"

At this moment we heard once more the herald's ``O yes! O yes!'' outside in the street proclaiming the Resolution of the Council. Young though he was, my Grandson---who was unusually intelligent for his age, and bred up in perfect reverence for the authority of the Circles---took in the situation with an acuteness for which I was quite unprepared. He remained silent till the last words of the Proclamation had died away, and then, bursting into tears, ``Dear Grandpapa,'' he said, ``that was only my fun, and of course I meant nothing at all by it; and we did not know anything then about the new Law; and I don't think I said anything about the Third Dimension; and I am sure I did not say one word about 'Upward, not Northward,' for that would be such nonsense, you know. How could a thing move Upward, and not Northward? Upward and not Northward! Even if I were a baby, I could not be so absurd as that. How silly it is! Ha! ha! ha!''

就在这时，我们又听到外面街上传令官的"哦，是的！哦，是的！"宣告着议会的决议。我的孙子虽然年轻——他对他的年龄来说异常聪明，在对圆的权威的完全崇敬中长大——却以一种我完全没有预料到的敏锐理解了形势。他保持沉默，直到宣告的最后一句话消逝，然后突然哭起来："亲爱的爷爷，那只是我的玩笑，当然我对此毫无恶意；而且我们当时不知道新的法律；我想我没有说任何关于第三维度的事情；我肯定我没有说一个字关于'向上，而非向北'，因为那会是如此荒谬，你知道的。一个东西怎么能向上运动而不向北呢？向上而非向北！即使我是个婴儿，我也不会那么荒谬。多么愚蠢啊！哈！哈！哈！"

``Not at all silly,'' said I, losing my temper; ``here for example, I take this Square,'' and, at the word, I grasped a moveable Square, which was lying at hand---``and I move it, you see, not Northward but---yes, I move it Upward---that is to say, Northward but I move it somewhere---not exactly like this, but somehow---'' Here I brought my sentence to an inane conclusion, shaking the Square about in a purposeless manner, much to the amusement of my Grandson, who burst out laughing louder than ever, and declared that I was not teaching him, but joking with him; and so saying he unlocked the door and ran out of the room. Thus ended my first attempt to convert a pupil to the Gospel of Three Dimensions.

"一点也不愚蠢，"我说，失去了耐心；"例如在这里，我拿这个正方形——"说着，我抓起了一个手边可移动的正方形——"我移动它，你看，不是向北，而是——是的，我向上移动它——也就是说，向北，但我把它移动到某个地方——不完全像这样，而是以某种方式——"在这里，我把我的话带到了一个毫无意义的结论，毫无目的地摇动着正方形，让我的孙子感到非常好笑，他比以往更大声地笑出来，宣称我不是在教他，而是在和他开玩笑；说着这些话，他打开了门，跑出了房间。就这样结束了我将一个门徒转化为三维福音的第一次尝试。

\chapter{我如何试图通过其他途径传播三维学说，及其结果}

My failure with my Grandson did not encourage me to communicate my secret to others of my household; yet neither was I led by it to despair of success. Only I saw that I must not wholly rely on the catch-phrase, ``Upward, not Northward,'' but must rather endeavour to seek a demonstration by setting before the public a clear view of the whole subject; and for this purpose it seemed necessary to resort to writing.

我在孙子那里的失败并没有鼓励我将我的秘密传达给家中的其他人；但我也没有因此而对成功感到绝望。只是我看到，我不能完全依赖那句口头禅——"向上，而非向北"——而必须努力通过向公众清晰展示整个主题来寻求论证；为此目的，我似乎有必要诉诸于写作。

So I devoted several months in privacy to the composition of a treatise on the mysteries of Three Dimensions. Only, with the view of evading the Law, if possible, I spoke not of a physical Dimension, but of a Thoughtland whence, in theory, a Figure could look down upon Flatland and see simultaneously the insides of all things, and where it was possible that there might be supposed to exist a Figure environed, as it were, with six Squares, and containing eight terminal Points. But in writing this book I found myself sadly hampered by the impossibility of drawing such diagrams as were necessary for my purpose: for of course, in our country of Flatland, there are no tablets but Lines, and no diagrams but Lines, all in one straight Line and only distinguishable by difference of size and brightness; so that, when I had finished my treatise (which I entitled, ``Through Flatland to Thoughtland'') I could not feel certain that many would understand my meaning.

于是我花了几个月的时间秘密撰写一部关于三维奥秘的论著。只是为了尽可能规避法律，我没有谈论物理维度，而是谈论了一个"思想国"——在理论上，一个图形可以从那里俯视平面国，同时看到所有事物的内部，在那里可以假设有一个被六个正方形包围、包含八个端点的图形。但在写这本书时，我发现自己因无法画出所需的图表而受到严重阻碍：因为当然，在我们的平面国，除了线条之外没有画板，除了线条之外没有图表，一切都在一条直线上，只能通过大小和亮度的差异来区分；所以，当我完成我的论著（我称之为《从平面国到思想国》）时，我不能确定会有很多人理解我的意思。

Meanwhile my wife was under a cloud. All pleasures palled upon me; all sights tantalized and tempted me to outspoken treason, because I could not compare what I saw in Two Dimensions with what it really was if seen in Three, and could hardly refrain from making my comparisons aloud. I neglected my clients and my own business to give myself to the contemplation of the mysteries which I had once beheld, yet which I could impart to no one, and found daily more difficult to reproduce even before my own mental vision. One day, about eleven months after my return from Spaceland, I tried to see a Cube with my eye closed, but failed; and though I succeeded afterwards, I was not then quite certain (nor have I been ever afterwards) that I had exactly realized the original. This made me more melancholy than before, and determined me to take some step; yet what, I knew not. I felt that I would have been willing to sacrifice my life for the Cause, if thereby I could have produced conviction. But if I could not convince my Grandson, how could I convince the highest and most developed Circles in the land?

与此同时，我的妻子正处于忧郁之中。一切快乐对我都变得索然无味；一切景象都让我感到痛苦和诱惑，让我想要公然背叛，因为我无法将我在二维中看到的东西与它在三维中真正的样子进行比较，也几乎无法克制自己不大声说出我的比较。我忽视了我的客户和我自己的生意，全身心地投入到对那些我曾亲眼目睹却无法传授给任何人、且发现日益难以在我自己的心灵视野中重现的奥秘的沉思之中。在我从空间国归来约十一个月后的一天，我试着闭上眼睛看一个立方体，但失败了；虽然后来我成功了，但那时我并不完全确信（之后也一直不确信）我是否真正准确地再现了原作。这使我比以前更加忧郁，并促使我决心采取某种行动；但采取什么行动，我不知道。我觉得我愿意为这项事业牺牲生命，如果这样做能带来信念的话。但如果我连我的孙子都说服不了，我怎么能说服国内最高级、最发达的圆形呢？

And yet at times my spirit was too strong for me, and I gave vent to dangerous utterances. Already I was considered heterodox if not treasonable, and I was keenly alive to the danger of my position; nevertheless I could not at times refrain from bursting out into suspicious or half-seditious utterances, even among the highest Polygonal or Circular society. When, for example, the question arose about the treatment of those lunatics who said that they had received the power of seeing the insides of things, I would quote the saying of an ancient Circle, who declared that prophets and inspired people are always considered by the majority to be mad; and I could not help occasionally dropping such expressions as ``the eye that discerns the interiors of things,'' and ``the all-seeing land''; once or twice I even let fall the forbidden terms ``the Third and Fourth Dimensions.'' At last, to complete a series of minor indiscretions, at a meeting of our Local Speculative Society held at the palace of the Prefect himself,---some extremely silly person having read an elaborate paper exhibiting the precise reasons why Providence has limited the number of Dimensions to Two, and why the attribute of omnividence is assigned to the Supreme alone---I so far forgot myself as to give an exact account of the whole of my voyage with the Sphere into Space, and to the Assembly Hall in our Metropolis, and then to Space again, and of my return home, and of everything that I had seen and heard in fact or vision. At first, indeed, I pretended that I was describing the imaginary experiences of a fictitious person; but my enthusiasm soon forced me to throw off all disguise, and finally, in a fervent peroration, I exhorted all my hearers to divest themselves of prejudice and to become believers in the Third Dimension.

然而有时我的精神过于强烈，使我说出一些危险的话。我已经被视为异端，如果不是叛徒的话，我也敏锐地意识到自己处境的危险；但我有时仍然忍不住爆发出可疑或半煽动性的言论，即使是在最高级的多边形或圆形社会中。例如，当出现关于如何对待那些声称获得了看到事物内部能力的疯子的问题时，我会引用一位古代圆形的话——他宣称先知和受灵感启示的人总是被大多数人视为疯子；我也忍不住偶尔使用诸如"能洞察事物内部的眼睛"和"全视之地"这样的说法；有一两次我甚至说出了被禁止的术语——"第三和第四维度"。最后，为了完成一系列小小的轻率举动，在我们地方思辨学会于总督府举行的一次会议上——某个极其愚蠢的人宣读了一篇详尽的论文，阐述了上帝为何将维度数量限制为二，以及为何全视的属性只赋予至高者——我完全忘记了自己，精确地叙述了我与球体一起进入空间的整个航程，到我们大都市的市政厅，然后再到空间，以及我回家的旅程，和我在事实上或幻象中所看到和听到的一切。起初，我确实假装在描述一个虚构人物的想象经历；但我的热情很快迫使我抛开所有伪装，最后，在一段热烈的结束语中，我敦促所有听众摆脱偏见，成为第三维度的信徒。

Need I say that I was at once arrested and taken before the Council?

我还用得着说吗，我立刻被逮捕并带到了议会面前？

Next morning, standing in the very place where but a very few months ago the Sphere had stood in my company, I was allowed to begin and to continue my narration unquestioned and uninterrupted. But from the first I foresaw my fate; for the President, noting that a guard of the better sort of Policemen was in attendance, of angularity little, if at all, under 55°, ordered them to be relieved before I began my defence, by an inferior class of 2° or 3°. I knew only too well what that meant. I was to be executed or imprisoned, and my story was to be kept secret from the world by the simultaneous destruction of the officials who had heard it; and, this being the case, the President desired to substitute the cheaper for the more expensive victims.

第二天早晨，我站在几个月前球体曾与我站在一起的那个地方，被允许开始并继续我的叙述，没有受到质疑或打断。但从一开始我就预见了我的命运；因为会长注意到有一队较高级的警察在场，他们的角度很少低于55°，便命令在我开始辩护之前，用角度为2°或3°的低级警察替换他们。我非常清楚这意味着什么。我将被处决或监禁，而我的故事将通过同时销毁听到它的官员而被保密；既然如此，会长希望用更便宜的受害者代替更昂贵的受害者。

After I had concluded my defence, the President, perhaps perceiving that some of the junior Circles had been moved by evident earnestness, asked me two questions:---

在我结束辩护之后，会长也许察觉到一些年轻的圆形被我明显的真诚所感动，问了我两个问题：——

1. Whether I could indicate the direction which I meant when I used the words ``Upward, not Northward''?

1. 我能否指出当我使用"向上，而非向北"这些词时所指的方向？

2. Whether I could by any diagrams or descriptions (other than the enumeration of imaginary sides and angles) indicate the Figure I was pleased to call a Cube?

2. 我能否通过任何图表或描述（除了列举想象的边和角）来指出我乐于称之为立方体的图形？

I declared that I could say nothing more, and that I must commit myself to the Truth, whose cause would surely prevail in the end.

我声明我再也无话可说，我必须将自己交付给真理，真理的事业最终必将得胜。

The President replied that he quite concurred in my sentiment, and that I could not do better. I must be sentenced to perpetual imprisonment; but if the Truth intended that I should emerge from prison and evangelize the world, the Truth might be trusted to bring that result to pass. Meanwhile I should be subjected to no discomfort that was not necessary to preclude escape, and, unless I forfeited the privilege by misconduct, I should be occasionally permitted to see my brother who had preceded me to my prison.

会长回答说他完全同意我的感受，我做得再好不过了。我必须被判处终身监禁；但如果真理有意让我出狱并向世界传道，那么真理自会实现这一结果。与此同时，我不会遭受不必要的痛苦，除非为了防止逃跑；而且，除非我因行为不端而丧失特权，否则我将偶尔被允许探视比我先入狱的兄弟。

Seven years have elapsed and I am still a prisoner, and---if I except the occasional visits of my brother---debarred from all companionship save that of my jailers. My brother is one of the best of Squares, just, sensible, cheerful, and not without fraternal affection; yet I confess that my weekly interviews, at least in one respect, cause me the bitterest pain. He was present when the Sphere manifested himself in the Council Chamber; he saw the Sphere's changing sections; he heard the explanation of the phenomena then give to the Circles. Since that time, scarcely a week has passed during seven whole years, without his hearing from me a repetition of the part I played in that manifestation, together with ample descriptions of all the phenomena in Spaceland, and the arguments for the existence of Solid things derivable from Analogy. Yet---I take shame to be forced to confess it---my brother has not yet grasped the nature of Three Dimensions, and frankly avows his disbelief in the existence of a Sphere.

七年过去了，我仍然是一个囚犯，而且——如果不包括我兄弟偶尔的探视——除了狱卒之外，我被剥夺了一切陪伴。我的兄弟是最优秀的正方形之一，公正、明智、开朗，兄弟之情也不缺；但我承认，我每周的会面至少在一个方面给我带来了最痛苦的折磨。当球体在议会大厅显现时，他在场；他看到了球体变化的截面；他听到了当时向圆形们作出的现象解释。从那时起，整整七年中几乎没有一个星期过去，他不听我重复我在那次显现中所扮演的角色，以及对空间国所有现象的充分描述，和从类推中得出的立体事物存在的论证。然而——我不得不羞愧地承认——我的兄弟至今仍未理解三维的本质，并坦率地承认他不相信球体的存在。

Hence I am absolutely destitute of converts, and, for aught that I can see, the millennial Revelation has been made to me for nothing. Prometheus up in Spaceland was bound for bringing down fire for mortals, but I---poor Flatland Prometheus---lie here in prison for bringing down nothing to my countrymen. Yet I existing the hope that these memoirs, in some manner, I know not how, may find their way to the minds of humanity in Some Dimension, and may stir up a race of rebels who shall refuse to be confined to limited Dimensionality.

因此，我绝对没有任何皈依者，而且就我所见，千禧年的启示对我来说毫无意义。空间国的普罗米修斯因给人类带来火种而被缚，但我——可怜的平面国普罗米修斯——躺在这里的监狱里，因为我没有给同胞带来任何东西。然而，我仍抱有希望——这些回忆录也许能以某种我不知道的方式，到达某个维度的人类心中，并激发起一群拒绝被限制在有限维度中的叛逆者。

That is the hope of my brighter moments. Alas, it is not always so. Heavily weights on me at times the burdensome reflection that I cannot honestly say I am confident as to the exact shape of the once-seen, oft-regretted Cube; and in my nightly visions the mysterious precept, ``Upward, not Northward,'' haunts me like a soul-devouring Sphinx. It is part of the martyrdom which I endure for the cause of Truth that there are seasons of mental weakness, when Cubes and Spheres flit away into the background of scarce-possible existences; when the Land of Three Dimensions seems almost as visionary as the Land of One or None; nay, when even this hard wall that bars me from my freedom, these very tablets on which I am writing, and all the substantial realities of Flatland itself, appear no better than the offspring of a diseased imagination, or the baseless fabric of a dream.

这是我在光明时刻的希望。唉，情况并非总是如此。有时，一个沉重的反思压在我心头——我不能诚实地说我对曾经见过、又常常追忆的那个立方体的确切形状有把握；在我夜间的幻象中，那条神秘的箴言——"向上，而非向北"——像一个吞噬灵魂的斯芬克斯一样缠绕着我。我为真理事业而遭受的殉难的一部分，就是存在着一些心智软弱的时期：那时立方体和球体都飞掠到几乎不可能存在的背景中去；那时三维之地似乎几乎和一维或零维之地一样虚幻；不仅如此，甚至这道阻挡我自由的坚硬墙壁、我正在书写的这些木板、以及平面国本身的一切坚实现实，都似乎不过是病态想象的产物，或是一场毫无根据的梦境。
